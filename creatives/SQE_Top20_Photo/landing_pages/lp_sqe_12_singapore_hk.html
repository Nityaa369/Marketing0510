1. The problem

Why you, and why now

Singapore and Hong Kong are closer, familiar and full of Indian professionals. The legal roles there, though, go to lawyers with a recognised common-law qualification.

Both hubs run on English-law work. A quarter of SIAC cases apply English law, and it is the second most common governing law in Hong Kong as well. International firms in both cities staff those matters with English-qualified lawyers.

An England and Wales qualification earned from India is the credential that moves you from the Indian desk to the international one.

2. Once you see it

Did you know? 12 reasons this is worth your attention

  1. Singapore and Hong Kong hire English-law lawyers.English law is the second most common governing law at SIAC, and at the Hong Kong centre too.
    Source: The Law Society of England and Wales, International Data Insights Report 2025; SIAC and HKIAC annual reports 2024
  2. 78 percent of LCIA arbitrations in 2024 were on English-law contracts.English law is also the single most common governing law in new ICC cases, and applies in a quarter of SIAC cases.
    Source: The Law Society of England and Wales, International Data Insights Report 2025; LCIA, ICC and SIAC annual casework reports 2024
  3. Awards under English law travel.Arbitral awards are enforceable in more than 170 countries under the New York Convention.
    Source: UN Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards, 1958
  4. One exam, recognised worldwide.The SQE is the single national exam to qualify as a solicitor of England and Wales. It replaced the older routes and is open to qualified lawyers from other countries, including India.
    Source: SRA
  5. The written exams are taken in India.SQE1 and the written part of SQE2 run at Pearson VUE test centres, including six Indian cities. Only the SQE2 oral sittings require a trip to England or Wales.
    Source: sqe.sra.org.uk, test centre locations
  6. About 40 percent of global business and financial transactions run on English law.Parties choose it because the case law is mature and the courts are trusted.
    Source: The Law Society of England and Wales, International Data Insights Report 2025, citing TheCityUK
  7. UK legal services earn about GBP 11 billion a year abroad.The UK is the second largest legal market in the world, behind only the United States.
    Source: The Law Society of England and Wales, International Data Insights Report 2025
  8. No UK degree is required.The route asks for a degree or an equivalent qualification in any subject, plus the SQE passes. Your Indian law degree counts.
    Source: SRA, Qualified lawyers
  9. Enrolled advocates can apply to skip SQE2.A qualified lawyer with at least two years of professional experience can apply to the SRA for an SQE2 exemption. If granted, it removes five half-days of assessment, the SQE2 fee and the UK trip. The SRA decides each case.
    Source: sra.org.uk, SQE exemptions
  10. 57 percent of UK law firms say they struggle to hire.56 percent of in-house teams report the same. A firm that cannot hire does not care where a qualified English-law lawyer sits.
    Source: The Access Group, The 10 biggest challenges facing the legal profession
  11. Firms shortlist on profile before the interview.Day 5 builds your international profile: the LinkedIn, the CV and the work samples that get a response from UK and Gulf firms.
  12. Most learners pass SQE1 within about a year of starting.A typical plan is nine to twelve months of part-time study, around work. The next realistic window for most of this group is SQE1 in July 2027.
    Source: SRA, SQE assessment dates
