1. The problem

Why you, and why now

You spent a year and a serious sum on an LLM. It was a good year. It did not give you practising rights anywhere, and in India it is another line on the CV.

A foreign LLM does not by itself qualify you to practise in England and Wales. The SQE does. And the SQE route accepts your degree toward its entry requirement, so the LLM is not wasted; it is the first half of a qualification you never finished.

If you are also an enrolled advocate with two years of practice, the SQE2 exemption may cover the practical exam.

Did you know? 12 reasons this is worth your attention

  1. An LLM does not qualify you. The SQE does.A foreign LLM does not by itself give practising rights in England and Wales. The SQE is the route, and your degree still counts toward the entry requirement.
    Source: SRA, Qualified lawyers
  2. One exam, recognised worldwide.The SQE is the single national exam to qualify as a solicitor of England and Wales. It replaced the older routes and is open to qualified lawyers from other countries, including India.
    Source: SRA
  3. No UK degree is required.The route asks for a degree or an equivalent qualification in any subject, plus the SQE passes. Your Indian law degree counts.
    Source: SRA, Qualified lawyers
  4. The written exams are taken in India.SQE1 and the written part of SQE2 run at Pearson VUE test centres, including six Indian cities. Only the SQE2 oral sittings require a trip to England or Wales.
    Source: sqe.sra.org.uk, test centre locations
  5. Enrolled advocates can apply to skip SQE2.A qualified lawyer with at least two years of professional experience can apply to the SRA for an SQE2 exemption. If granted, it removes five half-days of assessment, the SQE2 fee and the UK trip. The SRA decides each case.
    Source: sra.org.uk, SQE exemptions
  6. SQE1 is 360 multiple-choice questions.Two papers, FLK1 and FLK2, closed book, on a computer. It rewards method, which is what a daily MCQ habit builds.
    Source: SRA, SQE1 Assessment Specification
  7. About 40 percent of global business and financial transactions run on English law.Parties choose it because the case law is mature and the courts are trusted.
    Source: The Law Society of England and Wales, International Data Insights Report 2025, citing TheCityUK
  8. UK legal services earn about GBP 11 billion a year abroad.The UK is the second largest legal market in the world, behind only the United States.
    Source: The Law Society of England and Wales, International Data Insights Report 2025
