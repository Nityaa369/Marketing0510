1. The problem

Why you, and why now

Every year you tell yourself this is the year. Then the question stops you: which country, which exam, which cost, and will it even be recognised. So nothing moves.

Here is the mistake most lawyers make. They think migration first, then qualification. Move, hope, and pay for it with visa pressure. Qualification first is the durable order: earn under English law from wherever you are, and move only if it suits.

The SQE is the qualification that travels. England and Wales, the UAE free zones, Singapore and Hong Kong all run English-law work.

2. Once you see it

Did you know? 12 reasons this is worth your attention

  1. One exam, recognised worldwide.The SQE is the single national exam to qualify as a solicitor of England and Wales. It replaced the older routes and is open to qualified lawyers from other countries, including India.
    Source: SRA
  2. No UK degree is required.The route asks for a degree or an equivalent qualification in any subject, plus the SQE passes. Your Indian law degree counts.
    Source: SRA, Qualified lawyers
  3. The written exams are taken in India.SQE1 and the written part of SQE2 run at Pearson VUE test centres, including six Indian cities. Only the SQE2 oral sittings require a trip to England or Wales.
    Source: sqe.sra.org.uk, test centre locations
  4. You can sit the written exams in many countries.Pearson VUE runs the sittings at more than 100 test centres across 26 countries. Check your nearest centre on the SRA site.
    Source: sqe.sra.org.uk, test centre locations
  5. About 40 percent of global business and financial transactions run on English law.Parties choose it because the case law is mature and the courts are trusted.
    Source: The Law Society of England and Wales, International Data Insights Report 2025, citing TheCityUK
  6. UK legal services earn about GBP 11 billion a year abroad.The UK is the second largest legal market in the world, behind only the United States.
    Source: The Law Society of England and Wales, International Data Insights Report 2025
  7. Gulf free-zone courts work in English on common-law lines.The DIFC courts in Dubai run an English-language, common-law system, and apply English precedent where their own code is silent.
    Source: DIFC Courts, difccourts.ae
  8. Singapore and Hong Kong hire English-law lawyers.English law is the second most common governing law at SIAC, and at the Hong Kong centre too.
    Source: The Law Society of England and Wales, International Data Insights Report 2025; SIAC and HKIAC annual reports 2024
  9. Enrolled advocates can apply to skip SQE2.A qualified lawyer with at least two years of professional experience can apply to the SRA for an SQE2 exemption. If granted, it removes five half-days of assessment, the SQE2 fee and the UK trip. The SRA decides each case.
    Source: sra.org.uk, SQE exemptions
  10. 57 percent of UK law firms say they struggle to hire.56 percent of in-house teams report the same. A firm that cannot hire does not care where a qualified English-law lawyer sits.
    Source: The Access Group, The 10 biggest challenges facing the legal profession
  11. Firms shortlist on profile before the interview.Day 5 builds your international profile: the LinkedIn, the CV and the work samples that get a response from UK and Gulf firms.
  12. Most learners pass SQE1 within about a year of starting.A typical plan is nine to twelve months of part-time study, around work. The next realistic window for most of this group is SQE1 in July 2027.
    Source: SRA, SQE assessment dates
