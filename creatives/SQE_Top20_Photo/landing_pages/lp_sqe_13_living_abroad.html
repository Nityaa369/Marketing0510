1. The problem

Why you, and why now

You moved for family or for a partner's job, and your law degree came with you as a line on a CV nobody reads. Local requalification looks like starting again.

The SQE is different. It is open to qualified lawyers from other countries, it does not need a UK degree, and the written exams run at Pearson VUE centres in more than 100 locations across 26 countries.

If you are an enrolled advocate with two years of Indian practice behind you, you can also apply for the SQE2 exemption from abroad.

2. Once you see it

Did you know? 12 reasons this is worth your attention

  1. You can sit the written exams in many countries.Pearson VUE runs the sittings at more than 100 test centres across 26 countries. Check your nearest centre on the SRA site.
    Source: sqe.sra.org.uk, test centre locations
  2. One exam, recognised worldwide.The SQE is the single national exam to qualify as a solicitor of England and Wales. It replaced the older routes and is open to qualified lawyers from other countries, including India.
    Source: SRA
  3. No UK degree is required.The route asks for a degree or an equivalent qualification in any subject, plus the SQE passes. Your Indian law degree counts.
    Source: SRA, Qualified lawyers
  4. Enrolled advocates can apply to skip SQE2.A qualified lawyer with at least two years of professional experience can apply to the SRA for an SQE2 exemption. If granted, it removes five half-days of assessment, the SQE2 fee and the UK trip. The SRA decides each case.
    Source: sra.org.uk, SQE exemptions
  5. No two years of UK work experience for you.Qualified lawyers do not need the two years of qualifying work experience that fresh graduates must complete.
    Source: SRA, Qualified lawyers
  6. About 40 percent of global business and financial transactions run on English law.Parties choose it because the case law is mature and the courts are trusted.
    Source: The Law Society of England and Wales, International Data Insights Report 2025, citing TheCityUK
  7. UK legal services earn about GBP 11 billion a year abroad.The UK is the second largest legal market in the world, behind only the United States.
    Source: The Law Society of England and Wales, International Data Insights Report 2025
  8. Gulf free-zone courts work in English on common-law lines.The DIFC courts in Dubai run an English-language, common-law system, and apply English precedent where their own code is silent.
    Source: DIFC Courts, difccourts.ae
  9. Singapore and Hong Kong hire English-law lawyers.English law is the second most common governing law at SIAC, and at the Hong Kong centre too.
    Source: The Law Society of England and Wales, International Data Insights Report 2025; SIAC and HKIAC annual reports 2024
  10. 57 percent of UK law firms say they struggle to hire.56 percent of in-house teams report the same. A firm that cannot hire does not care where a qualified English-law lawyer sits.
    Source: The Access Group, The 10 biggest challenges facing the legal profession
  11. Firms shortlist on profile before the interview.Day 5 builds your international profile: the LinkedIn, the CV and the work samples that get a response from UK and Gulf firms.
  12. Most learners pass SQE1 within about a year of starting.A typical plan is nine to twelve months of part-time study, around work. The next realistic window for most of this group is SQE1 in July 2027.
    Source: SRA, SQE assessment dates
