01

You see how the money moves. The contracts go to someone else.

Your year runs on notices, replies, classification questions, assessments and appeals. Some of your clients sell software, run online stores or host games, and you understand their revenue better than most of their advisers.

Yet when the same client needs a subscription agreement, a seller agreement or a refund policy, they ask another lawyer. You are seen as the tax person, called when a notice arrives.

Tax work is steady, but it is reactive and seasonal. The part of the client's business that grows, its products and contracts, happens without you.

02

A tech law practice built on reading business models

Tech law is the everyday legal work of technology companies: their contracts, privacy, compliance and disputes. For a tax lawyer, it means becoming the tech lawyer for software, SaaS and e-commerce companies, drafting the documents behind their revenue rather than only defending it afterwards.

This is a new practice area, not extra hours on your tax files. You could serve new tech clients with it, join a firm's tech team, or move to a tech company's legal side.

03

What tax lawyers usually do instead

Referring contract work to another lawyerIt keeps the client happy for now. It also tells the client that you are not the lawyer for their business, only for their tax.
Enrolling in a broad corporate law courseIt covers company law broadly. It does not show how a SaaS or marketplace business is structured, or which documents it signs.
Waiting for the next tax change to bring workEach change brings a burst of notices and then quiet. It deepens your tax practice without moving you into a new field.
04

Tech law follows the business model. You already read business models.

The camp's starting point is that tech legal work depends on how a company makes money, not on code. A tax lawyer reads exactly that every day, through invoices, revenue streams and where income is booked.

So the move is about positioning, not starting over. You are already the lawyer who understands the client's money. The gap is a handful of tech documents and the words that tell founders you do this work. When a SaaS founder hears that you can explain both the subscription contract and how its revenue is treated, you become the first call, not the last.

If you stay the notice lawyer

Your tax practice stays useful and respected. The cost is gentle: the tech clients you already know keep growing their contract and policy work, and each year it is a little more settled with another lawyer.

05

What tech law work looks like for a tax and GST lawyer

Day 2 maps 6 ways tech companies make money: advertising, subscription, commission and affiliate, marketplace and third-party sellers, hardware and IoT, and tech consulting. For each, you would draft the contracts the model creates, such as subscription terms or marketplace seller agreements.

Structuring suits a tax mind well. Founders ask whether a business can be done, in which jurisdiction and at what cost. The 6-month plan includes structuring SaaS, e-commerce, fintech, gaming and blockchain businesses, and international expansion.

E-commerce companies also need policies that touch money directly: cancellation, returns and refunds. You understand what happens to an order after it is reversed, which helps you draft a policy that works in practice. Day 2 also lists the 8 services e-commerce companies need, from structuring and contracts to data protection, IP and disputes, so you can see where to grow next.
