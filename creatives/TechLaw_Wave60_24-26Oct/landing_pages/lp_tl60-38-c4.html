01

Applications out. Replies slow. Days that feel long.

You have updated your CV, written to contacts and applied to roles like the one you had. Some days there is a reply, most days there is not.

The longer it goes, the more you wonder whether to take the first similar role that comes, or to use this time for something better. Both feel risky.

You have the time now, but time without a plan tends to go into refreshing job portals. You want something that uses your legal training, gives you something to show, and does not lock you into the same search again.

02

Turning this time into a tech law start

Tech law is the legal work technology companies hire lawyers for: their contracts, privacy, compliance and disputes. It is a field where clients and employers judge the documents you can show. That makes a gap between jobs a good time to build them.

Instead of returning to the same kind of role, you could move into tech law: a firm's tech team, a startup's legal work, legal tech, or remote work for foreign lawyers and founders. Each path values a lawyer who has already drafted tech documents, which is something you can start on this month.

04

You do not need more effort. You need a field where your work speaks.

Between jobs, most lawyers try harder at the same search. The issue is usually positioning: your CV looks like many others in a crowded field. In tech law, a finished refund policy or terms review says more than a line on a CV.

Your legal training is already enough to start. Tech law does not need coding, and you can begin with 2 or 3 kinds of work, as the script says. What changes is where you point your days. A clear plan for each day also makes the wait easier to carry.

If the gap stays unplanned

A gap is not a problem on its own, and many lawyers have one. The quiet cost is that these weeks pass without anything to show for them, when they could have produced the first pieces of a new career.

05

What tech law work looks like for a lawyer between jobs

In the first weeks, your work is building samples. You draft a refund policy for an online store, review a startup's terms, and write the key clauses of a software development agreement. Each one becomes proof of what you can do. Keep them in a simple folder you can share with a founder or an interviewer.

Next comes outreach, which the camp treats as a skill. Day 1 gives two message templates, one offering help with a review and one pointing out missing clauses in a startup's terms. You reach founders in the US, UK, Canada, Europe and India.

In parallel, you can apply to firm tech teams, startups and legal tech companies with work to show. If you prefer remote work, Day 3 shows how to set up an Upwork profile as a data protection lawyer.
