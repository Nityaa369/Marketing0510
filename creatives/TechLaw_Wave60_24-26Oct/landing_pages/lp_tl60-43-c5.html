Your draft is your CV now

In tech law, much of the work arrives as one task at a time: a refund policy, a set of terms, a contract. Each finished task becomes proof of what you can do, no matter when you last practised.

So the gap is a positioning problem, not a question of ability. You still know how to read carefully, ask the right question and put a promise into words. Those skills did not expire while you were away. The camp shows you where to point that skill.

The quiet cost of putting it off

Each month you spend unsure is a month the degree stays on the shelf. No one will push you to start, and that is the hard part. Three evenings from home is a gentle first step.

05

What tech law work looks like for a lawyer back after a career break

You might start with an e-commerce store whose return policy is vague. You redraft it so the buyer knows when they can cancel, when they can return, how the return works, and how and when the refund arrives. It is a short document with real effects for the store and its buyers.

Next could be an influencer agreement for a brand. Five clauses carry most of it: the engagement and fee, the deliverables, the licence to use content, who owns the content, and the brand's guidelines. Each one is short enough to draft in a single sitting.

Over time you could review a startup's terms of use or help with privacy policies. Every piece is done on a laptop, in the hours you choose, and each one adds to a portfolio that speaks louder than the gap.
