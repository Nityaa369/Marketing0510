How contract lawyers usually try to find startups

Waiting for referrals from existing clientsReferrals bring more of the same kind of client. Your current clients may not know early-stage founders, so startups seldom reach your desk this way.
Posting general updates on LinkedInPosts build visibility slowly but do not start conversations with a specific founder. Without a direct message, nobody knows you can fix their documents.
Sending a generic introduction emailA founder is likely to ignore a message that only says you offer legal services. It asks for their time without giving anything first, so it reads as a sales pitch. Even a well-qualified lawyer gets ignored this way.
04

Lead with their document, not your services

The camp's approach flips the usual pitch. You read a startup's own public terms first, find what is missing, and write to the founder about that. The message gives value before it asks for anything.

So the gap is positioning, not drafting skill. Your eye for a weak clause is the pitch. The camp shows you where to find the startups, what to check, and what to write. You stay a lawyer offering a genuine observation, never a salesperson.

05

What tech law work looks like for a contract lawyer who wants startup clients

On Day 1 you see where founders gather in public. The trainers demonstrate finding startups through Y Combinator and Shark Tank, and suggest aiming at young companies before their first large funding round, with fewer than 20 people. You learn to read a startup's website for its terms before you ever write to anyone.

On Day 3 you review a startup's terms using six checks. The checks are no liability for free services, liability capped at the fee for paid ones, clear termination, community standards, a compliant privacy policy link, and IP ownership.

Then you write. One template the camp teaches opens with "I reviewed your terms and noticed 3 missing clauses". Another offers help with a review on LinkedIn or Upwork. You follow up politely. The Day 3 goal is a steady daily rhythm of 5 cold emails and 3 follow-ups, building towards 100 founders contacted.
