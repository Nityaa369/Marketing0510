It feels like a field for the young

You have practised for years. You know your courts, your clients and your craft. When tech law comes up, the faces in the photos are young, and the words sound like they belong to another generation. Nobody seems to be speaking to someone at your stage.

Part of you is curious. Part of you says a lawyer at your stage should not be starting from zero, sitting beside fresh graduates and learning new vocabulary.

So you watched, perhaps visited the page, and let it pass. You told yourself it was a career for people with more years ahead of them. Yet the thought keeps returning, which is why you are here.

02

What changes when experience becomes the advantage

Tech law is the legal work that technology companies need across their contracts, compliance, privacy, policy and disputes. Much of that work is judgement about risk, and judgement is what years of practice build.

You could add a tech law career to what you already have, advising startups and foreign clients alongside your practice. It is not starting again. It is taking a seasoned skill into a newer market, where clients need someone who has seen how things go wrong.

What experienced lawyers usually do instead

Sending tech questions to younger colleaguesIt keeps the client happy for now, but the expertise, and the relationship, slowly move to whoever answers. You remain the lawyer for everything except the growing part.
Considering a full degree programmeA long academic course feels thorough, yet it takes years and teaches theory first. At your stage you need practical skills you can put to work while you are still learning.
Deciding to stay with what you knowA settled practice is valuable. But staying put by default, rather than by choice, leaves the curiosity unanswered and the option unexplored. Years later, the same question can return with less time to act on it.
04

The rules are new for everyone

Most of India's tech rules are recent, so nobody has decades of experience in them. The camp script puts it this way: a veteran and a fresh graduate stand on the same starting line. Only one of them brings years of judgement.

So the question is positioning, not age. You already know how deals go wrong, how clients think and how disputes unfold. Tech companies need exactly that judgement wrapped around their contracts and structures.

What it costs to decide it is too late

Deciding it is too late is the one decision that makes it true. The practice continues either way. Three evenings let you test the field from your own chair, with nothing to give up.

05

What tech law work looks like for an experienced lawyer

Structuring is a natural fit. A startup asks whether its business can be done at all, in which jurisdiction and at what cost. The script's example is a lending-algorithm company that wants to work with banks and NBFCs.

Advisory work suits you too. Founders need someone who has seen deals fail to tell them where their contract, policy or plan is weak. Your years in practice make that advice worth hearing. A young founder rarely has anyone like you on call.

When things go wrong, tech disputes follow: contract arbitrations, consumer and regulatory complaints, impersonation and takedown fights. You bring a litigator's or adviser's instinct to a field that is still young. The disputes are new, but the instinct for how a case turns is not.

What you will have done by Monday night

On Day 3 you see how a blockchain founder's venture is structured as a Wyoming DAO LLC, a company form built for that kind of business. It is a structuring question from start to finish: what form, where, and why. For a lawyer who has advised clients for years, this is familiar thinking applied to a new kind of business. It shows how your existing judgement transfers.

The six-month plan from Day 2 asks for about two hours a day, one learning and one on outreach. For an experienced lawyer, outreach can lean on the network you already have. Over six months you could add tech skills steadily and introduce them to clients who already trust you. Some of those clients may already run, or invest in, technology businesses.
