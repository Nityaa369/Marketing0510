Two qualifications. One of them does all the work.

Board meetings, minutes, registers, annual filings, secretarial audits and a compliance calendar that never empties. You keep a company on the right side of its deadlines, and you are trusted to do it.

Your LLB sits beside your CS on the CV, but your job title rarely uses it. Colleagues see you as the person who files, not the person who advises.

You may have thought about using the law degree properly. Litigation feels like starting over, and a corporate law role often looks like more of the same compliance in a bigger office.

02

Using your LLB as a tech lawyer

Tech law is legal work for technology companies, mostly contracts, privacy, compliance and disputes. Moving into it puts your law degree at the front. You would work as a lawyer on privacy programmes, company structuring and tech policies, for startups, firm tech teams or tech companies.

Your compliance discipline comes with you, but the role changes. Instead of tracking someone else's legal work, you would be the lawyer doing it.

04

Your compliance habit is the asset. Your LLB is the licence to use it.

Tech companies carry a long list of duties: privacy compliance, sector filings, sandbox applications, and the duties platforms owe for what their users post. The script calls compliance work recurring. Tracking recurring duties is your trade. A CS already knows what it takes to keep a company in good standing year after year, and tech companies need that rhythm applied to privacy and platform rules.

So the move is a matter of positioning, not of working harder. You already think like a compliance lawyer. What you need is the tech vocabulary, a few legal documents in your own name, and an introduction that leads with the LLB.

If the LLB stays in the second line

Staying put is safe, and your CS work is valued. The cost is that the law degree you worked hard for keeps waiting, while privacy and tech compliance work goes to lawyers who said yes to it first.

05

What tech law work looks like for a company secretary with an LLB

Privacy work is the most natural start. The 6-month plan begins with the easiest skills: DPDP, GDPR, policies and contracts. You would map how a company collects and uses personal data, draft the privacy policy, and keep its privacy duties on a calendar.

Structuring is the second fit. Founders ask whether a business can be done, where and at what cost. On Day 3 the camp structures a blockchain venture as a Wyoming DAO LLC, and the plan includes foreign investment and international expansion.

You would also handle the compliance side of fintech and e-commerce companies: sector filings, sandbox applications and the policies a platform must publish. This is legal advice, not filing support, and your LLB is what lets you give it.
