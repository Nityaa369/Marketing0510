03

How juniors usually fight the jargon

Searching every term as it comesYou get a definition from an engineering site, written for engineers. It tells you what the thing is, not why a contract cares about it.
Skipping the technical clausesThose clauses are often where the risk sits: who pays when the service is down, or what is actually handed over. Skipping them means missing the point of the contract, and a client will notice.
Asking a friend who works in ITThey explain the technology well, but not the legal consequence. You come away knowing how an API works and still unsure what to draft.
04

Jargon is vocabulary, not engineering

Each of these terms names a business arrangement. An SLA is a promise about service. White labelling is a branding deal. Source versus object code is a question of what gets delivered. Once you see the arrangement, the clause around it reads like any other contract.

So your gap is positioning, not intelligence. Your legal training already handles promises, remedies and ownership. Day 1 gives you the five words that let that training work on tech documents.

What staying lost in the jargon costs

Every tech contract you avoid is one more reason to stay in work you already know. Nothing is lost today, but the field keeps feeling closed. Learning five words over one evening can open it, and the rest of the camp builds on them.

05

What tech law work looks like for a junior advocate who knows the five words

With SLAs understood, you read a service levels schedule properly: the uptime promised, what counts as downtime, the credits owed when the promise is broken, and the support response metrics. You can tell a client whether those remedies are real.

With APIs understood, you can follow an API integration agreement, where two companies agree how their products will connect and share data. With source and object code clear, you know what a developer must hand over and what the client will actually own.

With white labelling and SaaS clear, reseller and co-branding agreements make sense, and so do subscription terms for software rented online. These are the everyday documents of tech companies, and you can now read them like a lawyer. When a senior or a client asks what a clause means, you have the answer.
