A law degree, and a shift that has nothing to do with it

You finished your LLB, and then a BPO or back-office job came first. It paid on time, and the family needed that. Your day now runs on tickets, quality scores and handling time.

02

Legal work for tech companies, built on what you already know

Tech law is the legal work that technology companies need: their contracts, compliance, data privacy, policy and disputes. It is desk work done on a laptop, mostly in writing. A startup needs a service agreement drafted or its terms checked, and it needs a person who can read law to do it.

If you move into this field, your LLB stops being a certificate and becomes your trade. You could draft for startups, support US lawyers as a remote paralegal, or later move into a tech company's legal team.

You are not behind. You are pointed in the wrong direction.

The problem has never been effort. You work long shifts and hit hard targets. The problem is positioning: you present yourself as a process associate, so the world buys process work from you.

Here is the part most people miss. One of the first concepts on Day 1 is the SLA, the service level agreement, with its uptime, downtime and service credits. Day 1 also covers service-level metrics for support. You have lived inside those metrics for years, and a lawyer who knows how a support desk really runs reads an SLA with far more sense.

05

What tech law work looks like for an LLB holder from a BPO

Much of tech law is service contracts. A software company that sells support signs an SLA promising response times, uptime and credits when it falls short. You would draft and check those schedules, and you already know what a realistic response time looks like.

Another large share is policy work. Every app that collects customer details needs a privacy policy and terms of use that match what it actually does. You have handled customer data under strict process rules, so you know where real data flows inside a business.

On Day 1 the camp also walks through the kinds of work US tech startups send to paralegals. These are tasks done by email and shared documents, one at a time. That suits someone who already works to written instructions and fixed deadlines.

By the end of Day 1 you will have worked through a software development agreement with the faculty, clause by clause. You will know the appointment, milestones, specifications, payment, acceptance testing, IP assignment and handover clauses, and the mnemonic that keeps them in order.
