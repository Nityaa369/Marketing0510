01

An extra degree, and the same old question

You gave a year, sometimes two, to a master's in law. You wrote a dissertation, read more case law than most of your batch, and came out knowing one subject in real depth.

Now the interviews ask a different question. Not what you studied, but what you have drafted. The LLM sits on your CV, and the reply is still that they need someone with experience.

Quietly, you are wondering whether the degree was worth it, and how to make it count. You do not want to start again from zero. You want the LLM to carry you somewhere new.

02

Turn the depth you built into a practice

Tech law is the body of legal work behind technology companies: contracts, privacy, regulatory compliance and disputes. It rewards what an LLM builds: patience with dense rules, comfort with new statutes, and the habit of reading the full text before forming a view.

Moving into tech law gives your LLM a job to do. Instead of a line on a CV, it becomes the base of a practice in contracts, privacy and policy that tech companies, firm tech teams and think tanks need.

You have the depth. What you need is a document

Your problem is not a lack of knowledge. You may know more law than many practising associates. The gap is that none of that knowledge has yet been turned into something a client can use.

That gap closes quickly once you aim at tech law. On the first evening, the camp drafts a software development agreement with you, live, clause by clause. Your LLM training lets you follow why each clause exists, not only what it says.

What keeps the LLM idle

If the LLM stays a credential without a use, nothing is lost overnight. It simply sits there while you keep applying with it. Pointing it at a field that needs it is a gentler way forward than another round of the same applications.

05

What tech law work looks like for an LLM graduate

Much of the work starts with a rule set. A fintech startup asks whether its model can run in India, under which regulator, and at what cost. That is structuring work: research done for a client instead of a dissertation. The answer shapes what the company builds, so it is read carefully and acted on.

Then comes drafting. A cloud software company wants its promises on uptime, downtime and credits written into the contract. A founder needs a software development agreement that settles who owns the code. Your comfort with dense text makes these faster to learn.

There is also policy work. Tech law includes research, white papers and responses to draft laws. Anyone who has argued a dissertation from sources already knows how to build a position, which is what policy teams and think tanks ask for.
