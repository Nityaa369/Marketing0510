01

Study hours, and nothing else to show for them

Your day is bare acts, previous papers and answer writing. You have revised the CPC more times than you can count. The preparation is serious work, and it deserves respect.

But the months add up. Friends from your batch are drafting, arguing and earning. Your CV shows the year you graduated and then a long, quiet stretch. Relatives ask what you are doing, and 'preparing' sounds smaller each time you say it.

You do not want to give up the goal. You want something real building beside it, something you can point to whatever the next notification brings.

02

A legal career that grows on about 2 hours a day

Tech law is legal work for technology businesses, from contracts and privacy to compliance and disputes. It is done on a laptop, one task at a time, and most of it can be scheduled around your study hours. Nobody expects you in court at 10.

Start now, and the coming months can produce legal work under your own name: a reviewed set of startup terms, a drafted contract, an agreement for a brand. That is a career growing alongside your preparation, not instead of it.

03

What aspirants usually do beside preparation

Preparing full time and nothing elseIt gives every hour to study. It also leaves your CV without any legal work for as long as the preparation lasts.
Joining a chamber part timeCourt hours are fixed and long. Chamber work eats exactly the morning hours you set aside for revision, and the files belong to your senior, not to you.
Teaching at a coaching centreIt pays and keeps the syllabus fresh. But it keeps you inside exam material, and it builds no legal career outside the classroom.
04

This is not a choice between the exam and a career

Many aspirants assume they must pick one: prepare fully or start working. That is a scheduling question, not a test of commitment. The useful question is which work fits around study without breaking it.

Tech law fits because it is task based and done by email. It also uses what you are already sharpening every day. Reading a provision closely and spotting what a clause leaves out is the same discipline your answer writing builds.

The cost of an empty stretch

Preparing with nothing alongside is not a mistake. It simply means the time shows up only as preparation. A small, steady practice next to it gives those months a second result, without taking anything from the first.

05

What tech law work looks like for a law graduate preparing for judiciary exams

Your tech law work comes in small, self-contained pieces. One evening you check a startup's terms for a missing liability cap. Another day you draft a short privacy policy. Each task has a clear finish line, so it never spills into the next morning's revision.

The law underneath overlaps with your syllabus. Contract law sits under every software agreement. Consumer protection sits under every refund policy. Interpretation sits under every review. You are applying familiar law to a business problem.

Because the work runs by email, you decide when it happens: an early morning slot, an hour after lunch, a weekend block. The camp's outreach method, a short note on a real gap in a startup's terms, can go out at any hour.
