01

Disputes about work, rarely about new work

Your practice is terminations, wage claims, standing orders, industrial disputes and labour court dates. You read appointment letters and settlements, and argue over what an employer promised and when. Each matter turns on what was written down at the start.

You know how working relationships are built and how they break: who is an employee, who is a contractor, who owns what was made, and what happens when someone walks out.

But most of your clients are older businesses and the matters move slowly. Newer companies, the startups hiring small teams and outside developers, rarely come to your door. Their first contracts are often copied from templates by whoever is nearest.

02

The same contracts, for companies being built now

Startups need employee, contractor and IP assignment agreements early, often before their product launches. They also hire outside developers, and those relationships run on software development agreements. These are documents about work, ownership and exit, the questions you handle every day.

A tech law career lets you draft and review that work for founders and tech companies. Your labour practice continues, and the new work comes from businesses that are still being shaped, where a good contract matters most.

What labour lawyers usually try

Taking on more compliance retainers for factories and officesIt adds steady files, but it is the same work for the same kind of client. It widens the practice without changing its direction. Your skills stay pointed at older businesses rather than at companies still forming.
Moving into a salaried HR legal roleA full-time salaried job usually means stepping away from practice. That trades your independence for a role that may still be mostly HR paperwork.
A general corporate law certificateIt covers company law broadly, but founders look for someone who can show them a tech contract. A certificate on its own does not show one.
04

Who owns the work is your question

In a tech company, the most valuable asset is often code written by employees or contractors. Whether that code belongs to the company depends on the contract, and contracts about work are your field.

So you are not changing craft. You are repositioning, from disputes after a working relationship fails to the agreements that set it up properly. Effort was never the missing piece; visibility to founders was.

If the new businesses never meet you

If nothing shifts, the companies being built today will keep finding other lawyers for their first contracts. Your understanding of working relationships stays tied to older disputes, when it could shape how new teams are formed. A few focused hours each week would be enough to start.

What tech law work looks like for an employment and labour lawyer

Drafting the IP assignment and handover clauses of a software development agreement, taught on Day 1. They decide whether the client owns the code a developer delivers, the tech version of a question you already know well.

Engaging a developer properly. The same Day 1 agreement opens with appointment and milestones, terms you would recognise from any engagement letter, then sets them against specifications and acceptance testing. Labour lawyers spot vague engagement terms quickly.

Understanding how the client earns. Day 2 covers six business models, including tech consulting and services, where the people doing the work are the product. Each model creates its own contract needs. A services company depends on clear contracts with its own people and with its clients.

Questions lawyers ask us

Is employment law really relevant to tech companies?Very. Tech companies are built by employees, contractors and outside developers, and the contracts with each decide who owns the work. That is where you begin.
I have practised labour law for years. Is it too late to switch?The rules tech companies follow are new for everyone, so a long career is no handicap and nobody has a decades-long head start. Your judgement about working relationships is an advantage.
How do advocates find startup clients?Not by soliciting, which the Bar Council restricts. We show how to build relationships instead: share a genuine observation about a public document, and network with US lawyers by helping them first.
Is coding part of the work?No. The legal work follows how the business makes money. Day 1 explains five tech concepts, such as source code versus object code, in plain words. Your existing contract reading carries most of the load.
