Searches, filings, oppositions, renewals. Then the next batch.

Your days are trademark searches, applications, examination replies, oppositions and renewal reminders. You are good at it, and much of it arrives in volume, with deadlines set by the registry rather than by you.

The clients who come to you often have a brand and little else to protect. When a software company does arrive, it wants a mark registered, while its bigger IP questions sit inside contracts someone else drafts.

You have probably wondered where your IP practice goes next. Copyright questions come in now and then, scattered across clients, and none of them add up to a field you can call your own.

02

IP work for the companies whose product is IP

Tech law means the legal work tech companies need, from contracts and compliance to privacy and disputes. For an IP lawyer it is a natural next field, because a software company's main asset is its code, its content and its brand. You could advise tech companies, creators and platforms on who owns what, and protect it when it is copied.

The move keeps your IP training at the centre. It widens the work from registering marks to drafting and enforcing the rights that decide how a tech business earns.

04

Software is IP. You already speak half the language.

Ownership, licensing, assignment and infringement are the core of most tech deals. Most lawyers entering tech law have to learn these from scratch. You start with them.

So the gap is not knowledge or effort. It is that tech founders do not yet see a trademark lawyer as their tech lawyer. A few tech documents with your name on them change how you are seen. Once a founder has seen you draft the IP clauses of a software contract, you are no longer only the person who files their mark.

If the batches keep coming

Your trademark practice can carry on comfortably. The quiet cost is that the most interesting IP questions of the next few years, about code, content and platforms, get answered by other lawyers while you renew marks.

05

What tech law work looks like for an IP and trademark lawyer

In a software development agreement, the clause that matters most to the client is IP assignment and handover. You make sure the code passes fully to the client, and you know the difference between source code and object code, which decides what is actually handed over.

With creators and brands, you draft influencer agreements, where the IP licence and content ownership clauses decide who can reuse a video and for how long. Platforms need terms that set out IP ownership for everything users upload.

On the dispute side, tech companies need takedown notices for copied content, action against trademark abuse on marketplaces, and cybersquatting complaints. Each builds directly on enforcement work you already do. The difference is that the client is a tech business with a steady flow of content, code and sellers to protect, so the work tends to repeat.
