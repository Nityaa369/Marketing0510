02

What changes when you have drafts to show

Tech law is the practice of advising technology companies on their contracts, privacy, compliance and disputes. It is built on documents, and documents can be judged on their own merit. A clean draft reads the same whichever college its author attended.

You could move into tech law at a firm's tech team, inside a startup, or by working directly with founders. Your entry point becomes a portfolio of real drafts, not the line on your degree certificate.

Evidence beats pedigree in a new field

This is a positioning problem, not a talent problem. You already read statutes, judgments and contracts closely. Once that skill is pointed at tech documents and shown in finished drafts, the college name matters far less.

What it costs to keep believing the filter

If you accept that tech law is closed to you, you stop trying, and the belief proves itself. The field is new enough that the starting line is unusually level. Three evenings will show you how others like you began.

05

What tech law work looks like for a non-NLU graduate

Start with the contract the camp teaches first: a software development agreement. You set out who builds what, by which milestone, to which specification, and who owns the code once it is handed over.

Then move to the documents startups need early. You review a young company's terms of use, check whether liability is capped and termination is clear, and flag that the privacy policy link is missing or weak. Then you write a short note to the founder explaining the fix.

Each task becomes a work sample with your name on it. When you write to a firm or a founder, you attach that sample. The reader sees your thinking first, and your college only after. Over time, the samples add up to a track record no admission list can take away.
