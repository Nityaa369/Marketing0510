01

Loan files and KYC every day, and the law degree on the shelf

You process loan files, check KYC, explain account terms to customers and follow circulars that change often. You know the paperwork of money better than most people outside a bank.

Yet when a document needs a legal view, it goes to the legal department. Your LLB does not appear in your job description, and your next promotion has nothing to do with it.

Meanwhile, the bank's own customers are moving to apps for loans and payments. You can see where the work is going. You are not sure where you fit.

02

Fintech law: banking knowledge, used as a lawyer

Fintech is one of the clearest parts of tech law, the legal work behind technology businesses. A lending app is a lending business with software in front, so the law on loans, KYC and data still applies. What changes is that the business runs on apps, partner contracts and APIs.

Moving into tech law could let you work on that side: drafting and checking the contracts and policies of fintech startups, or later joining a fintech's legal team. You would use the banking sense you have built, with your law degree finally doing the work.

05

What fintech law work looks like for an LLB holder in banking

Structuring is where fintech work often starts. A founder wants to lend, collect payments or offer a product with a partner bank. The lawyer maps which entity does what and which approvals apply, and you already know how the bank side thinks.

Contracts come next. White labelling, one of the Day 1 concepts, is how one company's product is sold under another's brand, as with many co-branded financial products. APIs are how a fintech app talks to a bank. Each shows up as clauses you would draft or review.

Then compliance and privacy. Payment and lending apps hold sensitive financial and personal data. Their privacy policies, sector filings and sandbox applications need someone who reads rules closely, which you have done with circulars for years.

06

Five words every tech contract uses

On Evening 1 you learn the five concepts behind most technology contracts. None of them needs coding.

SLAsA service level agreement sets the uptime a software provider promises, what counts as downtime, and the credits a customer gets when the promise is missed.
Support metricsHow fast a provider must answer and fix a problem, written as response and resolution times. Lawyers decide what happens when they slip.
APIsThe connection that lets one app talk to another. The contract decides who may use it, for what, and who is liable when it breaks.
Source code and object codeSource code is what programmers write. Object code is what the machine runs. Licences and ownership clauses turn on the difference.
White labellingSelling another company's product under your own brand. The agreement sets who owns what, the quality promised and who answers to the customer.
