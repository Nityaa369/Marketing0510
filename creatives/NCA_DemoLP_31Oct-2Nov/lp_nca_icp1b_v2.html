Three routes Indian lawyers try first

Each one is a sensible idea. Each one leaves the same gap: no Canadian work on your record when you need it.

Route 1A Canadian LLM before anything else

A year of tuition abroad and a year away from your practice, before you have done a single piece of Canadian work or know whether it suits you. The bootcamp explains why this is a gamble and what it does and does not prepare you for.

Route 2PR first, figure out the work after landing

Permanent residence gives you the right to live in Canada. It does not give a Canadian law office a reason to hire you. Lawyers who arrive with no Canadian work history start from zero.

Route 3Clear the NCA exams alone, from notes

People do clear them this way. You finish with a Certificate of Qualification, which is not a licence to practise, and with no Canadian client, no Canadian drafting sample and no one in Canada who knows your name.

The gap is not effort. It is where you stand when Canada looks at you.

You already argue matters, read judgments and draft contracts every week. A Canadian solo lawyer cannot see any of that, because it is all Indian work for Indian clients.

The lawyer who gets a reply is the one who has already reviewed a non-compete under Canadian law, drafted a Canadian employment contract, or fixed a Canadian startup's privacy policy. Same skills. Different position.

How the qualification path runs

  1. NCA assessment The NCA reviews your Indian LLB and assigns the exams you must clear.
  2. NCA exams Online, open book, three hours each, pass or fail, offered every month.
  3. Certificate of Qualification Your legal knowledge treated as equal to a Canadian law graduate's. Not a licence to practise.
  4. Ontario licensing The Law Society of Ontario's licensing exams and articling or the Law Practice Program, then the call to the bar.

As set out by the NCA (nca.legal). Since 1 March 2026 the NCA also screens English language ability and requires an Indigenous law competency; Day 1 covers both. Source: CIC News, March 2026.

Is remote paralegal work a step down for a practising lawyer?

No. It is Canadian legal work you can start before you are licensed there: research, drafting and review for a Canadian lawyer or startup. In Ontario, "paralegal" is a licensed title, so until you are licensed you offer this work as a legal assistant or legal researcher. The bootcamp shows you how to present it.

Can I write the NCA exams from India?

Yes. They are online and proctored, written from home.

Does remote work for Canadian clients count as Canadian experience for PR?

No. Canada counts experience for its Canadian Experience Class only when you are physically in Canada working for a Canadian employer (source: IRCC). The remote work is for skills, income and Canadian clients who know you. Day 3 covers how PR points work.

Do I have to move to Canada before I start?

No. You can write the NCA exams online from India. Moving is a separate decision you can make later, and the bootcamp shows you the order. VERIFY: nca.legal, exams online

Do I need a Canadian degree or an LLM first?

No. The NCA exists to assess law degrees from outside Canada, and you apply with your Indian LLB. The bootcamp explains where an LLM does and does not help. VERIFY: nca.legal, who can apply

My English is not perfect. Is that a problem?

Since 1 March 2026 the NCA screens English language ability for every applicant. Day 1 shows what that check is, so you can plan for it instead of guessing. VERIFY: nca.legal English requirement

I am a fresher. Is this too early?

It is a good time to start. Your degree is recent and the exams are open book, so the work is understanding Canadian law, not memorising. SCRIPT-CHECK: open book for 2026
