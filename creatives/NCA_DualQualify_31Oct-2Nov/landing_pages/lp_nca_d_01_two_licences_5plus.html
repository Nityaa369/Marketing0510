This page is for advocates, litigators and in-house counsel with five or more years of practice, who want a Canadian licence beside the Indian one they already hold.

You have one licence, and every year it is worth more

Five years in, your Indian licence carries real weight. Clients trust it, courts know your name, and juniors learn from you. But it is still a licence for one country.

When a client expands to Canada, or a family member asks about moving, the advice you can give stops at the border.

What you want: a second licence, not a second career

You do not want to start over in Canada. You want to dual-qualify: to hold your Indian licence and a Canadian one, so the same experience works in two markets.

Failed alternative 1: treating Canada as a fresh start

The common picture of Canada is a lawyer who sells up, moves and starts again. For a senior advocate, that throws away the most valuable thing you own: a running practice and a name.

Dual qualification is the opposite. You add, you do not replace.

Failed alternative 2: waiting for a sabbatical that never comes

Many experienced lawyers plan to look at Canada during a quiet year. A busy practice never offers one. The NCA exams are online and monthly, so they do not need a sabbatical. They need a plan that fits around the practice.

Failed alternative 3: sending clients abroad to someone else

When a client needs Canadian advice today, you refer them to someone else and the relationship moves with the matter. A second licence keeps that work, and that client, with you.

The problem is not your seniority. It is a one-country position

Seniority is not the barrier. Your skills are proven. The barrier is that your licence positions you in one country.

The NCA's Certificate of Qualification, and then a provincial licence, repositions the same experience as recognised in Canada too. It is an addition to who you are, not a change.

What one licence costs a senior lawyer

One licence quietly limits what your experience can earn and who can instruct you. Each year you stay single-licensed, the gap between what you know and where you may use it grows a little wider.

The NCA gives you five years to finish once you are assessed. Starting is the only part that cannot wait.

How a second licence is added, in 3 steps

  1. NCA exams, from India. The NCA assesses your LLB and assigns core subjects such as Canadian constitutional, administrative and criminal law, and professional responsibility. You write them online while you keep practising.
  2. Canadian work from India, alongside. Contracts valid in Canada, privacy policies for Canadian startups, research support for Canadian lawyers. It gives your second licence a track record.
  3. Bar licensing. With the Certificate of Qualification you take your province's licensing step, such as Ontario's licensing exams plus articling or the Law Practice Program. Then you hold two licences.
