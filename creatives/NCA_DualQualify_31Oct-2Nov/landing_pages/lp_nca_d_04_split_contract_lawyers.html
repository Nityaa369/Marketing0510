This page is for lawyers who draft, review or negotiate contracts for a living, at a firm, in-house or in their own practice.

Your documents are good enough for any market. Your licence is not

A shareholders' agreement you draft in Mumbai would read well in Toronto. The structure, the protections, the boilerplate: you know it all. What you cannot do is advise on it under Canadian law, because your licence stops at the border.

So the Canadian side of a deal gets drafted by someone else, even when your clients would rather keep it with you.

What you want: to draft the same documents for Canadian clients

You want to draft the same documents for Canadian clients, as a lawyer qualified to advise them.

Failed alternative 1: drafting for foreign clients without a licence

Some drafters sell contract work abroad on platforms without a local qualification. Serious clients notice, and you end up competing on price with every unqualified drafter online.

Failed alternative 2: waiting for a cross-border deal to come to you

Cross-border mandates go to the few lawyers already qualified on both sides. Waiting for one to arrive leaves your position exactly where it is.

Failed alternative 3: a general LLM in 'international law'

A general LLM in international law sounds relevant, costs lakhs and still does not license you to practise in Canada. A Canadian licence comes through the NCA route.

For a drafter with clients and a track record, a year abroad also means handing those clients to someone else while you study.

The problem is not your drafting. It is your position

Your drafting is not the gap. Canada, like India, is a common-law country. The gap is position: you are seen as an Indian-law drafter. Dual qualification makes you a drafter Canadian clients can instruct directly.

Some rules do differ, and they are exactly what the NCA exams test: how Canadian courts read restraint clauses, consumer protections and privacy duties. The bootcamp shows you where.

What a one-country licence costs a drafter

A one-country licence caps who can hire you for the documents you already draft well. The skill keeps growing; the market it is allowed into stays the same size.

It also caps the matters you see. The more interesting cross-border work goes to whoever is qualified on the Canadian side.

The route, in 3 steps

  1. NCA exams from India. The NCA assesses your LLB and sets the subjects. You write them online alongside your work.
  2. Canadian contract work while you prepare. Contracts valid in Canada, startup privacy policies and incorporation support, found on LinkedIn, Upwork and law-society directories.
  3. Bar licensing. With the Certificate of Qualification you take your province's licensing step, then advise Canadian clients as a qualified lawyer.

What a dual-qualified drafter's work looks like

Your client list stops at no border. Indian companies entering Canada, Canadian startups and businesses with work in both countries can all instruct you directly.

Inside a firm or legal team, you become the person who can take the Canadian side of a deal. In your own practice, you offer Canadian clients the same careful drafting you already offer Indian ones.
