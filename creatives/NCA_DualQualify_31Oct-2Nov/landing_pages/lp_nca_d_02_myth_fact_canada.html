This page is for anyone with an Indian LLB who wants to practise law in Canada and keeps hearing reasons it cannot be done.

Everything you have heard about Canada starts with 'you would need'

You would need a Canadian degree. You would need to move first. You would need to be younger, or richer, or from a different university. Every conversation about Canada seems to start with what you lack.

Most of it is out of date, and some of it was never true.

What you want: the facts, current to 2026

You want facts that apply to your degree in 2026, and a clear route you can act on.

That means three answers. Can my degree apply? What changed this year? And what exactly happens between applying and practising?

Myth 1: you need a Canadian law degree first

The fact: for an Indian LLB from a recognised university, the NCA usually assesses the degree you already hold and assigns exams. You do not need a Canadian degree to apply.

Many lawyers spend years and lakhs on a degree they did not need.

A Canadian degree remains a valid route for some people. For most Indian LLB holders, it is simply a longer and costlier one than they need.

Myth 2: you have to move before you can start

The fact: the NCA exams are online and proctored, so you write them from India. Only the later bar licensing step brings you to Canada, and by then you know exactly what you are moving for.

Myth 3: an English-medium LLB means no English test

The fact, since 1 March 2026: every NCA applicant now takes an English screening, whatever language the degree was taught in. Every applicant also completes a short Indigenous law course. Advice older than this year misses both.

Neither is a reason to stop. Both are short, defined steps, and once you know about them you can plan for them from the first week.

The real gap is not your degree. It is your position

Your degree is already common-law training. The real gap is position: today your LLB is recognised in India only. The NCA process positions the same degree as recognised in Canada. Facts close that gap; myths keep it open.

What believing the myths costs

Believing the myths has a price. Some lawyers pay for a degree they did not need. Others never start. Many wait for a moment that already came.

There is also a quieter cost. A lawyer who believes the route is closed stops reading about it, and so never learns when the facts change in their favour.

The route, in 3 steps

  1. Apply to the NCA and clear its exams. Your degree is assessed and your subjects are set. Add the 2026 English screening and Indigenous law course. Write the exams from India.
  2. Get the Certificate of Qualification. It puts you level with a Canadian common-law graduate for licensing.
  3. Pass the bar licensing, then practise. Each province runs its own. Ontario's is the licensing exams plus articling or the Law Practice Program. Ontario is reviewing its exams, so we teach what is current.
