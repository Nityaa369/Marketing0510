Three things property lawyers often try first

1

Taking more conveyancing files

More sale deeds keep the practice busy, but each client leaves after registration. That work does not create the ongoing business relationships that commercial contracts do, year after year.

2

Copying a commercial format from the internet

A downloaded format gives you clause headings. It does not tell you how a lock-in period or a service level should read for this client, so you hesitate to sign off.

3

Referring business work to a corporate firm

Referring keeps the client happy for now. It also teaches the client that a different lawyer handles their business, and that habit is hard to reverse later.

What conveyancing lawyers already carry into commercial drafting

Property work is drafting at its most careful. Every boundary, payment instalment and possession date has to be exact, or registration stops. That training is the base commercial clients want, and three parts of it transfer almost unchanged into business contracts.

  • Leases are already your territory. A commercial lease uses the rent, deposit and lock-in ideas you know, with added fit-out, signage and permitted use clauses. It is the natural first step.
  • You know staged payments and possession. Joint development agreements split built area and pay landowners in stages. Your sale deed and possession experience makes those clauses easier to draft correctly.
  • Clients already trust your signature. Shop owners and builders who used you for property already know your work. Offering them a lease or a vendor contract is an easy conversation to start.

Clause 4. Lock-in and Fit-out Period4.1 The Tenant shall have a rent-free fit-out period of sixty days from the Handover Date. 4.2 Neither party may terminate this Lease during the first thirty-six months (the Lock-in Period), except for material breach not cured within thirty days of written notice. 4.3 After the Lock-in Period, either party may terminate on ninety days' notice.
You know lock-ins from residential leases. A business tenant cares more about the fit-out period, so they read this clause first.
