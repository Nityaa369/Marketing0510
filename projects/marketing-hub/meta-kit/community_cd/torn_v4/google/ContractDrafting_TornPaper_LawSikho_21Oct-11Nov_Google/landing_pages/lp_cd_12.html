08

How your banking years help you draft deal papers

Bankers read documents for what can go wrong with money. That instinct is the core of good drafting. Your experience with due diligence, sanction conditions and covenants gives you a head start on the clauses founders and investors argue about most.

  • You read conditions precedent every day. Sanction letters list what must happen before money moves. A term sheet and a SAFE work the same way, so the structure feels familiar from your first draft.
  • You know what an investor checks. You have seen lenders ask for financials, approvals and disclosures. Representations and warranties clauses are those checks written as promises, and you will recognise each one.
  • You can talk numbers with founders. Founders often feel lost when valuation and dilution come up. You can explain the money side calmly, which makes your drafting advice easier for them to trust.

You draft a term sheet for a startup raising its first round

On term sheet day you write the key commercial terms of an investment: amount, valuation basis, investor rights and exclusivity. Other days add the SAFE, the ESOP and the warranties the company gives.

Clause 9. Exclusivity9.1 For forty-five days from the date of this Term Sheet, the Company and the Founders shall not solicit, negotiate or accept any investment offer from any person other than the Investor. 9.2 This clause and the confidentiality clause are binding; all other terms are subject to definitive agreements.
Like a lock-in on a loan offer. Most of a term sheet is non-binding, so mark clearly which clauses bind the parties.
