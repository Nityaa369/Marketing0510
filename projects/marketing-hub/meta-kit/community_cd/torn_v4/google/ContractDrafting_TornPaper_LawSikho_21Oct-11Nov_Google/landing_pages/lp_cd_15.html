What BPO and back-office work brings to drafting

Back-office work rewards the same habits that make a drafter reliable. You check details, follow a defined process and document everything. Those habits do not disappear when you switch to contracts. They make your drafts tidier from the start.

  • You catch errors others miss. Quality checks trained your eye. In drafting, the same eye spots a wrong cross-reference, a missing defined term or a date that does not match.
  • You work to a checklist. Processes run on steps. Contracts do too: parties, recitals, definitions, obligations, exit. Your comfort with structure helps you build a document in the right order.
  • You have served demanding clients. Meeting service levels for clients teaches you what they expect from a supplier. That understanding helps when you draft service agreements and the obligations inside them.

You start with the parts of a contract and build up

Day one breaks a contract into its parts. Each day adds a document, from NDAs to service agreements, until you draft a joint development agreement, where two companies build something together and must agree who owns the result.

Clause 8. Ownership of developed IP8.1 Each Party retains ownership of the intellectual property it owned before the Effective Date. 8.2 Intellectual property created jointly under this Agreement shall be owned by both Parties in equal shares. 8.3 Neither Party shall license jointly owned intellectual property to a third party without the prior written consent of the other Party.
Your habit of checking every field matters here. Separate what each side brought from what they built together, or ownership gets confused.
