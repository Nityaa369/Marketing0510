Three things HR professionals with an LLB often try first

1

A course in HR analytics

Useful for a promotion inside HR, but analytics keeps you on the people side of the table. Companies still send the agreement itself to a lawyer, and you remain the one forwarding it.

Reading the labour codes at night

Evening reading builds knowledge, yet nobody hires you for knowing sections by heart. Without drafting practice and work to show, the reading stays private and the job title stays the same.

3

Asking to shadow the legal team

A kind manager may let you sit in on reviews. You learn by watching, but you rarely hold the pen, and the drafts carry another lawyer's name when they go out.

What you bring from HR to contract drafting

HR work has trained you in things many fresh drafters lack. You know how people read a policy, where disagreements start, and what a manager actually needs from a clause. These habits carry straight into drafting. Here is how each one helps.

  • You know the employee lifecycle. From offer to exit, you have seen every stage. That helps you draft notice, probation and termination clauses that match how companies really run.
  • You hear the questions people ask. Joiners ask what a clause means. Those questions show you where wording is unclear, so your drafts can answer them before anyone needs to ask.
  • You already work with founders and managers. You know how to explain a policy to a busy manager. The same skill helps when you take instructions from a founder before drafting.

In week one you draft a non-compete clause for an employer

The non-compete day comes early. You write the clause, test it against the facts of a role you know well, and then fit it into a full employment agreement a few days later.

Clause 11. Non-compete and non-solicitation11.1 During the term of employment, the Employee shall not engage in any business that competes with the Company, directly or indirectly, without prior written consent. 11.2 For twelve months after the employment ends, the Employee shall not solicit any client or employee of the Company with whom the Employee dealt in the last year of service.
You have explained notice periods to joiners for years. See how the ban covers the job period, and only soliciting continues after exit.
