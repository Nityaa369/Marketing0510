Three things final-year students usually try first

1

Watching more lectures on contract law

You learn the theory again, neatly packaged. But watching someone explain a clause is not the same as writing one, so your folder stays empty when interview season arrives.

2

Hoping an internship will teach drafting

Some internships are excellent. Many keep you on research and file indexing for weeks, and you leave with a letter of thanks rather than a contract you wrote yourself.

3

Downloading sample contracts from the internet

Samples show what a finished contract looks like. They do not tell you why a clause is there, or what to ask a founder before you change a single word of it.

08

What final-year law students already bring to drafting

Your years in law school are not wasted time. You have trained your eye on wording, read judgments that turn on a single phrase and written long answers under pressure. Those habits are the base of good drafting. Here is how each one carries over into business contracts.

  • You read bare acts closely. Drafting is careful reading in reverse. The habit of spotting what a section leaves out helps you notice what a clause forgets to say.
  • You have studied contract law for exams. Offer, consideration, breach and remedies are the bones of every agreement. Now you will see where each one sits inside a real document a business signs.
  • You write long answers against a deadline. A client needs a clean first draft by a fixed date. Writing structured answers in exam halls has already trained you for that pace.

11

Your showcase draft: a co-founder agreement clause

Two friends start a company and split the shares equally. What happens if one of them leaves in the first year? You learn to answer that in writing, in a clause both founders can read, understand and sign.

Clause 6. Vesting of founder shares6.1 The Founder Shares held by each Founder shall vest over forty-eight months, with one quarter vesting on the first anniversary of the Effective Date. 6.2 If a Founder stops working full time for the Company before that anniversary, the Company may buy back all unvested Founder Shares from that Founder at face value.
An interviewer may ask why the one-year cliff exists. If you can answer in plain words, you stand apart from your batch.
