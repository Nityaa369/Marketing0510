07

Why content writing is a strong base for drafting

Drafting is writing for a reader who may one day disagree with you, so every word must hold. Writers who already think about their reader have a head start. Here is what your content work brings to contracts and policies from your first draft.

  • You write for a specific reader. Briefs taught you to ask who reads the page. A privacy policy is read by customers and, later, by lawyers on the other side. Knowing your reader helps you write for both.
  • You edit without mercy. Cutting a blog to its word count is good training. Contracts improve the same way when every vague word and repeated idea is removed.
  • You understand how websites work. You have seen sign-up forms, cookie banners and checkout pages from the inside. That helps you draft terms and policies that match what the website really does.

The rules differ, but the habits overlap. You will learn defined terms, obligations and how clauses fit together, starting from the parts of a contract. Your habit of clear, reader-first sentences helps from the first day.

Some documents are long, but the program teaches one at a time, a day each. The aim is drafts that are precise and readable, which is where your editing habit pays off.
