What sales and operations work gives a contract drafter

Many contract disagreements begin with a gap between what was sold and what was written. You have seen that gap from the inside. That experience makes you a practical drafter, and here is where you will notice it most.

  • You know what was promised on the call. You remember uptime promises, delivery dates and discounts agreed in meetings. That helps you write a scope and service levels that match the real deal.
  • You handle vendors and timelines. Operations work shows you where suppliers slip. Vendor service agreements need clear deliverables, acceptance and exit terms, and you already know why each one matters.
  • You speak the language of business teams. Sales heads and finance teams trust people who understand targets. You can take their instructions quickly and turn them into clauses they actually read.

You draft a SaaS agreement for a software company's customers

On SaaS day you draft the subscription terms between a software company and its business customers. You set out access, fees, service levels and renewal. The vendor service agreement and commercial lease follow on their own days.

Clause 5. Service levels5.1 The Provider shall make the Platform available for at least 99.5 percent of each calendar month, excluding scheduled maintenance notified forty-eight hours in advance. 5.2 If availability falls below this level, the Customer shall receive service credits as set out in Schedule 2, as its sole remedy for the shortfall.
You have heard sales promise near-perfect uptime. Write the number the team can really meet, and say what the customer gets if availability slips.
