08

What company secretaries already bring to founder contracts

Your secretarial training is a strong base for startup drafting. Founder and investor documents are full of share capital, vesting, board seats, consent rights and approvals, which is ground you already cover every week. Here is how that knowledge carries over directly.

  • You know the share capital table. Cap tables, allotments and transfers are your daily work. That makes ESOP (employee stock option plan) pools and dilution clauses feel familiar from your first draft.
  • You know what the board must approve. Reserved matters and consent rights in a co-founder agreement or term sheet mirror the approvals you already track. You can draft them so they work in practice.
  • You write formal, precise language. Resolutions and minutes have trained you to write without ambiguity. Contracts reward exactly that habit, especially in vesting schedules, exit terms and investor rights.

You draft a co-founder agreement and an ESOP vesting clause

In the community you practise the co-founder agreement and the ESOP on separate days. The clause below joins them: what happens to a founder's shares when that founder leaves before vesting is complete.

Clause 6. Founder Vesting and Leaver Terms6.1 The Founder's Shares shall vest monthly over forty-eight months from the Effective Date, subject to a twelve-month cliff. 6.2 If the Founder ceases to be engaged by the Company before full vesting, the Company may buy back the unvested Shares at their face value within ninety days of the leaving date.
You will record this buyback in the register later. Drafting it first means the board approval and the filing match what the founders agreed.
