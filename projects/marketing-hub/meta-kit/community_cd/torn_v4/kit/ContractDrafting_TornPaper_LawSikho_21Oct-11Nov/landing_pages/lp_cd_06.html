Three things small practice lawyers try first

1

Taking more matters of the same kind

More recovery suits and notices fill the diary, but the clients still arrive after the damage. The work stays reactive, and each fee depends on how long the dispute runs.

2

Downloading contract formats from the internet

A format gives you headings, not judgement. You cannot explain to a founder why each clause is there, so you hesitate to offer the draft as your own work.

3

Waiting for referrals from existing clients

Your existing clients send you people with disputes, because that is what they know you for. Referrals repeat your current practice; they rarely bring a startup founder to your door.

05

Draft an IP licence clause a founder can rely on

Startups license software, designs and content all the time, often on a handshake. In the community you draft the IP (intellectual property) licensing clause, and learn the questions that decide its scope, term and payment.

Clause 9. Licence of intellectual property9.1 The Licensor grants the Licensee a non-exclusive, non-transferable licence to use the Licensed Software in India for the Term, solely for the Licensee's internal business. 9.2 The Licensee shall pay the royalty set out in Schedule 2 within 15 days of each invoice. 9.3 All rights not expressly granted remain with the Licensor.
You have argued over vague licences in court. Here, scope, territory and term are written down before anyone signs, so that argument never starts.
