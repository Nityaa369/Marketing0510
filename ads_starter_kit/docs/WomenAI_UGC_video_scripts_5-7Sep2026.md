---

## The one thing that changes because it is a guy

He is not the customer. He cannot say "I bill those hours" or "nobody asked me about my
gap" — that is the woman's line and in his mouth it is a lie the viewer can smell.

So he has exactly two honest positions, and every script below is one of them:

- **The explainer.** He is telling her about a thing that exists. Second person throughout.
  "If your house is quiet from nine to two, those five hours are billable."
- **The messenger.** He is telling the man watching to send it to his wife, sister or mother.
  First person about *himself*, not about her. "My sister has a degree she has not used in
  six years."

Both work. The messenger angle is the one that is actually stronger for a male speaker,
because it is the only thing he is genuinely the expert on. It also reaches a buyer the
statics never targeted: the husband or brother who pays the ten rupees.

## What the past women/AI ads tell us

1. The ICP is a **situation**, never a job title. Quiet house, career gap, night shift,
   school hours, small city.
2. One sentence always defines the field, word for word:
   *"AI work means using tools like ChatGPT to do the writing, design, admin and
   customer-reply tasks businesses pay for."* Drop it and the ad reads as cryptic.
3. Best measured angles: **quiet house** (4.27% CTR, the highest), **career gap** (4,332
   conv), **night shift** (5,394 conv), **not too late** (3.30% CTR).
4. Biggest objection is **trust**, not price.
5. **Never use AI fear.** Offer a better job, do not threaten. No income figures.

## What Google says, and what it means for UGC

ABCD: **Attract** and **Brand** in the first 5 seconds, **Connect** by showing proof not
claims, **Direct** with one specific CTA said out loud and on screen.

Two notes for this format:

- **Proof, without a screen to show.** A UGC guy cannot demo anything. His proof is
  specificity: "three afternoons, two to five, nine hours total, ten rupees" is checkable.
  "It changes lives" is noise. Every script has one line whose only job is to be too specific
  to be invented.
- **Brand early but casually.** He says "SkillArbitrage" out loud inside the first ten
  seconds. No logo bug in the corner for the first cut — that is what makes UGC read as an
  ad. Put the lockup on the end card only.

Lengths: 45s for in-stream, 30s and 15s vertical for Shorts, 6s bumper for retargeting only.

## Shape of every script

Who it is for → the turn → what AI work is → one very specific detail → what she gets →
dates and price → one CTA.

## Script 5 · "Ten rupees" · 90s long form · ~250 words

**This is the lead script.** The hook works because ten rupees is a genuinely strange number
and the viewer stops to find out why. But the same number creates the objection two seconds
later: *what is the catch.* A 30 second cut raises that and never answers it. This version
exists to answer it, and answering it is the whole job, because trust is the number one
friction on this audience across 5,926 recorded calls. Nothing else in the script is doing
real work.

Runs as skippable in-stream. Also the best of the five for retargeting anyone who watched
another cut and did not book.

**Why it is built in this order.** The price is the hook, so it stays on screen alone for four
seconds with nothing competing. Then it hands straight to the value: "here is what ten rupees
buys you" is a hinge line, and everything after it is the better job. The value prop is fully
delivered by about forty seconds, which is the point of the whole restructure. The catch comes
after, at a minute, once she already wants the thing. Raising the objection before she wants
anything just gives her a reason to leave.

The catch is still said out loud, and still by him, unprompted. "Live only, no recordings" is
normally a compliance line we are obliged to carry. Positioned here it reads as the reason the
price is ten rupees, which is the cheapest trust this format can buy, and trust is the number
one friction on this audience. "If you can use WhatsApp you can do this" is lifted from the
Wave 4 cell built directly on that finding.

## Shooting UGC so it does not read as an ad

- **Phone, held, selfie.** Slight handheld movement is correct. A tripod and a ring light
  kills it.
- **A real room or outdoors.** Car, balcony, street, desk with actual mess on it. Not a
  studio, not a plain wall.
- **Casual, not corporate.** T-shirt. No blazer, no lapel mic on show.
- **No intro.** First frame, first word. No "hey guys", no name, no channel intro.
- **Hinglish.** Matches the statics and it is how this actually gets said out loud.
- **Jump cut every 8 to 10 seconds.** Cut the pauses out. A clean unbroken take reads as
  scripted.
- **He can use his own words** for everything except the field-definition sentence and the
  offer lines, which are said as written.
- **Captions burned in**, high contrast. Most views are sound-off.
- **Logo on the end card only**, not a corner bug throughout.
- **Audio over picture.** Phone close in, or a clip mic hidden. Room echo is the giveaway.

## Rules

- **He never claims a result he did not get.** No "I make money from this." If he did not do
  the camp, he does not say he did.
- No income figures, no rate, no monthly number. Not spoken, not on screen.
- No job or placement guarantee.
- No AI fear. Test: if the line still works with "or you will be left behind" added, cut it.
- No disclaimer or hedge before the CTA.
- Say "live only, no recordings" in every cut of 30 seconds or more.
- Price always as "ten rupees including GST, refundable any time you ask."
- SkillArbitrage only. Never with LawSikho.
- One script, one landing page. Test-buy the ₹10 link in a real browser before spend.

## Open

- Approve the five angles before anything is filmed.
- **Script 5 says "twenty-nine guides."** That number comes from the kit README. Confirm it is
  still 29 before he says it on camera, because it is the one checkable claim in the script and
  a wrong one costs more than it is worth.
- **Script 5 deliberately does not break the three days down day by day.** A day-one /
  day-two / day-three list would be stronger, but nothing in this repo records the actual
  running order, and inventing one is a claim we cannot stand behind. If someone can supply
  the real breakdown, it goes in at 0:58 and the script gets better.
- **Script 1 targets a different buyer than the rest** — the man who sends it, not the woman
  who attends. It needs its own ad set and probably its own landing page, since the page
  should say "for her" not "for you." Worth testing, but do not pool it with the other four
  and read the numbers as one thing.
- Five landing pages and five ₹10 funnel slugs do not exist yet.
- `check_all.py` only reads statics, so the rules above were checked by hand.
