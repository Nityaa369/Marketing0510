## US ACCOUNTING

**ICP callout as it ran:** "Indian accountants who want US clients."

**Awareness:** solution aware. The reader knows offshore accounting work exists and may already
be doing it inside an agency salary. What is missing is the route to the client, not the concept.
So a new ad should not spend a fold explaining that US clients exist. If a new ad is written for
a **problem aware** slice (a B.Com in a Rs 25k local job, unaware the same close is bought
abroad), the fold has to change shape, not just the headline. Do not ship five ads that are all
solution aware; that is the failure preflight now warns on.

**better_job:** Do the same monthly close for the payer who pays properly for it, and own the
client relationship instead of sitting behind an agency's invoice.

**field_definition (Rule 2 step 4), already proven:** US bookkeeping is closing a US business's
books each month in QuickBooks or Xero, running payroll through Gusto, and handing them a
tax-ready pack.

**Deliverables, as shipped:**
- Three live sessions covering every service on the chart: monthly closes, cleanups, payroll,
  tax-season work and reporting packs
- The US toolstack that ships with the work: QuickBooks Online, Xero, Gusto
- The chart's full rate card with sources, so you can price your own work
- Session notes, private WhatsApp group, certificate

**Qualifier row material:** B.Com, M.Com, CA-Inter, accounts executives, people already doing
books, AR/AP, payroll or reconciliations for Indian clients or an employer.

**The strongest single hook line, already approved:**
> The ledger does not change. The payer does.

**Money-ladder figures (published, LP only):**

| Same monthly close | Payer | What the client pays |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly books | Indian client | Rs 2,000 to Rs 5,000 a month |
| Same close | US agency or BPO buying it offshore | $200 to $400 a month |
| Same close | US small business, direct | $300 to $1,500 a month |
| CFO-lite dashboard | US client, direct | $3,000 to $5,000 a month |

**Other dots worth their own ad angle.** Each is a sourced, buyable service, and each is a
different hook:

- **Catch-up books**, 12 months behind: $1,500 to $3,500, and $3,500 to $8,000+ for a year
  plus. Highest-ticket pure bookkeeping project on the chart, spikes Nov to Mar.
- **1040 season support**, Jan to Apr: the structural demand engine. IRS lost 27% of staff in
  a year, paper backlog 5.6x, 73% of firms turning clients away.
- **Law-firm trust books (IOLTA)**: smallest buyer pool, fattest premium, $600 to $3,000 a
  month by firm size. A good "specialist corner" ad.
- **Shopify / Amazon books**: fastest-growing vertical, $300 to $3,000+ a month by channel count.
- **QuickBooks Desktop sunset to Xero migration**: 2026 is a switching wave, $399 to $4,000
  a project.
- **Sales-tax filings**: TaxJar's Feb 2026 hike reset the benchmark to $50-55 a filing, so a
  five-state client is roughly $200-275 a month.

**Compliance guardrails carried from the fact bank, non-negotiable:** all chart and ladder
figures are what the CLIENT pays, never presented as Indian earnings. Salary framing stays
TRAINED Rs 50-90k/month and EA-credentialed Rs 1L+/month. Say "EA-relevant openings", never
"EA jobs". No US salary figure may be implied as Indian pay. USD to INR at Rs 88, and state
the conversion on the creative.

**Rows marked UNVERIFIED in the research must not be plotted or quoted:** the India column
for catch-up, standalone bank-rec, QBO-to-Xero, CFO-lite, inventory and e-commerce books.

## INDEPENDENT DIRECTOR

**ICP callout as it ran:** "Senior professionals who want board seats."

**Awareness:** problem aware at best, and for many readers **unaware** that the seat market
below the Nifty-50 even exists. That is the whole opportunity in this camp. Most retired CXOs
think "board seat" means one unreachable listed seat, and do not know that expert-network calls,
incubator mentoring, SME boards and advisory seats open constantly. An ad that shows the
**bottom of the ladder as the entry door** is a genuinely different ad from the one that ran.

**better_job:** Be paid for judgment by the seat rather than by the hour, and start on a real
seat this year instead of waiting to be invited onto a listed board.

**field_definition (Rule 2 step 4), already proven:** An independent director sits on a
company's board, is paid a sitting fee per meeting plus a commission, and serves on the audit
or nomination committee. Keep a definition of that plainness on every new face. This ICP is
the one where "cryptic" bites hardest, because the vocabulary sounds familiar but the job is
not known.

**Deliverables, as shipped:**
- Three live sessions mapping every seat type: SME and unlisted boards, listed seats, PSU
  routes, advisory boards, expert networks
- How boards actually find their directors: the databank, the search firms, the referral
  routes, and where your profile must sit
- The chart's full rate card with sources: sitting fees, commissions and retainers by company type
- Session notes, a board-ready profile template, private WhatsApp group, certificate

**Qualifier row material:** 15+ years as a CXO, functional head, CA, CS, banker or entrepreneur;
50s and 60s; planning the next innings.

**The strongest single hook line, already approved:**
> The experience does not change. The seat does.

And the sharpest supporting line, which is the real value prop:
> Your decades of experience are the qualification. The route is what is missing.

**Money-ladder figures (published, LP only):**

| Seat | Source | Pay |
|---|---|---|
| Incubator mentor | AIM / NITI Aayog guidelines | honorary, up to Rs 2L a year, and where board CVs get built |
| Expert-network call | published expert-network rates | Rs 17,000 to Rs 87,000 per call |
| SME / small-cap board seat | Prime Database FY24 | about Rs 4L a year |
| Nifty-500 company | Prime Database FY24 | Rs 28L a year median |
| Nifty-50 company | Deloitte 2025 / Prime Database FY24 | Rs 87.4L a year median |
| Audit committee chair | Deloitte 2025 | ordinary ID pay +25 to 50%, a worked large-cap seat about Rs 1.2Cr |

**Demand engines.** Each is a separate ad angle, and all of them are seats OPENING, never
companies in trouble:

- **45,022 IDs registered on the IICA databank, 4,495 companies searching it, 20,050 searches.**
  The databank is a concrete artefact and an obvious hero device for an ad face.
- **549 ID seats vacated in FY25** (Prime Database), plus five-year terms expiring on schedule.
- **Record 2025 IPO year: 103 mainboard plus 270 SME.** Every SME IPO is a new board.
- **534 companies on NSE Emerge**, the least competed seats on the chart.
- **Woman independent director mandate:** the top-1000 listed companies must have at least one,
  8 Nifty-100 firms still had none in FY25, and women's ID pay grew faster than men's FY20-25.
  This is a clean, positive, regulation-driven ad for a woman ICP, and it is not a threat.
- **16 Nifty-100 firms were short of the ID minimum in FY25.**
- **Average Top-200 ID age is 64.1** (Russell Reynolds). Age is the qualification here, which
  is the exact inverse of the rejected "at 45+, your judgment is what X needs" ad. State the
  work, not the age.

**Hard bans on this ICP:**
- Never use "the average Nifty-50 independent director earns Rs 3 crore". That is a
  per-company aggregate mislabelled as person-level. The correct person-level figures are
  median Rs 87.4L and average about Rs 1Cr FY25 (Deloitte).
- Never frame a seat as a liability, a compliance exposure or a regulator problem. Rule 1.
  Every demand line stays "seats are opening", not "boards are in breach".
- Startup advisor equity (0.15 to 1%, FAST standard) is a callout, not a plotted pay dot,
  because the ranking is cash only.
- Rows marked UNVERIFIED must not be quoted as sourced: PSU annual totals, committee add-on
  bands, trustee sitting fees, strategy-day rates, and the India advisory-retainer band.
