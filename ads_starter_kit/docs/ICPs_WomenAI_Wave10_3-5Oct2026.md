# Women AI (Remote work revolution for women) · 3–5 Oct 2026 · wave 10 — 20 ICPs

Built 1 Oct 2026, after Ruchika: *"I want to create ads for Women AI since the price for
that is rising as well"*, with the brief: the readers that have worked or sit next to them,
the design that has worked **approved before building**, twenty ads with no riddle in the
headline, and landing pages whose value proposition is **completely different** from the
pages already live for this bootcamp.

Design approved by Ruchika on 1 Oct 2026: **struck list + clock band**.

## 1. The cost rise, diagnosed before anything was built

Live campaign `WomenAI_FREE_3-5Oct-2026_BC_CBO_v1` (Skill Arbitrage). This is a **free**
camp, so the unit is cost per registration, never a ₹10 CPP — the two must never be blended
(`feedback_ads_report_columns`). Day by day, 20 days to 1 Oct:

| day | spend | registrations | cost/reg | CPM | CTR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 19 Sep | ₹28,403 | 1,038 | **₹27** | ₹98 | 1.87% |
| 21 Sep | ₹37,354 | 1,231 | ₹30 | ₹123 | 2.32% |
| 23 Sep | ₹38,198 | 1,053 | ₹36 | ₹123 | 2.04% |
| 25 Sep | ₹45,196 | 1,171 | ₹39 | ₹159 | 2.29% |
| 27 Sep | ₹48,008 | 1,288 | ₹37 | ₹150 | 2.23% |
| 29 Sep | ₹34,877 | 988 | ₹35 | ₹144 | 2.32% |
| 30 Sep | ₹41,566 | 1,116 | ₹37 | ₹124 | 2.05% |
| 1 Oct | ₹33,619 | 835 | **₹40** | ₹137 | 2.25% |

**₹27 to ₹40 in twelve days, a 48% rise off the floor.** 30-day par is ₹36/registration on
16,467 registrations and ₹5.88L.

The mechanism matters, because it decides what is worth building:

| | 20 Sep | 1 Oct | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| CPM | ₹117 | ₹137 | **+17%** |
| CTR | 2.07% | 2.25% | +9% |
| registrations per click | 0.170 | 0.152 | **−11%** |

So: the auction got dearer, people still click at least as readily, and **fewer of them
register after landing**. That is not primarily creative fatigue. A face that has stopped
earning attention shows up as falling CTR, and CTR has not fallen. **The decay is on the
page**, which is why the landing-page half of this brief is the higher-leverage half.

By placement over the same window: Instagram ₹37/reg on 63% of spend, Facebook ₹40 on 29%,
WhatsApp ₹22 on 8%. No placement artefact here — WhatsApp is cheap but small, and its CPM
of ₹54 is not dragging the blended figure the way Status did on Data Privacy
(`feedback_whatsapp_status_fakes_frequency`).

## 2. Why the pages are the problem, measured

The three live pages carrying **10,700 of the 16,467 registrations** run an **identical H2
sequence** and change only the H1:

| H2, in order | `11-cpp112-r3-stop-waiting` | `13-cpp144-n08-her-fees` | `18-cpp107-w6-x4-three-hours` |
|---|---|---|---|
| What you actually get, free | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| 10 kinds of simple work… | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Which industry will value you… | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| You are likely in the right room if | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Saturday, Sunday, Monday. Two to five. | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| How the money builds | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| The doubts women actually raise with us | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |

This is exactly the Rule 4b defect the founder reported on Wave 8 — *"they see different
ads, and then they click on it, but the lp is the same over and over again, making them
disinterested"* — and it is live right now on the pages taking two thirds of the spend. A
woman who sees three of these ads and clicks two lands on the same page twice.

## 3. The design, measured and approved

Every live creative pulled from Meta on 1 Oct and classified by eye, then indexed against
its **own** campaign's par, 20+ results only, weighted by results.

| design | index | results | band | reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **struck list** | **0.88** | 5,327 | ±3% | under par |
| **clock band** | **0.93** | 856 | ±7% | under par |
| pay dot-plot | 0.96 | 320 | ±11% | at par |
| day list | 0.97 | 269 | ±12% | at par |
| label rows | 1.01 | 5,211 | ±3% | at par |
| day-part cards | 1.07 | 3,445 | ±3% | over par |

**The struck list and the clock band are the only two under par with a band that clears
1.0.** Both are new `adkit` layouts, built 1 Oct off the live creatives, with their
provenance and cost recorded in `adkit/layouts_custom.py`.

**One finding worth keeping:** the same struck-list copy ran as two palette variants and
scored 0.82 (n=3,104) and 0.97 (n=2,223). Identical words, identical device, 18% apart,
well outside both bands. Palette is not decoration on this product
(`feedback_same_copy_different_face_10x`).

**The honest caveat.** Design and reader are confounded here as they always are: the struck
list carried the money-pressure readers and the clock band carried the hours readers. The
aggregate is the right bet, not a proven law (`project_style_index_sep23`).

## 4. The value proposition, and why it is not what is live

Camp facts are sourced from the camp's own live funnel page, read 1 Oct 2026. Women AI is
**not in `docs/October_2026_bootcamp_scripts.md`** — the 3–5 Oct week lists Senior AI, RE US
Corporate Law and RE Data Privacy only — so the funnel page is the source of record here,
and every claim below traces to it.

What the camp actually does: nine hours live across Saturday, Sunday and Monday, 2 to 5 PM
IST, free. Day 1 the map of remote work foreign clients pay for, and she leaves with her own
shortlist of three. Day 2 the profile and a one-page work sample, written live on her own
background. Day 3 where the clients are, what to send and in what order, and she leaves with
a written outreach plan and her first ten names. Notes, templates and the attendee group on
WhatsApp. A certificate of participation. No recordings. **They do not find clients for her**
— the page says so plainly.

**The live pages argue a catalogue**: ten kinds of simple work, which industry will value
her, and how the money builds from small work to repeat work to specialised work.

**This wave argues the opposite end of the same three days: what exists on her screen on
Monday night that does not exist today.** Three things, all named on the camp's own page and
none of them the argument on any live page:

1. her shortlist of three kinds of work, chosen for her hours
2. her profile and one finished work sample, written in the room
3. her outreach plan and the first ten names, with the first message drafted

The camp page admits the sending is hers to do. The live pages treat that as a footnote in
the last section. **Here it is the whole argument**, because it is the real bottleneck: this
reader does not lack a list of jobs, she lacks a first message she is willing to send.

This is Rule 1 in its plainest form — a different and better job, nothing threatened — and
it answers the blind reader's third question (*what will I be able to do on the last day
that I cannot do today?*) literally rather than by implication.

**Compliance carried into every line.** Free camp, so no price claim beyond "free for all
three days". No income promise and no earnings figure on any ad face; the $10–20/hour
market band and the ₹50,000 figure women name in counselling calls stay on the page,
labelled as what they are, and never on a face. No placement guarantee. Live only, no
recordings. No em dashes or en dashes. No "this is not for you if" block. SkillArbitrage
only, never co-branded.

## 5. The twenty readers

The axis both proven winners share is **a named block of free hours plus a money reason**.
Every reader below is a situation, not a credential — a practising credential has failed as
an ICP for this camp three separate ways (`feedback_credential_icp_cannot_work`).

### Part A — proven, rebuilt with a new argument and a new face (5)

1. **Single mothers on one income** — every bill lands on one salary and there is no second
   earner to absorb a bad month. Live: **0.83× par**, n=3,175, ±3%. The cheapest reader on
   this product at scale, across both the Oct free camp and the Sep ₹10 camp.
2. **Mothers free between drop-off and pick-up** — a four-hour weekday window that is
   already quiet and currently earns nothing. Live: **0.86× par**, n=360, ±10%. Under par in
   both campaigns, which only this reader and the one above manage.
3. **Women caring for family all day** — the care does not stop, and the paid work has to
   fit in the gaps rather than replace them. Live: 0.97× par, n=219, ±13%.
4. **Teachers who want an evening income** — the school day ends at a fixed hour and the
   salary does not move with the work. Live: 0.98× par, n=496, ±9%.
5. **Women on BPO night shifts** — already working foreign hours for foreign customers,
   paid an Indian salary for it. Live: 1.01× par, n=1,256, ±6%. At par, but the largest
   proven cell after the single mothers, and the only reader who already does the work.

### Part B — adjacent, same axis, never run (15)

6. **Nurses on rotating shifts** — whole weekdays off between duty blocks, and no local way
   to turn them into money.
7. **Wives who move with every posting** — a new city every two or three years, so no local
   employer is ever worth starting with. Remote client work is the only kind that survives a
   transfer.
8. **Households that have dropped to one income** — a salary stopped, and the gap has to be
   covered from home without waiting for a job search to land.
9. **Women who left a job at marriage** — trained, employed once, and out for years with no
   obvious way back in through a resume.
10. **Women running tuition at home** — already earning from their own hours, already selling
    their own time, and at the ceiling of what one town will pay per hour.
11. **Women on fixed bank or insurance branch hours** — a predictable 10-to-6 and a salary
    that rises on a scale, not on effort.
12. **Women on retail and showroom rosters** — long shifts, weekday mornings free, and pay
    that does not move with how well the day went.
13. **Women with a degree and no local employer** — the qualification exists, the jobs are in
    a metro, and moving is not on the table.
14. **Women already freelancing in rupees** — writing, design or tutoring sold locally, where
    the work is proven and only the payer is small.
15. **Women at home with elderly parents** — tied to the house on someone else's schedule,
    with real stretches of free time inside it.
16. **Engineers and IT women on a long career break** — the skill is intact, the gap on the
    resume is what every interview is about.
17. **Women whose maternity leave is ending** — a return-to-office date on the calendar, and
    a decision to make before it arrives.
18. **Salon and beauty workers with quiet weekdays** — weekends are full, Monday to Thursday
    is not, and the quiet days currently earn nothing.
19. **Women preparing for government exams** — years already invested, no income yet, and a
    need for paid work that does not cost the preparation.
20. **Women working unpaid in a family business** — doing the accounts, the orders and the
    customers for the family firm, with nothing arriving in their own name.
