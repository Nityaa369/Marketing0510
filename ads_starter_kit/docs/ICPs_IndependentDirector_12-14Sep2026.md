## 0. First, a correction to the brief

The product is filed as the **Retired** Independent Director Bootcamp, but nothing about the
seat requires retirement, and the shipped LP already says "Senior professionals who want board
seats" rather than "retired".

**Recommendation: drop "retired" from everything ad-facing.** Two reasons, both material:

1. **A serving executive can hold independent director seats.** SEBI LODR Reg 17A caps a person
   at 7 listed entities as an ID, and at 3 if they are a whole-time director or MD at any listed
   entity. The cap of 3 exists precisely because serving executives hold these seats routinely.
2. The still-employed 48-to-58 slice is the most solvent part of the population and the one with
   a real reason to act now, because the seat has to be built *before* the exit, not after it.
   Calling the camp "retired" hands that slice a reason to scroll past.

Keep "retired" as an internal kit name if the funnel already carries it. Do not put it on a face.

## 1. Who can actually attend — the eligibility gate

This matters more than usual, because an ICP that cannot legally take the seat is an ICP we
cannot sell to. Verified 27 Aug 2026:

- **Registration on the IICA Independent Directors Databank is mandatory** for anyone who wants
  to be appointed an ID of a covered company. Registration is required of everyone, including
  people exempt from the test.
- **The Online Proficiency Self-Assessment Test must be passed within two years of registration**
  or the name is removed from the databank.
- **Exemption from the test**, not from registration: broadly, service of not less than 10 years
  as a director or KMP in a listed public company or an unlisted public company with paid-up
  capital of Rs 10 crore or more; and a 3-year route for certain specified categories.
- **Section 149(6)** sets the independence criteria: integrity, relevant expertise and
  experience, no material pecuniary relationship with the company, not related to promoters or
  directors, no significant shareholding.
- **Tenure:** two consecutive five-year terms, then a three-year cooling-off. This is the engine
  under "boards refresh every year" and it is a genuine, non-invented cycle.
- **Every listed public company must have at least one-third of its board as independent
  directors.**

**What this gives us that we are not currently using.** The databank and the proficiency test
are the two most concrete, nameable objects in this entire field, and neither appears in the
camp's stated deliverables. A senior professional who has heard "board seat" as an abstraction
has never been told there is a register you must be on and a test you must pass or be exempt
from. That is Rule 2's field definition sitting there unused.

> **Product suggestion, for Ramanuj:** add "databank registration walked through, and whether
> your years exempt you from the proficiency test" to the deliverables list. It is checkable,
> it is nameable, and it converts a vague camp into a camp with a finish line. If the camp does
> not in fact cover this, say so and I will keep it off every face.

**The floor and the ceiling of the ICP band, set by the product:**

- **Floor:** roughly 15 years of senior experience for anything paid; **10 years for the free
  bootcamp**, which is where the LawSikho page sets its own bar ("finance, law, management,
  sales, marketing, research, or other related fields for ten years or more"). Below ten there
  is no seat to be had and no exemption route, so the ad would be selling a queue, not a job.
- **Ceiling:** anyone already sitting on two or more boards. They have the outcome the camp
  sells. `ICPs_20_Wave2.md`'s rule applies in reverse here: the deliverables cap how senior an
  ICP can usefully be, and "how boards find their directors" is worthless to someone boards have
  already found.

**The "practising credential cannot be the ICP" lesson does not apply to this camp.** That was
found on the women's remote-work product, where the deliverables were inbox and calendar work
and a credentialed reader read the offer as a demotion. Here the product is a board seat, the
credential is the qualification, and every route on the chart is a step *up* from the reader's
current identity. Do not carry that warning across. The self-image filter still runs, it just
comes back the other way.

## 2. The screen — five questions, all five must pass

Adapted from the D/S/G/N test used on the remote-women kit, refitted to this camp:

1. **ELIGIBLE** — can this person actually be appointed, or get onto the databank?
2. **SEATS** — is there a *counted* engine of seats that fit them? Not "boards exist".
3. **ROUTE GAP** — do they lack the route rather than the qualification? If they lack the
   qualification, the camp cannot fix it in three days.
4. **STEP UP** — does a board or advisory seat read as a promotion from the identity they
   currently claim? If it reads sideways, the ad will be ignored.
5. **PRESSURE** — a named force with something like a date on it. Never "wants to grow".

### ID-01 | COMPANY SECRETARIES

- **Identity band:** `COMPANY SECRETARIES`
- **Situation line:** You write the board's minutes. You have never sat in a seat.
- **Awareness:** **most aware.** They know precisely what an independent director is, what the
  sitting fee is, and what DIR-12 says, because they file it. So no education, no curiosity gap:
  this ad is offer and route, stated flat.
- **Buying pressure:** the CS ladder tops out at company secretary and compliance officer, and
  the pay curve flattens there. A seat is the only rung above the room they are already in.
- **Better job:** Sit in the board seat instead of servicing it, and be paid a sitting fee for
  judgment rather than a salary for the paperwork around it.
- **Route on the chart:** unlisted public company seats, SME and NSE Emerge boards, then listed.
  They already understand the eligibility rules, which shortens their runway more than anyone.
- **Why it is the strongest cell on the list:** it is Rule 1 in its purest available form. Same
  room, same vocabulary, other side of the table. It is also the exact shape of the approved
  corporate-finance ad ("a company secretary who handles the paperwork of a deal but never the
  deal"), which this account has already shipped and approved.
- **Screen:** eligible yes · seats yes · route gap yes · step up unambiguous · pressure named.

---

### ID-02 | CHARTERED ACCOUNTANTS IN PRACTICE

- **Identity band:** `CHARTERED ACCOUNTANTS`
- **Situation line:** Every audit committee needs a financial expert. There is one seat per board.
- **Awareness:** **solution aware.** Most practising CAs know ID seats exist and many already
  hold a DIN. What they do not have is the route, and they over-estimate how contested the seats
  below the large-caps are.
- **Buying pressure:** practice income is capped by billable hours and by how many clients one
  person can sign off for. A board seat is annual, and it does not stop when they stop billing.
- **Better job:** Be the financial expert who sits on the audit committee and reviews the numbers
  for the board, instead of preparing numbers for someone else's board.
- **Route on the chart:** audit committee member, then audit committee chair. Per the research,
  the chair seat carries 25-50% over ordinary ID pay, it is the rarest seat on the chart, one per
  board, and every board refresh reopens exactly one.
- **Note:** the independence rules bite on the company they audit, never on other companies.
  Handle plainly, never as a warning. Rule 1.
- **Screen:** all five pass. Highest pay ceiling of any cell here.

### ID-03 | CFOs AND FINANCE HEADS, STILL SERVING

- **Identity band:** `CFOs · FINANCE HEADS · FINANCE DIRECTORS`
- **Situation line:** You can hold a board seat while you are still in the job.
- **Awareness:** **unaware**, and this is the whole point of the cell. Most serving CXOs believe
  a board seat is something you take up after you retire. The SEBI cap of three listed seats for
  a serving whole-time director is proof of the opposite, and it is a genuinely surprising fact
  presented as an opening rather than a rule.
- **Buying pressure:** the corporate runway shortens sharply after 55, and a board CV is built
  over years, not after a farewell lunch. The seat has to exist before the exit does.
- **Better job:** Add a second, board-level income and a post-exit landing place while the
  current job is still running.
- **Route on the chart:** unlisted and SME seats first, advisory boards, then listed mid-cap.
- **Screen:** all five pass. This is the cell that widens the camp beyond "retired".

### ID-04 | RETIRING PSU AND BANK EXECUTIVES

- **Identity band:** `GENERAL MANAGERS · DGMs · EXECUTIVE DIRECTORS`
  (public sector banks, insurers, oil and gas, power)
- **Situation line:** Superannuation is on the calendar. Twenty working years are not over.
- **Awareness:** **problem aware.** They know retirement is coming and that they intend to do
  something. They do not know the seat market exists below the marquee names.
- **Buying pressure:** a fixed superannuation date at 58 or 60 that they can name to the month,
  a pension that is a fraction of last drawn, and two decades of working life left. This is the
  single most concrete pressure on the whole list.
- **Better job:** Be paid by the seat for the same sector judgment, instead of stopping the day
  the service ends.
- **Route on the chart:** PSU non-official director via the DPE search-committee route, which
  the research calls process-heavy but a standing pipeline, plus BFSI seats where the research
  puts audit chairs at the top of the pay band.
- **Screen:** all five pass. Strongest `buying_pressure` in the set.

- **Situation line:** Every one of the top 1,000 listed companies must have a woman on the board.

### ID-06 | FOUNDERS AND BUSINESS OWNERS WHO HAVE HANDED OVER

- **Identity band:** `BUSINESS OWNERS · FOUNDERS · PROMOTERS`
- **Situation line:** The business runs without you now. The experience has nowhere to go.
- **Awareness:** **unaware.** This reader thinks in terms of "advising a few people", not in

- **Buying pressure:** they sold, or the next generation took over, and for the first time in

## 5. How we bring them in

Three separate answers, because these ICPs do not arrive the same way.

### The entry-door problem, which is the real conversion lever

The reader's blocker is not eligibility and it is not desire. It is that **"board seat" sounds
like one unreachable thing.** Most of this population has priced themselves out of the category
in their own head before an ad reaches them.

The chart's bottom rungs are the answer, and they are currently the least-used asset we have:

- **Expert-network calls.** Per the research this is the highest-volume engagement on the entire
  chart, thousands of India calls a month, and **15-plus years of sector experience is the whole
  qualification.** No appointment, no databank, no test, no board. For an unaware reader this is
  the single most convincing proof that their experience is a purchasable thing.
- **Incubator mentoring.** AIM and NITI Aayog require Atal Incubation Centres to appoint at least
  two mentors, with an honorarium of up to Rs 2 lakh a year, and there are 100-plus AICs. The
  research's own phrase is the hook: **this is where board CVs get built.**
- **SME and NSE Emerge boards.** 534 companies on Emerge, 270 SME IPOs in 2025, and the research
  calls these the least competed seats on the chart.

So the acquisition ladder is: *the smallest rung is real and open to you today* to bring the
unaware in, and *the marquee seat is where this leads* to give it a ceiling. That is the same
ladder argument this account already uses everywhere, applied to seats.

### Devices worth building, one line each (full hook work is step 2, not now)

- **The databank as an artefact.** 45,022 individuals registered, 4,495 companies searching,
  20,050 searches. A plain register device, framed as *this is where boards look and here is how
  to be findable*. Never as *you are not on it*, which is deficiency framing and `simplicity.py`
  S2 blocks it anyway.
- **The seat ladder.** Incubator mentor to expert call to SME board to Nifty-500 to Nifty-50,
  drawn as rungs, with the reader's current position marked. Positions, no rupee figures, per
  the money rule.
- **The board table, one empty chair.** For ID-01 specifically: the minute-taker's seat against
  the director's seat.
- **The audit committee seat.** For ID-02: one board, one financial expert, one seat, reopened
  at every refresh.

### Targeting, in one paragraph

Age bands sit naturally at 45-65, splitting 45-55 for ID-03 and ID-07 and 55-65 for ID-04 and
ID-08. Every one of these ICPs is a job title, which means the targeting can be built from
titles and employers rather than interests. The credential research is best used exactly the way
the remote-women kit learned to use it: in targeting and on the landing page as proof, while the
ad face stays on the identity and the seat. **Account routing, CPP baseline and ABO/CBO are out
of scope for this file** and sit in `SOP/02_LAUNCH_SPEC.md` — I will leave the README's launch
spec marked `CONFIRM WITH RAMANUJ` unless you give me the routing.
