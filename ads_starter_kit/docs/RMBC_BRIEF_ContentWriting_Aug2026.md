### Awareness mapping - the one genuinely new thing in R

RMBC says awareness level sets how much education the copy has to carry before it can
sell. Mapping the five ICPs:

| ICP | Awareness | What that means for the ad |
|---|---|---|
| 3 ai-discount-request | **Problem aware.** Feels the squeeze acutely, has no name for it | Lead with the pain in their words. Mechanism does the work |
| 15 proofreader-editor | **Unaware.** Does not experience giving away judgment as a problem at all | Has to be shown the problem before the offer means anything. Highest education burden of the five |
| 19 agency-brief-taker | **Problem aware.** Knows exactly who gets the credit | Lead with the pain. Shortest education burden |
| 26 five-small-clients | **Solution aware.** Already wants fewer, bigger clients | Lead with the different unit, not the pain |
| 39 tier2-cost-base | **Problem aware, wrong diagnosis.** Believes the constraint is the address | Correct the diagnosis first. The evidence IS the lead |

**This changed three of the five ads.** Ad 02 carries the heaviest device because an
unaware reader has to be shown the problem before an offer can land. Ad 05 leads on the
evidence rather than on the pain, because the reader's own diagnosis has to be overturned
before anything else is heard. Neither of those calls comes out of the house playbook,
which treats all ICPs as equally problem-aware.

### The gap, stated plainly

**No competitor analysis exists.** RMBC treats it as one of four load-bearing pillars, and
this folder has nothing on what else a writer in this ICP is being sold at Rs 10 to Rs
500, what those offers claim, or where the positioning gap sits. That is a real hole and
it is not one an ad can paper over. `/competitor-offer-analysis` is the skill for it.

## M - Mechanism

This is what RMBC actually contributed. Everything else it did, the playbook was already
doing better.

RMBC's test for a mechanism is the "only we" test: a specific explanation of **why** the
thing works that no competitor can claim. The project had a spine, which is a positioning
statement, not a mechanism:

> AI writes the words now. It does not decide what to make, who to sell it to, or what the
> number has to be.

True, and every AI-era writing course says a version of it. It explains why the reader
should worry. It does not explain why nine hours produces a different price.

### The mechanism: the fourth part

> A campaign has four parts. The ad, the landing page, the follow up emails, and the
> tracking that shows what it sold.
>
> The first three are production, and production is exactly what got cheap. The fourth
> part is the number. The number is the only part a client cannot produce, cannot count
> down, and cannot haggle, because it is not an input. It is the result.
>
> Nobody is paid for the first three any more. Everybody is paid for the fourth.

**Why this is worth having.** The Rule 2 definition sentence was compliance furniture. Every
ad had to carry it and it sat there doing nothing, which is exactly why the playbook allows
dropping it when a device names the four parts. Under this mechanism the sentence becomes
the argument, because the fourth item in the list is the punchline. The reader reads a
four-item list and the last item is the one they have never owned.

It passes the "only we" test in a narrow but real way: the camp is structured as the four
parts across three evenings, so the mechanism is a description of the actual product rather
than a claim bolted onto it.

**It also survives Rule 1**, which the spine only barely does. The spine's second sentence
is one bad edit away from "AI is coming for you". The fourth part never mentions AI at all.
It says three things you already do got cheap and here is the fourth, which is a ladder.

### How each cell lands on it

| Ad | The pain | The fourth part, applied |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | The price is set by how long the typing took | The campaign has no input to count, so it is priced on what it returned |
| 02 | The fixing is billed, the margin note is not | The margin note is the decision behind all four parts |
| 03 | Three lines of the brief are filled by someone else | The fourth line is empty, and nobody on that floor owns it |
| 04 | Many invoices, many chases, one income | One brief, one price, one number at the end |
| 05 | The address sets the rate | No part of a campaign needs a metro address, and the customers are already local |

## B - Brief

| Element | Decision |
|---|---|
| **Angle** | Change the unit of sale. Never defend the writing, never mention the tool |
| **Tone** | Plain, flat, second person. No wordplay in a headline (14 Aug feedback). Reader at 20% brain function |
| **Proof strategy** | One D2C figure per ad, at payoff size, with its consequence attached. Never a strip of trivia under the punchline. Never the same figure twice in the pack |
| **CTA** | "Book my seat". The only urgency is true: three fixed dates, live, no recordings |
| **Risk reversal** | Rs 10 including GST, refundable anytime. Stated on every face and every page |

### Proof allocation

RMBC asks for at least two proof types per ad. Available here: statistical and logical.
**Testimonial and case study proof are not available** because no named past-cohort proof
exists in this project. That is blocker 4 in the wave 11 and bootcamp READMEs and it is
still open. Every ad in this kit therefore runs on statistical plus logical proof only,
which is one proof type short of what RMBC calls production ready.

| Ad | Figure | Why this figure for this ICP |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | 30 to 40% of revenue into digital advertising (Brandshark 2026) | The point is that the budget is continuous and is not measured in hours |
| 02 | 4,300 founders (Inc42 2026) | The buyer of judgment is a person making a call, not a company filling a role |
| 03 | Marketing and Communications hiring up 12% YoY (Naukri JobSpeak, Jun 2026) | The point is that the deciding roles exist and are being filled |
| 04 | 11,000 brands selling straight off their own website (Unicommerce 2026) | Scale. One buyer of a large thing instead of many buyers of small things |
| 05 | 66% of incremental order volume from Tier 2 and Tier 3 (Unicommerce 2026) | Reach. The reader's address is the asset, not the constraint |

Five distinct figures, five distinct consequences, no repeats.

## Where RMBC and the house playbook conflict

Three real conflicts. All three resolved in the playbook's favour, per the precedence note
in `CLAUDE.md`.

**1. The specificity gate.** RMBC: every claim carries a number, a name or a timeframe.
Playbook section 5: the reader's situation must be un-checkable, because "20 articles a
month" loses the writer who does 8 or 40. These are directly opposed on the reader's side
of the copy. Resolved: numbers live on the buyer side of the payoff only. Ad 04 is the test
case. RMBC would write "five clients, five invoices"; the ad says neither, and the device
shows six unnumbered chips.

**2. Fear and consequence hooks.** RMBC lists them as one of five hook types and the
`/hook-battery` skill generates them by default. Rule 1 bans them outright, and the
rejection record in `CLAUDE.md` is mostly this one mistake. Resolved: not used. Five cells,
five ladders.

**3. Manufactured urgency.** RMBC's CTA pattern is "specific action plus reason why now
plus risk reversal", and `/scarcity-urgency` exists to generate the middle term. The only
true scarcity here is that the camp is live on three fixed dates with no recordings.
Resolved: that, and nothing invented on top of it.

**Two more things RMBC wanted that were simply not usable:** a guarantee stronger than the
Rs 10 refund (no placement or income guarantee, ever), and a value stack with anchored
pricing (no salary ladder on an ad face, no income promise anywhere).

## What RMBC caught that the playbook does not cover

- **The awareness spectrum.** Three of five ads are structured differently because of it.
  The playbook has no equivalent and treats every ICP as problem aware.
- **The "only we" test on the mechanism.** It is what exposed the spine as a positioning
  statement rather than a mechanism, and produced the fourth part.
- **Proof-type diversity as a gate.** It is what surfaced, mechanically, that this whole
  campaign has been running on one proof type because no named cohort proof exists.

## What the playbook has that RMBC does not

Relatability. Section 5 of the playbook, the un-checkable situation, the reader's own
vocabulary, no pronouns in the payoff, the slab and the situation naming the same
population, is finer-grained than anything in RMBC and it is the thing that has actually
been causing rejections here. RMBC would have shipped several of the rejected ads.
