## The work, in the script's words
Drafting manuscripts (with co-authorship), literature reviews, systematic reviews, journal
formatting and submission, content and English editing for non-English-speaking professors
(Europe, Japan, Korea, China), data analysis, survey and field research "in the Indian context",
transcription and coding, pre-submission review, grant proposals, research communication (books,
articles, LinkedIn, podcasts), policy briefs and reports for think tanks, white papers, technical
documents, medical writing, course and e-learning material, ghostwriting books.
Professors need help four ways: research and writing; admin and liaison with journals; grants;
public-facing work.

## The clients
Professors and researchers in the US, UK, Canada, Europe, Japan and Korea; think tanks and policy
institutes (via project managers); corporate R&D teams and labs; deep tech and biotech startups;
writing agencies that serve global clients; edtech companies, coaches and experts building
courses; international NGOs; publishers; pharma, medical-device firms and hospitals (medical
writing).

## Skills, by day
Day 1: find and pitch professors abroad (Google Scholar, faculty pages; separate template for
Japanese, Korean and European professors); a literature review with AI (Claude, Trinka); five
levels of research automation and the free tools (Gemini, NotebookLM, Elicit, Consensus,
Semantic Scholar); network with think-tank project managers; run a free AI model on your own
computer (LM Studio); an outreach routine for when nobody replies.
Day 2: the 6-month roadmap (skills, track record, outreach); a grant proposal for a professor or
think tank (NIH format, live demo); pre-publication review with five AI agents - Literature
Reviewer, Citation Checker, Methodology Auditor, Devil's Advocate, "Reviewer 2" mock peer review -
"each a service you can sell"; finding projects on Kolabtree, People Per Hour and Guru (live).
Day 3: turning raw public data into a research report; freelance platforms incl. Mercor;
pitching edtech companies in London and globally; getting a book published with a publisher (and
selling that as a service); a research-to-content service with NotebookLM (podcast, video,
flashcards, quiz, study guide from one set of papers); preparing a brief before any client call;
a gap analysis of a foreign professor's research, extended into a survey study in India;
networking with deep tech startups.

## Given to attendees
Day 1: guide to descriptive, analytical, persuasive and critical writing; cold email templates for
international clients; Kolabtree profile optimisation checklist; how Indian PhD freelancers
attract international clients; ghostwriting guidebook.
Day 2: the 6-month roadmap; 30-day academic writing challenge (portfolio prompts); a guidebook on
medical writing for beginners; academic writing style guide; guide to technical writing in 2026;
top 10 sample grant proposals.
Day 3: consultancy agreement and invoice template; SEO basics for academic writers; time
management toolkit; lists of "10 industries you can pitch to" for technical, medical and academic
writers; a list of 25 directories; email templates for edtech, deep tech, publishers, professors.
Prep doc also lists: SCOPUS journal finder list of 500 journals; LinkedIn profile checklist.
Certificate: attend at least 2.5 hours on each of the three days; instructions shared as a PDF.
Giveaways each day need 2.5 hours' attendance that day.
NOT PROMISED: any WhatsApp group or community (none in any script). Do not claim one.

## Who can do it (quotable)
"Having masters or PhD is not necessary. In fact, school teachers, college students, and
professionals from any background can step into this work." Humanities graduates can. Medical
writing: "You don't need to be a doctor." Teachers can use existing skills for curriculum work.
Part-time alongside a job is covered.
