# Content Writing Bootcamp · 29, 30, 31 Aug 2026 (Sat–Mon) · ₹10 · SkillArbitrage — 40 candidate ICPs

Built on the reworked value proposition (21 Aug), not the D2C salary-ladder memo.

**Offer anchor:** 9 hours live across three evenings. Evening one: pick a buyer, write the ad,
make the creative yourself. Evening two: the landing page, the warm up sequence, the tracking.
Evening three: what campaign work is quoted at, and where to find businesses already spending.
₹10 incl. GST, refundable anytime, live only, no recordings.

**The spine every ad on this list runs:**
AI writes the words now. It does not decide what to make, who to sell it to, or what the number
has to be. That decision is the campaign, and the campaign is what gets paid.

**The Rule 2 sentence every ad must carry, in the first fold, no exceptions:**
> A campaign is the ad, the landing page, the follow-up emails and the tracking that shows what it sold.

**Rule 1 discipline:** every ICP below is framed as *what they already own that trades up*. Their
current work is never a liability. No ICP here sells more work at the same desk, and none of them
threatens the reader with AI. The machine took the floor; the reader climbs.

**Funnel discipline (from the ad-analysis house rules):** this is a ₹10 tripwire. Every ad sells
three evenings, not a career. No salary figures on any ad face. No income promise anywhere. Judge
these cells on cost per registration and click-to-buy, never on CTR.

**Existing coverage, so we know what is new.** Twelve broad ICPs already run across waves 1 to 5:
paid by the word · clients haggle · stuck in volume · cannot find buyers · in-house job stopped
growing · senior writers and editors · social media writers · women writing for a living · told AI
will replace them · hears "AI se ho jayega" · wants outcome clients · US SMB, dollar billing and EU
D2C. **Families A, C and D below are deliberately sharper versions of four of those broad ICPs**,
a named situation instead of a category. Families B, E and F are new territory.

## A · The unit of sale is the problem (1–8)

*Sharpens the existing "paid by the word", "clients haggle" and "stuck in volume" cells.*

1. **rate-frozen-three-years** — ₹1.50 a word in 2023, ₹1.50 a word today. The writing got better and the rate did not move, because word count is the only thing being measured. Hook: change what is being counted.
2. **word-count-haggle** — Client wants 1,200 words for the price of 1,000. Hook: a campaign has no word count to negotiate down.
3. **ai-discount-request** — "You used AI, so it took less time, so it should cost less." Asset: they are pricing the typing, not the deciding. Hook: quote the outcome and the argument disappears.
4. **per-article-treadmill** — Twenty articles a month, every month, income flat because it is capped by hours. Hook: one campaign priced once, not twenty pieces priced twenty times.
5. **invoice-line-item** — The invoice reads "4 blog posts, 1,000 words each". Hook: an invoice that reads "one campaign", with what it returned attached.
6. **portfolio-not-working** — Sends writing samples, hears nothing back. Asset: the samples prove writing, and nobody is buying writing any more. Hook: send a one page results note instead of a portfolio.
7. **no-number-to-show** — Cannot answer "what did your writing actually do for the business". Hook: three evenings that end with a number attached to your work.
8. **cheaper-writer-always** — There is always someone charging less per word, and there always will be. Hook: compete on the thing that has no per-unit price.

## B · Specialists whose specialism became the commodity (9–16)

*New territory. Each one owns a real skill that sits one step from campaign work.*

9. **seo-blog-writer** — Writes 1,500 words to a keyword brief. Asset: knows what makes a page get found. Hook: own the page the traffic lands on, and what happens next.
10. **technical-writer** — Documentation, manuals, release notes. Asset: makes complicated things clear, which is the hardest part of any ad. Hook: the same clarity, pointed at what the product sells.
11. **email-newsletter-writer** — Writes a weekly newsletter nobody measures. Asset: already writes sequences, just not the campaign around them. Hook: the follow-up emails are a quarter of a campaign. Learn the other three quarters.
12. **script-video-writer** — Reels and YouTube scripts to someone else's brief. Asset: writes the hook, the highest-value line in any ad. Hook: decide the hook, and decide what it is selling.
13. **ux-microcopy-writer** — Button labels, error states, onboarding flows. Asset: writes at the exact point where people decide. Hook: own the whole path, not one screen of it.
14. **translator-subtitler** — Paid per word or per minute to move copy between languages. Asset: knows two markets, which most campaign people do not. Hook: build the campaign for that market instead of translating someone else's.
15. **proofreader-editor** — Paid to fix other people's drafts. Asset: exercises judgment about what works every single day, and is paid for none of it. Hook: the judgment is the job that pays.
16. **ecommerce-catalogue-writer** — Three hundred product descriptions, priced per SKU. Asset: knows what makes someone buy at the last click. Hook: own what happens before they ever reach the listing.

## C · Employed, one desk, no ladder (17–23)

*Sharpens the existing "in-house job stopped growing" cell.*

17. **only-writer-in-company** — The entire content function is one person, and it is them. Asset: already doing four jobs. Hook: those four jobs have a name and a market price outside the building.
18. **marketing-team-of-one** — Writes, posts, emails and reports. No title change in three years. Hook: the title exists elsewhere.
19. **agency-junior-brief-taker** — Fills briefs written by an account manager. Asset: does the work the strategist gets credited for. Hook: write the brief instead of filling it.
20. **startup-content-hire** — Hired to "do content", never told what it is for. Hook: define the number, then own it.
21. **appraisal-no-raise** — Good review, no increment, budget constraints. Hook: a second income line that does not need an appraisal cycle.
22. **writer-in-name-only** — Job description says writer; the day is meetings, decks and coordination. Hook: one skill that is billable outside the job.
23. **notice-period-thinking** — Has decided to leave, has not decided what to leave for. Hook: three evenings before the resignation, not after it.

## D · Freelance, and the floor keeps dropping (24–30)

*Sharpens the existing "cannot find buyers" cell.*

24. **marketplace-floor** — Bidding against five dollars an article on Upwork or Fiverr. Asset: has already sold to strangers, which most employed writers cannot do. Hook: sell a different thing to the same buyers.
25. **lost-client-to-a-tool** — Retainer cancelled, "we will do it in-house with a tool". Asset: the client kept the production and lost the judgment. Hook: sell the part the tool cannot do.
26. **five-small-clients** — Five clients, five invoices, endless chasing, and the income of one salary. Hook: one campaign retainer instead of five small gigs.
27. **subcontracting-invisible** — Writes under another freelancer's name, at their margin. Asset: the client relationship is the only missing piece. Hook: evening three is how you find your own.
28. **solo-shop-owner** — Runs a two or three person content shop selling pages and posts. Hook: sell campaigns instead, and change what the shop is worth.
29. **feast-famine** — Two good months, two empty ones, and no way to know which is coming. Hook: outcome-priced retainers renew. Per-piece work does not.
30. **never-raised-rates** — Has not raised a rate since starting, because there has never been an argument for it. Hook: the argument is the number the last campaign returned.

## E · Already write for a living, in another trade (31–35)

*New territory. Highest Rule 1 clearance in the list: the existing craft is plainly the asset.*

31. **journalist-newsroom** — Files copy and watches the outlet shrink. Asset: interviews strangers and finds the story, which is exactly what a campaign brief needs. Hook: the same reporting, for buyers who pay better.
32. **pr-comms-executive** — Writes press releases nobody prints. Asset: already writes to a business objective. Hook: an objective you can actually measure.
33. **academic-thesis-writer** — Paid to write dissertations and papers. Asset: research and structure, at length, on deadline. Hook: client work with your own name on it.
34. **teacher-explaining-for-a-living** — Explains things all day; evenings and holidays are free. Asset: explaining is the whole job of an ad. Hook: three evenings, then a skill that bills.
35. **linkedin-ghostwriter** — Writes other people's posts for a flat monthly fee. Asset: already holds a retainer, which is the hard part. Hook: widen the retainer from posts to campaigns.

## F · Life stage and where they sit (36–40)

*New territory. Note that 39 and 40 are argued on cost base and market access, not on "you understand the customer better", which has no evidence behind it.*

36. **final-year-student-writer** — Writes well, no experience, and campus placements do not want writers. Hook: one campaign you built beats an empty resume.
37. **returning-after-a-break** — Wrote before the break, unsure what changed while away. Asset: the writing did not change. The pricing did. Hook: come back at the new unit, not the old one.
38. **second-career-after-45** — Long career elsewhere, wants work that runs from home. Asset: has sat on the buying side and knows how a business actually decides. Hook: campaigns are sold to businesses, and you have been that business.
39. **tier2-cost-base** — Writes from Indore, Kochi or Nagpur, where local rates are the only rates on offer. Asset: nothing about a campaign requires a metro address. Hook: clients who never ask where you sit, paying what they pay.
40. **regional-language-writer** — Writes Hindi, Tamil, Marathi or Bangla copy, priced as "translation" and treated as a downgrade. Asset: reaches a market the brand cannot reach any other way. Hook: price the campaign that reaches that market, not the word count of the translation.

## Before any of these become ads

- **1 ad = 1 LP, and 40 pairs needs a palette register.** Rule 4. Start the register before ad one, not after ad twelve.
- **Every ad still needs its own Rule 2 sentence in the first fold.** The shared campaign definition above is the floor, not the whole job. Every role name and every piece of jargon must be followed by what you do all day.
- **No salary figures on any ad face**, and no rupee ladder on the landing pages either. The LP payload is the unit-of-sale table (what you sell now, how it is priced, what sets the price), not income bands.
- **The payment bridge is still a blocker.** All 17 wave 5 pages carry a TODO stub, and the earlier waves carry TODO or PAYMENT_LINK_PENDING. Nothing here ships until that is wired and test-bought in a real browser.
- **Run `python preflight.py` on the built ads and pages**, not on this file. This is an ICP list, not creative.
- **Pick the first batch on evidence, not on taste.** Once the Meta token is in, pull the live content-writing cells and see which of the twelve existing broad ICPs is already converting. The families that sharpen a winner go first.
