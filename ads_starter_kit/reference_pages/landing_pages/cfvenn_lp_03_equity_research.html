Two kinds of company, two ways of arriving at a number

On a listed company almost every input is handed to you. Results land every quarter, a price is quoted all day, the shareholding pattern is public and the peers trade in the open. On a private company none of that exists. The method is the same. The sourcing of every single input is not.

The right hand column is the harder half of the same craft, and it is the half that is bought as a mandate rather than covered from a desk.

3 things an equity research analyst can already do that a private mandate needs

  1. You build forecasts that survive being questioned.A model whose assumptions can be defended line by line is the entire product in a private valuation. Most people sitting at that table have never built one and cannot tell a defensible forecast from a hopeful one.
  2. You have been trained not to trust a single method.When a discounted cash flow and a multiple disagree, that is a finding rather than a mistake. On a private company they always disagree, and explaining why is most of the report.
  3. You read a business, not only a filing.Coverage teaches you what actually drives a sector: where pricing power sits, which costs move first, what a downturn does to the order book. Private owners rarely meet anybody who has that view of their own industry.

A private company mandate, from the first meeting to a signed number

Nothing is handed over. This is the order in which the base gets built from scratch.

  1. Restate the accounts before you use themOwner remuneration, rent paid to a family property, personal costs run through the business, one-off items. Private statements are prepared for the tax position, not for a buyer, and restating them honestly is the first piece of work.
  2. Rebuild the revenue from the groundCustomers, contracts, pricing, how much of the turnover sits with the largest two buyers. There is no segment disclosure, so the segments have to be reconstructed from what the company actually sells and to whom.
  3. Test the forecast against an operating constraintCapacity, the order book, the hiring plan, the working capital the business can fund. With no consensus to lean on, the only honest check on a private forecast is whether the company could physically deliver it.
  4. Choose the comparables and write down whyListed peers with an adjustment, and transactions in the same space. Both need a stated reason, because the other side attacks the choice of comparable long before it attacks the arithmetic.
  5. Adjust for control and for the absence of a marketA stake that controls the board and a stake nobody can sell tomorrow are not worth the same per share. This is the adjustment listed coverage never has to make and private work always does.
  6. Hand over a range with the model behind itThe client is buying the reasoning. The model is what lets them keep arguing their own case after you have left the room.

The 4 inputs that disappear the moment the company is private

  1. A traded price.There is no anchor in the room. The first credible number tends to become the reference everybody negotiates around, which is an argument for it being yours and being defensible.
  2. A consensus to agree or disagree with.No other analyst has published on this company. The judgement is entirely yours, which is why the written reasons matter more than the number they support.
  3. A clean share count.Preference shares, a convertible note, a SAFE issued two years ago, an option pool nobody has documented properly. The cap table has to be assembled before anything per share means anything at all.
  4. An exit.A listed holding is sold on a screen. A private stake leaves only when the company is sold or the next round closes, which is why the term sheet decides who actually gets what, and why reading one is part of the valuation rather than a separate job.

How a first private company mandate actually arrives

Not from a fund with a mandate to hand out. From the professionals who sit around private companies and have no analyst among them.

  1. Write one private company up in full, as a samplePick a business whose statements you can obtain, restate them, value it two ways and write it up properly. That document is the pitch, and nothing you can say replaces it.
  2. Take it to the people who see a round before it closesChartered accountants in practice, company secretaries, corporate lawyers, angel networks. They meet owners who need a number and usually have nobody to send them to.
  3. Say what you do in one sentence anybody understandsYou price companies that have no share price. Every person on that list knows immediately which of their clients needs it.
  4. Scope the first mandate small enough to finishOne company, one range, one written report, on an agreed date. An unfinished mandate shows nobody anything.

Straight answers

Why is it only ₹10?

The fee is ₹10 including GST, and it is refundable at any time. It exists so that the people in the room have chosen to be there, not to price the training.

I already value companies. What is actually new here?

The half where the inputs do not exist. Restating private accounts, building a cap table, reading a term sheet and a SAFE, and pricing control and illiquidity. The arithmetic you have. The deal side is what gets built in front of you.

Do I need a CA or a CFA?

No. The camp assumes you can read a profit and loss account and a balance sheet. Everything past that, including the valuation and the model, is built in front of you.

Do I have to leave my research desk to do this?

No. Most people add it. The camp runs on three evenings and the written plan on the last day is built around whatever time you actually have.

Does this cover startups, or only older private businesses?

Both, and the difference is the point. A business with a decade of statements is valued on its cash flows. A young company with none is priced on what the round itself implies, on the cap that sits in the SAFE and on the terms in the sheet.

What if I miss a session?

Sessions are live only, with no recordings, because your own questions get answered in the room. You keep the templates, the model and the notes, and the WhatsApp group helps you catch up.

Do you place me with a client or an employer?

No. Nobody can promise that. What you get is the work itself, the templates, and a written 6-month plan to your first client, which is the part most people never write.

Does this let me sign a valuation report?

No. A valuation the law requires, filed under company law or with a tax authority, is signed by a registered valuer, and a discounted cash flow valuation of unquoted shares for income tax comes from a SEBI-registered merchant banker. What you learn here is the analytical work behind a decision. Where a signed statutory report is needed, you work with the person entitled to sign it.
