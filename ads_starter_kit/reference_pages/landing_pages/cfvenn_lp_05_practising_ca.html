Two engagement letters for one client

Take a client whose business you know well and do not audit. Put a compliance engagement next to an advisory engagement for a business like theirs, and almost nothing about the two is alike except the kind of client. Section 144 of the Companies Act keeps advisory and audit apart for the same company, so this work goes to the clients on the other side of that line.

Nothing in the right hand column is out of reach of a practice that can already read the business. The difference is not access to the client. It is whether the work in that column is something you are able to scope and deliver.

3 reasons a practice is the shortest distance to advisory work

  1. You already hold the year of data a valuation starts from.The ledgers, the related party arrangements, the owner's own drawings, the contracts that were renegotiated in March. An outside adviser spends the first two weeks asking for exactly what is already on your server.
  2. The owner already tells you the truth.Advisory work depends on facts a business owner does not volunteer to a stranger: the customer who is slow to pay, the arrangement with the brother in law, the year that was propped up. You are past that conversation before it starts.
  3. An engagement letter is a document you write every year.Scope, deliverable, timeline, fee, what falls outside. Advisory is a different scope written into the same instrument, not a different business to set up.

One transaction advisory engagement, from the phone call to signature

A client calls in June because somebody has offered to buy half the company. This is the order the work happens in.

  1. The approach reaches you firstThe owner is not calling an adviser. They are calling the person who already knows the numbers, to ask whether the offer is serious.
  2. You scope it in writing before you touch a spreadsheetWhat you will deliver, what you will not, how long it takes, and that the fee is for the work whether or not anyone signs anything at the end.
  3. You normalise the accounts you already knowThe owner's salary at a market rate, the rent paid to a family trust at a rate nobody else would pay, the legal settlement that happened once. The buyer will do this. Doing it first is the whole advantage.
  4. You put a defensible range on the businessA discounted cash flow from the forecast you built, and a range from what comparable businesses changed hands at. Two routes to one range, with the reasons written down.
  5. You take the term sheet apart clause by clauseHeadline price, liquidation preference, what is held back and for how long, who sits on the board afterwards, what happens if the next round is priced lower. The headline number is rarely the clause that decides the outcome.
  6. You stay on until the pack is signedThe cap table before and after, the schedules the buyer asked for, and the owner understanding what they are keeping. Compliance work ends at a filing. This ends at a decision the owner made with you in the room.

Four advisory engagements a practice can scope from its existing book

Valuation

What is this business actually worth

A succession plan, a partner buying out another, an investor asking for a number. The deliverable is a valuation with a model behind it, not a rule of thumb multiple.

Buy side and sell side support

Should the owner accept this offer

You test the buyer's number against your own, read the term sheet against the valuation, and tell the owner which clauses cost more than the headline price.

Fundraise support

What does a round give away

The cap table before and after, the option pool, what a SAFE converts into at what price, and what the founder is left holding after the round after this one.

Cap table and ESOP work

Who owns what, on paper

Growing companies issue instruments they do not track. Rebuilding the cap table and modelling the dilution is a discrete, repeatable engagement.

All four are sold to businesses that already pay your practice for something else. None of them requires the client to change adviser. They require you to be able to do the work.

Where the first transaction advisory engagement comes from

Not from marketing. From conversations your practice is already having and currently lets pass.

  1. Name the succession conversations you have already hadOwners in their late fifties, children who are not in the business, a second generation that wants out. Every one of those is a valuation waiting for somebody to be asked.
  2. Say one sentence to the owners you do not auditThat you now do valuation and deal work, and the one question it answers for them. Referrals, family businesses in the same trade and clients you advise on tax are all outside the section 144 line.
  3. Quote it as a separate scope, not as part of the annual feeThe moment advisory is absorbed into the compliance retainer it is priced like compliance, and it never recovers. A separate letter is the whole discipline.
  4. Tell the lawyer and the banker on the deal what you doThey are on the same transactions and they need somebody who can build the number. They refer the work they cannot do themselves.

Does advisory work conflict with an audit I sign?

Not a matter of judgement, and not only the institute's code. Section 144 of the Companies Act prohibits an auditor from rendering investment advisory, investment banking or management services to a company it audits, or to that company's holding or subsidiary. So advisory goes to clients you do not audit, and the analytical work taught in the camp is identical either way.

Do I need a CA or a CFA?

No. The camp assumes you can read a profit and loss account and a balance sheet. Everything past that, including the valuation and the model, is built in front of you.

Is this a registered valuer qualification?

No. Registered valuer is a separate registration with its own examination and authority. This camp teaches the valuation and deal work advisers do for owners: the model, the DCF, the multiples, the term sheet and the cap table.
