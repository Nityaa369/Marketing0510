You already do the loan half of corporate finance every week. Here is the same skill, one step further.

The owners are the same people. The difference is that you stop at the loan, and corporate finance covers every way that owner can raise money.

3 reasons a DSA or loan consultant starts ahead

  1. You already have the opportunity funnel.In corporate finance, the hardest thing to build is a list of business owners who trust you with their money questions. Lenders and investors ignore a beginner who brings them no deals. You have been building that list for years.
  2. Debt is most of the early work.Working capital, term loans, secured and unsecured SME loans, invoice discounting (a loan against bills your client has raised), revenue-based finance (a loan repaid out of monthly sales), project finance and government schemes. You already speak this language.
  3. You know the people who hold the money.Bank relationship managers, NBFC sales heads and credit teams are the start of the capital funnel: the lenders and investors a deal is taken to. Corporate finance adds angels, funds and family offices to that list.

One raise for a client you already have, start to finish

Take a manufacturer who has used you for working capital. He has a large export order and needs a new line to fill it. This is how the deal runs, in the order it happens.

  1. The owner tells you what he needsThe money is for machinery, a bigger shed and more stock. The order is real, but his bank limit is already fully used.
  2. You choose loan, investment, or bothA term loan for the machines, a larger cash credit limit for the stock, and possibly a government scheme for the capital cost. If he wants to grow faster than debt allows, an angel or a small fund buys a share of the business instead.
  3. You prepare the documentsThree years of statements, projections for five years, and a project report or loan proposal. If investors are involved, a pitch deck and an information memorandum: a longer document that tells the investor everything about the business.
  4. You take it to lenders and investorsYou pick the banks, NBFCs or investors whose appetite fits this business, present the file and answer their questions.
  5. You negotiate and closeRate, security and tenure, or for investors the term sheet: how much they put in and what share they take. You see it through to disbursement.
  6. You are paid on what closesThe usual structure is a success fee on the amount raised or sanctioned: about 1.5 to 3% on bank loans, 2 to 5% on government schemes, 2 to 4% on equity. The documents carry their own fixed fee, paid even if the deal does not close.

4 things a loan consultant has not yet been taught

  1. How equity money is raised.Angel, VC and PE rounds, and how a raise moves from the first pitch to closing.
  2. How to build the pitch deck and projections investors ask for.Market sizing (TAM, SAM, SOM: how big the market is, how much of it the business can reach, and how much it can win), the story, the numbers and the design, built live.
  3. What a SAFE is, clause by clause.A SAFE is money now for shares later, at a price set by a future round. You draft one during the camp and see what each clause gives away.
  4. When a deal is bigger than you, and whom to pass it to.An IPO is run by a SEBI-registered merchant banker. You learn how to refer those deals for a referral commission, and how valuation fits in as one skill inside a raise.

How the first fundraising client arrives for a loan consultant

Not from an advertisement. From the owners whose loan files you already carry.

  1. Go back through your last twenty filesMark every owner who talked about expansion, a new unit or a partner. That is your opportunity funnel.
  2. Tell them what you now do besides loansOne short message: you can now help them raise money as investment too, and prepare the projections and deck that investors ask for.
  3. Take the first deal where debt is enoughMost first deals are still loans, only larger and better prepared: a project report, a scheme application, a mix of term loan and working capital. Charge a fixed fee for the documents as well as the success fee.
  4. Let the capital side come to youOnce you bring well-prepared deals, bank managers, NBFCs and investors start taking your calls first. A track record turns one-off fees into retainers.

Straight answers

Why is it only ₹10?

The fee is ₹10 including GST, and it is refundable at any time. It exists so that the people in the room have chosen to be there, not to price the training.

Do I need to stop my DSA or loan work?

No. Most people add this to what they already do. Debt is most of the early work in corporate finance, so your loan files are part of the new work.

Do I need a CA or a CFA?

No. The camp assumes you can read a profit and loss account and a balance sheet. Everything past that, including the projections and the deal documents, is built in front of you.

Do I need to become a merchant banker?

No. A merchant banker registration is needed to run an IPO. Arranging loans, preparing documents and bringing investors to a business does not need it. When a deal grows to IPO size, you refer it to a merchant banker for a referral commission.

What if I miss a session?

Sessions are live only, with no recordings, because your own questions get answered in the room. You keep the templates, the model and the notes, and the WhatsApp group helps you catch up.

Do you place me with a client or an employer?

No. Nobody can promise that. What you get is the work itself, the templates, and a written 6-month plan to your first client, which is the part most people never write.
