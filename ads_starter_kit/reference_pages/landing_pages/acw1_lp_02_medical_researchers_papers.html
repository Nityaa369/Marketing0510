The work

7 kinds of paper work medical researchers get paid for abroad

Each one starts from a draft, a finished paper or a journal's rules.

  1. Manuscript draftingTurn a team's data and notes into a full paper draft, work that can come with co-authorship.
  2. Pre-submission reviewRead a finished paper the way a journal reviewer will, and flag weak methods, missing citations and unclear claims before the journal does.
  3. Journal formattingMatch a paper to one journal's style guide: headings, references, word limits, tables and figures.
  4. Submission handlingWrite the cover letter, fill in the journal's forms and keep track of the paper through each round of review.
  5. English editingMake papers by researchers in Japan, Korea, China and Europe read cleanly in English, without changing the science.
  6. Background sectionsWrite the part of a paper that places a new study among the studies that came before it.
  7. Research communicationTurn a published paper into an article, a LinkedIn post or a podcast script that a wider audience can follow.
The buyers

5 kinds of clients who need medical papers written or fixed

  1. Professors in Japan, Korea, China and EuropeThey publish in English-language journals and want the language right before they submit.
  2. Professors in the US, UK and CanadaTeaching and grant deadlines fill their week, so they hand drafting and formatting to someone they trust.
  3. Pharma and medical-device companiesThey publish study results and need writers who can read a results table without help.
  4. Research and editing agenciesFirms that serve researchers worldwide and send papers out to freelance editors.
  5. PublishersJournals and book publishers need editors who understand medical content.
Why you

Why journal teams trust medical researchers with their papers

  • You know how a paper is built. Introduction, methods, results and discussion: you read them every week and know where each one tends to go wrong.
  • You can check a reference properly. You know whether a cited study really says what the sentence claims.
  • You speak the field's language. A general editor may smooth a sentence and change its meaning. You will not.
  • You already judge studies. Every week you decide whether a study is sound, which is the same judgement a peer reviewer makes.

One more reason. On Day 2 you see five AI review agents check a paper: one for the literature, one for citations, one for methods, one that argues against the paper and one that acts as a strict peer reviewer. The script calls each of them a service you can sell. Your medical training is what makes their output worth trusting, because you can tell when an agent has got it wrong.
