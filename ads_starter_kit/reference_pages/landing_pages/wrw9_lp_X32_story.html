Chapter One · Rajkot

Twelve years at home, then a first project in two months

Sneha Tanna got married in her third year of college. What followed was a twelve year gap with no paid work. That is a long time. Long enough for most people to assume the working chapter of life is closed.

Then she started learning how remote work for international clients actually operates. Within two months of starting, she landed her first project.

Within two months of starting, she had her first project. She has now completed more than 25.

Today Sneha has finished 25+ projects in transcription, translation and subtitling. All of this is work done from home, on a computer, on her own time. She earns ₹20,000 to ₹25,000 a month, working remotely, on a schedule that flexes around her home.

That is the shape of this kind of work. The client can be in another country, the files reach you online, and the working hours bend around your household instead of the other way round.

Her numbers are her own. Earnings differ from person to person, depending on the hours you put in and how consistently you work.

Bhavana Sharma graduated in 2008. When she began remote work from Indore, her first rate was 3 US dollars an hour, working for a Canadian company. It was a small start. Within 3 months, the rate rose to 5 dollars an hour.

Chapter Three · Thrissur

A 10 dollar thumbnail, then 32 projects across three countries

Lakshmi Vijay graduated in 2007 and then took a seven year break for marriage and motherhood. Her restart was about as small as a restart can be: a 10 dollar project making YouTube thumbnails for a US client.

She kept going. Since that 10 dollar job, she has completed 32 projects for clients in Australia, Canada and the US, and has earned over 6,000 US dollars on Upwork.

Her first project paid 10 dollars. The projects since have paid over 6,000.

As with every story here, her earnings are hers. Yours will depend on the time you give this and how steadily you work.
