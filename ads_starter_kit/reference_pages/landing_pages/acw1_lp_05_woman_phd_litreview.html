The work

6 kinds of literature review work a PhD holder can sell

One core skill, packaged six ways.

  1. Narrative literature reviewsSummarise the research on a topic and show how the studies connect to each other.
  2. Systematic reviewsA review done to a set method, with a clear record of what was searched and why each study was kept or left out.
  3. Research gap reportsA short report that ends with the questions still open, for a professor planning a new study.
  4. Review sections for papers and proposalsThe background section of a manuscript or a grant proposal, written by someone who knows the literature.
  5. Evidence summariesWhat the research says on one question, written for think tanks and R&D teams who have to decide something.
  6. Research-to-content packsTurn a set of papers into a study guide, flashcards, a quiz or a podcast with NotebookLM.
The buyers

5 kinds of clients who pay for literature reviews

  1. Professors starting a new studyBefore any new project they need to know what has already been done, and that reading takes weeks.
  2. Professors writing grant proposalsEvery proposal needs a review that shows the gap the money would fill.
  3. Corporate R&D teams and labsThey want the research on a question summarised before they invest in it.
  4. Think tanks and policy institutesTheir reports rest on a clear account of what the evidence says.
  5. Edtech companies and course buildersThey turn research into learning material and need someone to read it first.
Why you

Why professors hand their literature reviews to PhD holders

  • It is slow, careful work. A good review takes weeks of searching and reading, and professors abroad rarely have those weeks.
  • Their assistants are still learning. Research assistants often have not yet written a full review of their own.
  • You have written one to a doctoral standard. Your thesis review was read by examiners, which is a stricter test than most client work.
  • You know when a source is weak. AI tools find papers fast. Deciding which ones count still takes a trained reader.

A literature review is also the easiest service to begin with. It has a clear scope, a clear finish and a sample you can show. One good review becomes a portfolio piece, and it can lead the client to ask you for the next part of the project. Many professors who publish often need a new review for each study, so a client who trusts your first one has a reason to come back.

Where it can lead

3 paths you can take after this bootcamp

  1. Reviews as a side serviceOffer literature reviews to professors abroad a few at a time, beside teaching or family. Each review adds a sample to your portfolio and a name to your client list.
  2. Systematic reviews as a specialismSystematic reviews follow a strict method and suit someone who likes structure. Focusing on them gives you a clear, named service.
  3. A remote role with a writing agencyAgencies that serve global clients keep reviewers on their team, and many work from home.

Whichever you pick, three things decide how fast it works: the skills to do the work, a small portfolio of samples a client can check, and a steady habit of reaching out. The three sessions cover all three.
