Nobody on a deal desk is hostile to the left hand column. They simply have no way to test it, and they have forty of them on the screen. The right hand column is the only part of an application that answers a question they were actually asking.

3 reasons investment banking hiring turns on what you can show

  1. The first read is done by somebody with no way to test a claim.A sentence about interest and aptitude is unfalsifiable, so it is skipped. A file they can open is evidence, and evidence is what changes the next thirty seconds.
  2. Deal experience is the requirement that no first job hands out.The postings ask for it and the entry roles are how you would get it. That loop only breaks one way: by doing the analytical work on a company nobody assigned to you.
  3. The interview is a task, not a conversation.Walk me through a DCF. Here is a business, what is it worth. Why is your multiple that number. Somebody who has built one answers from memory of doing it, which sounds completely different from memory of reading about it.

Build the thing before anyone gives you a deal

You do not need a mandate to do the work. You need a company, its published numbers, and the discipline to finish. This is the order.

  1. Pick a business you can genuinely readA listed mid-size company whose filings are public, or an industry you already know from family or a job. Pick something you can explain in two sentences without notes.
  2. Rebuild three years of statements yourselfTyped in by hand, not downloaded. Doing it by hand is how you find the line that moves and the line that is noise.
  3. Put the assumptions on a sheet of their ownGrowth, margin, working capital days, capex, the discount rate. Anyone should be able to change one cell and watch the value move. A model with assumptions buried in formulas is the first thing an interviewer stops trusting.
  4. Value it twice, then reconcile the twoA discounted cash flow, and a range from what comparable companies trade at. They will not agree. The answer for why they disagree is the most interesting thing you will say in an interview.
  5. Mark up a term sheet on the same businessPrice, preference, anti dilution, board composition, what a SAFE converts into. Read it as though the founder were about to sign it and you had one minute to warn them.
  6. Write one page a stranger can read in three minutesThe business, the number, the range, the two or three things the value turns on. That page is the thing you send. The model is the thing you send when they ask.

4 questions on a deal desk that a syllabus never asks you

  1. Which clause in the term sheet decides the outcome.It is usually not the valuation. A liquidation preference can hand the whole downside to one side while the headline price stays flattering.
  2. Why your two valuations disagree, and which one you defend.A cash flow says what the business earns. A multiple says what somebody recently paid. When they diverge you have to say which evidence you trust and why.
  3. What the option pool does to everyone in the room.A pool created before the round dilutes everyone already on the cap table and not the incoming investor. Where the pool sits is a negotiated term, and knowing that is the difference between reading a cap table and understanding one.
  4. What a SAFE actually becomes.Money now, shares later, at a price set by a future round. The cap and the discount are the two numbers that decide what was given away, and both are easy to read past.

How a first deal-side role actually turns up

Not from a portal. From putting finished work in front of people who can tell whether it is any good.

  1. Send the work, not the CVOne page, the company, the number, and an offer to send the model. The email is short and the attachment does the arguing.
  2. Ask for a correction, not a jobSend it to somebody senior and ask where the model is wrong. People who ignore requests for jobs answer requests for review, and an answer is the beginning of a relationship.
  3. Go where the deals are small enough to need youBoutique advisory firms, transaction teams at mid-size practices, the finance team of a company that is raising. These are the rooms where an extra pair of hands on a model is felt immediately.
  4. Have the second company readyThe response to a good first page is often a name and the words do this one. Being able to turn that around in a week is what separates interest from an offer of work.

Straight answers

Why is it only ₹10?

The fee is ₹10 including GST, and it is refundable at any time. It exists so that the people in the room have chosen to be there, not to price the training.

I am not from a target college. Does that change anything here?

The camp does not ask where you studied, and neither does a model. What we teach is the work: a company valued live, a term sheet taken apart, a plan you write yourself. What that is worth to a hiring desk is their judgement, not a promise we make.

Do I need a CA or a CFA?

No. The camp assumes you can read a profit and loss account and a balance sheet. Everything past that, including the valuation and the model, is built in front of you.

No. A certificate of participation says you attended. The valuation you built, the model behind it and the marked up term sheet are the things worth showing anyone, and they are yours to keep.

What if I miss a session?

Sessions are live only, with no recordings, because your own questions get answered in the room. You keep the templates, the model and the notes, and the WhatsApp group helps you catch up.

Do you place me with a client or an employer?

No. Nobody can promise that. What you get is the work itself, the templates, and a written 6-month plan to your first client, which is the part most people never write.

Does this let me sign a valuation report?

No. A valuation the law requires, filed under company law or with a tax authority, is signed by a registered valuer, and a discounted cash flow valuation of unquoted shares for income tax comes from a SEBI-registered merchant banker. What you learn here is the analytical work behind a decision. Where a signed statutory report is needed, you work with the person entitled to sign it.
