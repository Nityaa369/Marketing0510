WHY THIS WORKS

Why a US small business owner pays you well

You already know this ladder: the same assistant work that pays twenty-five thousand in Nagpur pays double in Pune, and more again in Mumbai. Remote work simply lets you keep climbing the same ladder past Mumbai, to New York and London, without moving cities. Here is why the owner on the other end is happy to pay:

1. They need the help.

A small US business runs on everyday written work: messages answered, invoices raised, listings updated, calendars kept. The owner does it late at night, or it does not get done. Right now more than one in three American small businesses have job openings they cannot fill.

2. Local help is expensive for them.

A virtual assistant hired in America costs a business owner close to seventy thousand US dollars a year, plus office costs, for work that does not need anyone on site.

3. The same work from India works for both sides.

A fee that is ordinary for a US business is meaningful money at home. The owner saves, you earn well, and the work is identical. That trade has a name, skill arbitrage, and it is the whole model we teach.

This is not theory. One example we teach from: a London hotel owner happily pays a part-time assistant in India 800 pounds a month just to manage the hotel's reviews, because a local hire would cost several times that.

THE MARKET IS ALREADY THERE

We did not invent this. It already runs every day.

US small business owners already hand this work to remote assistants, every day. Entire agencies and hiring platforms exist only to supply them. When you enter, you are not testing a new idea on a stranger. You are joining a market that is already running, where the owner is already used to paying for remote help. The three days show you where you fit in it, and how to be the one they pick.

The size of it: the United States alone has over 36 million small businesses. In a Gusto survey of American small businesses that already hire internationally, 58 percent said they do it because they cannot find enough workers at home, 75 percent plan to hire even more people abroad, and India is one of the top three countries they hire from.

And it is growing: Deel's 2025 State of Global Hiring report found full-time hiring of Indian professionals grew 63.7 percent in a year from UK companies and 24.1 percent from US companies, with the workers staying right here in India. The demand side of this market is not the problem.

YOUR STARTING POINT

You already hold the qualification

The whole entry requirement is basic English, a computer or even a phone, and an internet connection. It does not matter whether you studied humanities, science or commerce, or whether you have prior work experience. Beyond that, this is what a business owner actually checks before handing work to remote help:

  • Careful written English. Clear, polite, correct. Not fancy.
  • Reliability. Work returned when you said it would be.
  • Attention to small things. The date, the name, the amount, right.
  • Years of managing work, people and a home. That counts here.
If you have been managing weekly groceries, family schedules and a household budget, you have been running calendars, meetings and projects without the titles. And if the gap on your resume worries you: a work profile does not show gap years the way a resume does. Clients read what you can do now. The three days convert what you already have into a profile, a sample and a plan a client can say yes to.

The old route back is stacked against you: an Ashoka University study found women with a career break get 49 percent fewer interview callbacks on identical resumes. Remote work removes that filter. In a randomized study in India, women offered the same job as work-from-home were more than three times as likely to start working.
