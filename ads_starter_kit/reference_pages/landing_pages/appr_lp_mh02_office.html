Why arbitration skills stay out of reach

The notice of arbitration comes to you by email on a Friday evening. There is a thirty day clock inside it, a nomination to make, and a board that will ask on Monday what our position is. You forward it to counsel and wait, because the process itself was never explained to you.

It is not that the skills are hard. It is that nobody sits you down and teaches them.

Nobody teaches you to draft a Procedural Order No. 1

Or what an ICC Terms of Reference has to say, or how a Redfern Schedule works. These are the documents that decide arbitrations.

Damages computation gets learned from a mistake on a live matter

Quantum experts charge lakhs. Knowing how to test their method, or present your own, is a skill you can hold before you need it.

International panels expect things Indian training never covers

Common law and civil law arbitrators want different things from counsel, and it shows in the room.

Empanelment looks like it runs on connections

DIAC, MCIA, the Delhi High Court Arbitration Centre. The criteria, the process and the documentation are all knowable, and we go through them.

Construction and FIDIC arbitration looks like a closed shop

NHAI, DMRC and PPP disputes are where the large fees sit, and they open once you know FIDIC contracts and how a DAB works.

Nobody maps how to get briefed independently

Indian and international arbitration practices brief researchers and drafters. Finding that work, pricing it and building from it is skill 14.
