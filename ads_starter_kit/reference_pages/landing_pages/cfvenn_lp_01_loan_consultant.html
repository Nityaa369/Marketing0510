What a loan consultant already does that a deal needs

A debt file and a valuation ask the same first question: can this business support what it is about to take on. You answer it every week for a lender. The equity side asks it about ownership instead of repayment.

The client is the same person. The question is whether you are the one they call when the question stops being about a loan.

3 reasons a debt consultant is close to the equity side

  1. You are already in the room when the money question comes up.Owners raise debt and equity in the same conversation: an expansion, a new plant, a working capital gap that will not close. The second half of that conversation often goes to somebody the owner has known for far less time than you.
  2. The documents overlap more than the names suggest.A projection, a sensitivity and a promoter contribution appear in a bank file and in an investor pack. What changes is who reads them and what they are deciding.
  3. Debt work ends at sanction. Advisory work does not.A file closes and the relationship goes quiet until the next facility. A valuation, a cap table and an investor pack are work that keeps a client in front of you.

One equity-side engagement, start to finish

This is what the work looks like when an owner asks you the second question, in the order it happens.

  1. The owner says they have been approachedA buyer, a fund or a larger competitor has asked what the business would take. The owner has no number and no way to test one.
  2. You build the base modelThree years of history, a forecast, the assumptions written down where the owner can challenge them. This is the same discipline as a bank projection, held to a harder test.
  3. You value the company two waysA discounted cash flow, and a multiple of comparable transactions. Two methods, one range, and the reasons the range is what it is.
  4. You read the term sheetValuation, dilution, liquidation preference, board seats, what happens if the next round is lower. The number in the headline is rarely the number that matters.
  5. You lay out the cap tableWho owns what now, who owns what after, and what the owner keeps in each version of the deal.
  6. You are paid for the engagementThe fee is for the work and the judgement, not for an amount sanctioned.

4 things nobody teaches a debt consultant

  1. Enterprise value against equity value.Two numbers that differ by net debt: what a company owes, less the cash it holds. A business with no borrowings and no cash shows the same number twice, and almost none do.
  2. How dilution actually works.What the founder holds after the round, after the option pool, and after the pool is topped up at the next one.
  3. What a SAFE is and when it bites.Money now, shares later, at a price set by a future round. The valuation cap and the discount decide what the owner gave away.
  4. Which multiple is defensible.A multiple you cannot defend is a number the other side rewrites for you.

How the first equity-side client actually arrives

Not from an advertisement. From the clients whose files you already carry.

  1. Go back through the last twenty filesMark every owner who talked about expansion, a partner exit, or a buyer sniffing around. That is your list, and it is warm.
  2. Send the message the camp writes with youOne paragraph: what you now do besides debt, and the one question it answers for them.
  3. Do the first one at a price that makes you finish itThe first engagement buys the sample you show the next five people, so it has to be real work delivered on a date.
  4. Ask for the introduction while the work is freshAn owner who has just seen their own business valued knows two other owners who have asked the same question this year.
