Every piece of feedback, verdict and instruction Ramanuj (founder, and the only approver whose
verdict decides what ships) has given on this account's ads, landing pages, ICPs and media
buying, in one place. Merged 28 Sep 2026 from:

- `docs/WhatsApp Chat with Ad avengers.txt`: the team approval channel, 6 to 13 Aug 2026. This
  is the raw primary source and stays where it is.
- `ad_research/RAMANUJ_BASELINE.md`: the verified analysis of that chat. Folded in here as
  Part 3 and removed.
- Kit READMEs, `CLAUDE.md`, `CAMPAIGN_MAP.md`, `SOP/`, `GOLDEN/`, `judging-static-ads/reference/`
  and the Claude memory notes, where later feedback was recorded as it was relayed.

**How to read an entry.** `date · VERDICT · subject`, then his words, then the lesson the team
took from it, then where it is recorded. Text in quotes is his, copied exactly, typos included.
`[paraphrase]` means the record describes what he said without quoting him. `(relayed)` means it
reached the team through Ruchika rather than from him directly.

**What is not here.** Feedback from Ruchika, Uday Wagh or anyone else. It lives in the kit
READMEs and memory notes. Only Ramanuj's approval counts as approval: the in-house Section A
"APPROVED" labels are not his and those pairs never shipped.

**Keep it current.** When he gives new feedback, add it to the right section of Part 2 with
the date and his exact words, and add the verdict to the ledger in Part 4. Part 6 records
what the deleted drafts taught about work that does not get approved. Do not start a new file.

## Part 1. What he wants, in fifteen lines

Each line points at the entries in Part 2 that carry the evidence.

1. **Offer a better career. Never threaten.** Not a regulator, not more work at the same desk,
   not "get named the person who fixed it". (2.4)
2. **No cryptic copy.** A stranger to the field must be able to name the job. Name the reader
   first, then the offer in plain words, then define the field. No riddles, no wordplay, no
   fragments. (2.1, 2.2)
3. **The reader is half-asleep.** The callout is huge, the heading is literal, nothing that
   matters is small or grey. (2.2, 2.5)
4. **Name the prize.** The biggest line names the move into the field and what they become,
   never the product, the pain or a task. (2.1, 2.4)
5. **Real, recognisable ICPs.** A self-recognised identity or situation, in words the reader
   uses. Generic job titles, loaded duties. (2.3)
6. **Read the camp script first.** Every claim about the work must match what the bootcamp
   teaches. (2.6)
7. **The landing page states the value in the first fold, in the ad's words.** One ad, one
   page, shipped together, and pages in a kit must say different things. (2.6)
8. **Read what you send.** No hedge above the pay button, no dead button, no page nobody
   opened. (2.6, 2.9)
9. **One palette per ad.** Light backgrounds, varied looks across a kit, Indian faces only. (2.5)
10. **No income figures on the face, no "not for you if", no viewer-describing numbers.** Income
    as a direction is fine; a figure is not. (2.7)
11. **His own copy and designs ship as given.** Four of the six cheapest faces in the account
    are his. (2.1, 2.5)
12. **Max 5 ads per ad set; never let two of our campaigns bid against each other.** (2.8)
13. **Judge on cost per result over a full window, never on a part-day or on CTR.** (2.8)
14. **Always hand over the landing pages with the ads.** (2.6, 2.9)
15. **Only make good ads.** "we make only good ads" (7 Aug). A gate passing does not mean an ad
    is good. (2.9)

### 2.2 Clarity: what "cryptic" means

**Said at least six times, 7 Aug 2026 onwards · REJECTED**
"Very complicated" / "Very cryptic" (7 Aug, the first Data Privacy ads) · "Why cryptic" / "Rest
cryptic" (7 Aug, Corporate Finance, two of the batch "ok") · "the beginning is cryptic - can
this be better?" (7 Aug, Senior consulting)
Lesson: cryptic means the ad only makes sense to someone who already knows the field. The fix is
the first-fold order: who you are as a question, a qualifier row, the offer in plain words, one
sentence defining the field, three or four named deliverables, then price, dates, live-only and
a risk reversal. The missing piece is usually the definition. (CLAUDE.md Rule 2.)
Source: WhatsApp chat 7 Aug 6:20pm, 11:22-11:23pm, 12:12am; CLAUDE.md Rule 2

why" then "the beginning is cryptic - can this be better?"
Lesson: two separate failures. (a) The hook arrives before the reader knows who is addressed.
(b) The qualifier row is too narrow. Full edit sequence in Part 3.2.
Source: WhatsApp chat 7 Aug 12:10-12:12am; reference_images/IMG-20260807-WA0000..0002.jpg

## Part 3. How his verdicts work: the verified analysis

Folded in from `ad_research/RAMANUJ_BASELINE.md`, which was built by opening every image and
page the 6 to 13 Aug chat verdicts reacted to.

### 3.1 The unit of work is an ICP batch, not an ad

He never asks for "an ad for data privacy". He asks for "10 new ICPs" (7 Aug) or hands over a list
of 40 named roles in one go. The creative work is finding new, specific people whose current job
already contains the raw material for the move, then filling a fixed scaffold per ICP: the H1, the
connecting paragraph and the boxed offer line change; price, dates and mechanics do not. Every
rejection in Part 3.3 was a rejection of those three elements, never of the mechanics.

### 3.2 The "cryptic" edit sequence, 7 Aug 2026

Three drafts of one Senior consulting ad, each answering his previous verdict:

1. **Draft 1** (`reference_images/IMG-20260807-WA0000.jpg`) opened with "IN MOST US STATES,
   FLIPPING BURGERS PAYS OVER $10 AN HOUR", with the audience only in a small tag above it.
   Verdict: "the flipping burger thing is not clear as a hook - who are you talking to and why".
   The metaphor was doing the work the audience line should do first.
2. **Draft 2** (`WA0001`) opened with the identity question "20+ years of experience?", then the
   qualifier row `SENIOR PROFESSIONALS · 45+ · ANY DOMAIN`, then the plain offer. Verdict: still
   "the beginning is cryptic - can this be better?"
3. **Draft 3** (`WA0002`) changed one thing: the field list widened from "finance, operations,
   marketing and law" to "finance, HR, operations, sales, marketing, tech, law: any field you have
   run for years." That shipped.

So "cryptic" has two causes with two different fixes: the hook arriving before the reader is
named (fix the order), and a qualifier row that leaves eligible readers out (widen it).

### 3.3 The Data Privacy batch of nine, 12 Aug 2026

The closest thing to a controlled experiment in the account: one offer (DPDP GrowthCamp, ₹10,
29 to 31 Aug), one template, one price, nine ICPs, nine verdicts in six minutes. Images arrived
in a different order from the pages, so each image was opened to match verdict to page; the
mapping below is the one where CLAUDE.md's independently recorded approvals and rejections line up.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Collections and recovery | "every call you make to a borrower is now data processing" | REJECTED: "This is not ok - you need to offer a different career to this person not threaten with a regulator" |
| 2 | Coaching and admissions | "Become the DPO your institute will need in 2027" | REJECTED: "This is also not ok" |
| 3 | Recruitment / BGV | "Tired of targets? Move into data privacy instead" | APPROVED: "This is ok" |

What separates them:

- **#1, #8 and #9** put a new obligation on the reader's current job, and their offer line was
  "the plan that gets you named the person who fixed/built it": the same job, done compliantly,
  for informal credit.
- **#2** has an approved-style "Become the DPO…" headline, but its paragraph says "targeted
  advertising to children is banned outright". A regulator threat in the body overrides a good
  headline.
- **Every clean approval (#3 to #6)** names a new destination ("Become the DPO…", "Move into data

  current job title.
- **#7 is a genuine exception.** Its headline and offer line match the rejected pattern, yet it

  absolute rule, and do not reverse-engineer a hidden rule from one survival.

When the team then blamed the ICP ("the icp can be confusing i.e. the call out"), he rejected the
diagnosis: "Nothing wrong with ICP / ICP is perfect / What you are offering to them / Not
something they want / Not something in their interest".

**Checklist before calling a rejected ad "cryptic":**
1. Does the hook arrive before the reader knows who is addressed? Fix the order (3.2).
2. Does the qualifier row cover everyone who should self-select in? Widen it (3.2).
3. Is the one-sentence field definition present? Add it (CLAUDE.md Rule 2).
4. If all three are fine, the problem is probably not clarity. It is Rule 1: the reader
   understood the offer and did not want it (3.3).

## Part 6. What did not get approved: lessons from the deleted drafts

On 28 Sep 2026, 15 draft folders and the `new ad types/` format tests were deleted (Recycle
Bin): Content Writing W3, RMBC, Neighbours Reworked, Collage and Section A (only its
`ad_copy*.md` kept, for `copy_census.py --selftest`), both content-writing-bootcamp kits, the 17
single-format tests, `_ALL_ADS_22Aug`, wave 6, formats v1 to v4, both senior-consulting reworks,
womens_ai_bootcamp_5-7Sep (its video script moved to `docs/`), WomensAI_Bootcamp_Wave1,
SeniorConsulting_MediaBuyer_22-24Aug, DataPrivacy_MediaBuyer_22-24Aug, and the four Women Remote
5-7 Sep folders (Situations, Verticals, HiddenMarkets, USRoles). As far as the repo records, none
ran and none was approved by Ramanuj. This part keeps what they teach.

**Read the tags.** **[R]** means Ramanuj rejected it or said the words quoted. **[never
approved]** means he gave no verdict: the work was halted, superseded or rejected in-house, and
never reached or passed him. Both are useful; only [R] is his judgement.

### 6.1 The patterns, most common first

1. **The biggest line named the product, a statistic or the pain, never the prize.** "3,700
   BRANDS NEED STORIES FOUND", "Join the 3 evening camp", "One bootcamp. Two credentials.", a
   pain headline with the prize at 23px. **[R]** 7 Jul "the value prop is not clear"; the rest
   [never approved]. *(RMBC, Neighbours, Section A, bootcamp variants, wave 6)*
2. **The reader was not a person.** A wish, an age or a broad function: "women looking to learn
   AI skills", "Your career paused. Your potential didn't.", "IT", "HR admin", "45+". **[R]**
   Data Privacy 22-24 Aug: "Very complicated" / "Very cryptic", then "still not good enough".
   *(DataPrivacy_22-24Aug, WomensAI_Bootcamp_Wave1, womens_ai_bootcamp)*
3. **A job nobody recognises reads as fake.** "admissions researcher", "global-mobility
   company", US titles like "intake coordinator". **[R]** "those jobs don't really seem real, I
   doubt anyone is hiring." and "generalise the roles". *(HiddenMarkets, USRoles, Verticals)*
4. **Assistance offered to a credentialed reader reads as a step down.** "Become a VA to a US
   dietitian": the harder the ad leans on her qualification, the worse the demotion. Blind
   readers returned step DOWN 6 of 6 and the wave was halted. [never approved] *(Verticals,
   USRoles)*
5. **Wordplay, aphorisms and jokes that need decoding.** "The code got cheap. Your judgement did
   not.", "Fully booked is not a business.", Hinglish punchlines, a symbol that is not the thing
   (an UNO skip card for sending samples), a problem and a fix on different axes (raise vs
   skill), two states differing by one word. **[R]** the 14 Aug no-wordplay rule and 24 Aug "too
   clever with the image and the hook". *(SeniorConsulting, womens_ai, single-format tests, wave 6)*
6. **Too much text on the face.** The first two_moods build ran 96 words; five of the six
   wordiest Section A faces were the rejects. **[R]** 21 Aug "there is wayyy too much text..."
   (probably this wave; the record does not name the file). *(format tests, Section A)*
7. **Deficiency and loss framing.** "you have never…", gig boards drying up, a lost retainer,
   "Nobody escapes the rat race". Four of the seven Section A in-house rejects opened this way.
   [never approved] *(Section A, W5 usmb cells, senior_consulting_v2)*
8. **Income on the face.** Salary-ladder cards, rupee ranges, "earn 5x more", "that one gets paid
   most". Salary ladders are landing-page only, and pay-chart faces cost about 2.3x par. [never
   approved] *(both bootcamp kits, Collage, eyetest, senior_consulting_v2)*
9. **Banned or wrong audiences.** Freelancers under another name ("SELF EMPLOYED WRITERS",
   "Freelance writers juggling five gigs"), freshers the relaunch had excluded, and
   appraisal-no-raise, which failed the relatability bar on three formats. **[R]** "this audience
   does not convert - always remember to exclude them." *(RMBC, bootcamp kits, calculator,
   listof3, uno_skip)*
10. **One template, one spine, one sentence.** Formats v1 and v2 swapped only the widget; the
    s-series pages are one page with a new H1; the bootcamp pages changed only step 1; one
    definition or delivery sentence sat on every face. **[R]** "I don't like the designs, look
    through the design styles of past best performing ads" and the Rule 4b "the lp is the same
    over and over again". *(formats v1/v2, RMBC, WomensAI_Wave1, bootcamp kits, womens_ai,
    Verticals)*
11. **The landing page did not say what is being built, or said too little.** **[R]** 7 Aug "the
    LPs do not talk about building a solo consulting business in the first fold", and on 700 to
    808-word pages, "super short, deliver no value." *(senior_consulting_reworked)*
12. **Everything built before anything was approved.** 24 full pages of about 2,900 words, a
    23-face wave and two 10+10 kits, all before one face was signed off: "about 105,000 tokens of
    landing page written for nothing". This is why the order is ICP list, value props, hooks, one
    pair, then the rest. [never approved] *(Section A, wave 6, bootcamp kits)*
13. **Ads with no working page.** Ads-only drops, 8 faces to 2 pages, `PAYMENT_LINK_PENDING`,
    `href="#"`, "Funnel not found", funnels that 404. **[R]** "But where are the landing pages" and
    "At least read what crap you are sending to me". *(almost every deleted folder)*
14. **Unsourced or untrue claims.** "6,000 BRANDS PAY THE WRITER DIRECT" (the 6,000 counted
    platform sellers), unattributed 3,700 and 4,300, "our students are getting this work", one
    statistic on six faces. [never approved] *(wave 6, Neighbours, Section A)*
15. **Rights and trade-dress risk.** A Google logo, a competitor's AI interface, a peaking film
    dialogue, marketplace brand names, art resembling a trademarked character, drawn silhouettes
    and CSS-drawn mockups. [never approved] *(didyoumean, senior_consulting_v2, bootcamp
    variants, wave 6, closerlook, formats v1)*
16. **In-house approval treated as approval.** Section A's 17 "APPROVED", the `_APPROVED`
    filenames in `_ALL_ADS_22Aug` and wave 6, "approved 20 Aug" with no approver. Fitting the
    gates to those labels made them block 72% and 84% of the faces Ramanuj actually approved.
    Only his verdict counts.
17. **Green gates did not mean shippable.** Overlapping text passed every gate; a wave logged as a
    gate REJECT was still zipped; Verticals passed hook_rules 6 of 6 and was still the wrong
    audience. Open the PNG, and put the ICP list in front of him first.
