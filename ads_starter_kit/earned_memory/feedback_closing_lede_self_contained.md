DPDP wave 3, 1 Oct 2026. Every closing fold was written as a heading plus a lede that
opened "These three evenings are about..." and then pointed back across the heading with
a pronoun or a demonstrative: "it", "them", "one", "those records", "that data", "yours".
Nine of the twenty did it. Four were caught by the blind closing read; five passed by luck.

**Why:** the reader of a closing fold is given the heading and the body together, so the
referent is technically present. They still refuse to restate it. A pronoun that crosses a
heading boundary reads as withheld, and the fold sits in the last slot before the pay
button.

**How to apply:** every closing lede names its own object inside its own sentence, and
addresses the reader. "Over these three evenings you learn to <verb> <named thing>, for
<named client>." The same rule killed three more folds whose HEADING carried the withheld
referent instead - "the rule this work is built on", "the files they are given", "the pack
somebody else signs", "the cross-border question". Name the work in concrete nouns.

Also from the same run: an acronym in the heading ("LPO associates") and an abstraction
("support work that goes out under another name") both read as riddles. Spell the role out.
