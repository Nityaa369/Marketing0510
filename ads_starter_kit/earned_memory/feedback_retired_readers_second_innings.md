Said 28 Sep 2026 on the first ID calendar face: *"these are very experienced folks who have
retired - so it should feel like a second inning, not a step down"*, and *"live online, live
only?"* about a terms strip that said "live" twice.

What read as a step down on that face: calendar rows of idle leisure ("Morning walk, no alarm",
"Lunch with old colleagues"), a caption about the ending ("Your week, after the farewell"), the
job-seeker phrase "a board-ready profile", and an FAQ that asked "Is that too late?".

The fix used, in the script's own frame (ID Day 2: "start a second innings without compromising
on your retirement"; Day 1: meeting directors, CEOs and investors "from a position of respect"):
caption "Your second innings, this week"; rows that show stature and the new role ("Golf with old
colleagues", "A call with the company's CFO", then the board meeting); offer line "Start your
second innings on company boards."; the plan named as "the 6-month plan to become an independent
director"; FAQ "Do boards appoint people after they retire?".

**Why:** the reader's identity is senior; anything that pictures them as idle or applying for
something tells them this is a demotion, and the blind reader will not catch it (the first
version still read UP).

**How to apply:** for any retired or senior reader on any line, each calendar or copy row must
show them with stature or doing the new senior work; never pad with leisure that implies nothing
to do. One word per idea on the terms strip. Related: [[feedback-calendar-layout]],
[[project-id-general-vs-specific]], [[feedback-blind-reader-value-prop]].

**28 Sep, later:** the winning ID lines were a belief flip, being wanted, or a promotion ("Retirement
can be a promotion to the boardroom", rid_quote_ Rs 47 on 4,180), so calendar rows should show board
POWER (being consulted, votes, decisions), not a pleasant week. But on a GENERAL reader's face, rows from
one function read as that function's job: "audit committee" + "vote on the accounts" drew "is this only
for auditors?". Use company-wide decisions (shortlisting the next CEO, s.178; voting on an acquisition,
s.179(3)); keep function-specific rows (audit) for that function's reader (banker).

**28 Sep, educator head:** "You have chaired meetings like this before" style lines were rejected as vague
("how does it sound like a step up or relate to them becoming an ID?"). What passed: the business-boardseat
pattern, past role then board role in two short clauses: "You led a school or college. Now guide company
boards." A retired reader's head must name both what they led and the board it leads to.

**28 Sep, "super vague and pointless" rows:** rejected "A meeting of independent directors only", "A visit to
the new factory", "The next generation runs yours", and Then/Now/Next rule rows ("3 years or more in one of
those roles", "You may skip the directors' online test"). Rows that pass show the reader deciding something,
and each reads alone: "Choosing a new director for the board", "Deciding if the company takes a big loan",
"The CFO now answers your questions", "Reviewing how the CEO did this year". Test each row: what power does
the reader hold in it? An event with no decision, or a rule, is pointless on the face.
