Ramanuj, on the DPDP wave 3 faces, 1 Oct 2026: "yes, name the Act and fix the company
reference." Two separate dangling references in the deliverables list:

- **"GDPR BESIDE THE INDIAN ACT"** — the statute is never named. It is the Digital
  Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.
- **"The privacy report a company buys"** — names no buyer, so the deliverable has no
  customer. The whole wave's argument is international clients, and "a company" erased it.

**Why:** a definite article promises a referent. "The Indian Act", "the company", "the
parent", "the review" each tell the reader there is a specific thing and then withhold it.
Every blind reader flagged this class at question 6 across four separate rounds, and no
mechanical gate catches it — the sentence is grammatical and comprehensible.

**How to apply:** on the face, where a statute's full name will not fit, say what it is in
ordinary words and let the page carry the name — `India's data law and Europe's GDPR`, not
`GDPR beside the Indian Act`. Name the buyer in the deliverable itself: `The privacy report
a foreign client buys`. On the page, name the Act in full on first mention per page and
stay short afterwards, so the copy does not become a recital. Expand an acronym the first
time the page uses it ("GDPR, Europe's privacy law").
