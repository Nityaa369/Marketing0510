On the LawSikho Data Protection wave, 21 Sep 2026, I rewrote a vague caller line into
"A data-protection practice you can lead, with no one ahead of you". Ruchika: *"with no one
ahead of you - is that true? has language like this been used in past ads?"* It was not
true, and I had made it MORE absolute than the approved precedent.

**Why:** data protection has been law in India since 2009 (IT Act s.43A in force 27 Oct
2009; SPDI Rules 11 Apr 2011) and firms have advised on it since. What is new is the DPDP Act
(assent 11 Aug 2023) and its Rules (notified 13 Nov 2025, phased). Precedent is not proof:
"The practice area with no seniors" ran cheapest (Rs 97) and "The desk with no seniors" is
Ramanuj-approved (USCL, GOLDEN, cited in SOP/07), and both still overclaim. Offered a hard
and a soft true version, she chose the soft one - "A practice under India's new
data-protection rules, while they are still phasing in" - and said *"use that sort of
language for all ads"*.

**How to apply:**
- Tie every "early / new / first" claim to a sourced, dated fact on the face or page.
- Never write "no one", "nobody", "almost no one", "quickest", "fewer lawyers", "short
  queue", "no seniors", "no veterans", "no head start". Scan the copy fields for them.
- A winner's headline that overclaims does not become true by having run cheap. Keep the
  angle, drop the absolute - and say so when it means giving up the exact tested line.
- When fixing a vague line, check the fix is still true. Clarity pushed this one past the
  evidence.
