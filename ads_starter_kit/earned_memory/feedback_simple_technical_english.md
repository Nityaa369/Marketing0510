Copy is written in **simple technical English**: short sentences, contractions, ordinary
construction, but the real terms kept. User, 27 Aug 2026, on the Independent Director video
scripts: *"why are the scripts in such fancy english... will they listen to such a language?"*
then *"keep it in simple technical english."*

**Why:** there are two opposite failures and both lose the viewer. Written-prose lines ("the
corporate runway shortens sharply after fifty five") are sentences off a page and nobody
listens past three seconds. But dumbing the terms down ("job site" for the IICA databank, "pay
committee" for the nomination and remuneration committee) destroys the only thing the script
sells to a professional audience, which is that the speaker knows how the thing actually works.
The terms are the credibility.

**How to apply:** read every line out loud; if you would not say it across a table, rewrite it.
Keep IICA databank, special resolution, audit committee, NRC, merchant banker, fixed term,
sitting fee. Say dates the way people say them ("12th to 14th September"). Convert figures the
viewer would otherwise have to compute ("three hours a day", not "nine hours"). The ads that
won already read this way: *"Here's what most don't realise"* at ₹40 CPL. See
[[feedback_icp_named_first]] and [[feedback_price_hook_then_value_fast]].
