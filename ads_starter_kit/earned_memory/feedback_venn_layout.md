Built from a DTC supplement static Ruchika supplied ("1 CHEW THAT DOES BOTH": blue ground, tall
condensed headline with one italic word, two outlined circles with a benefit in each, the packshot
in the intersection, a small caption). `adkit/layouts_custom.py` -> `venn`.

**What changed from the reference, and why:** an **ICP line above the headline** (a supplement sells
to everyone, this account never does, and both simplicity S6 and ad_lint C23 fail a face that names
nobody); the **offer in the intersection** instead of a packshot, because a camp has no object to
photograph and the reference's logic is "the product is where the two circles meet"; and a **CTA,
terms and caption**, because the reference is a brand static with no ask.

**The two circles are the argument.** A Venn only says something when the two sides are genuinely
separate today. Pair the work the reader already does with the work they cannot do yet; never pair
a skill with a restatement of itself.

**What it cost to get right, all of it copy rather than CSS:**
- Circle labels are 3 to 5 words. Longer ones spill out of the circle and collide with the lens
  (`fit` catches it as F7 SPILL), and single long words like "Transaction" overflow at 46px.
- `creative_judge` C1 reads the largest text as the call-out and calls a bare plural noun a
  fragment. **Make the ICP line a question** ("A company secretary?"), which is also what the one
  approved corporate finance kit did.
- C1's verb list did not know arrange, value, read, restart, sign, advise, score, invest, forecast,
  cost, cover, price, pass, close, sell, so seven imperative headlines were called fragments.
  Extended 18 Sep with a before/after on 39 shipped faces: verdicts unchanged.
