Before concluding a camp's problem is its copy, pull ad level and look for the same
copy running on two faces. Global Patent 19 Sep (3 Sep 2026) had one:
`pc_patent_rf04_medrep_sep19` and `pc_patent_break01_medrep_sep19` shipped a
**byte-identical body and headline** (both creatives pulled from Meta and diffed) at
**Rs 1,699.97** and **Rs 160.54** per buyer. The whole `rf_` design-refresh family
(`_swiss`, `_kawaii`) cost Rs 6,992 for 2 buyers, roughly 20x par; two of its five sold
nothing at all.

**Why:** the account's instinct when CPL rises is to rewrite the argument, which is the
expensive move and usually the wrong one. Copy on this line is already fitted (see
[[feedback_copy_is_filled_in_not_written]]); the variable that actually moved cost was
the rendered face and the chassis it was built on.

**How to apply:** on a rising-CPL camp, pull ad level with frequency, CTR and CPM before
touching words. Rising CPL with **falling CTR and rising CPM** is an exhausted audience
and needs new ICPs. Rising CPL with the *same copy* on a new face is a face problem and
needs rebuilding on a priced chassis, not new words. Check frequency on the cheapest ads
first: on this camp the two cheapest were the two most burnt at 2.6 against a campaign
1.3, which is what dragged par from Rs 129 to Rs 201. Related:
[[feedback_vary_the_design_style]], [[feedback_icp_is_the_biggest_lever]],
[[feedback_paychart_and_ctr_traps]].
