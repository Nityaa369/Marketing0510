DPDP wave 3, 1 Oct 2026. Ruchika, on a shipped face: *"what is this vague headline?? Legal
work that is written not heard??"*. "Not heard" means "not argued in court". The reader has
to work that out before the line means anything.

Auditing the other nineteen heads for the same class found **seven more**, in three kinds:

- **Wordplay.** "written, not heard"; "advisory work that survives an adjournment".
- **A definite article with nothing behind it.** "The same client argument"; "Scope the
  matter, instead of clearing the queue."
- **Jargon.** "the hardest data to govern" (twice, and two separate blind readers had
  already flagged the word); "a named specialism"; "without sending it out" (sending where?).

**Why this is not caught.** `hook_rules.py` passed all eight. The judge accepted all twenty.
The blind read passed them, because the blind read measures whether a face CAN be decoded,
not whether it reads WITHOUT decoding - a reader who tries hard enough gets there and
scores 3/3. This is the same blind spot recorded in
[[feedback_headline_names_career_not_chore]] for Corporate Finance, and I reproduced it
here eight times in one kit after writing that note.

**How to apply:** before the gates, read all the heads in one column and ask of each one:
is there any word here the reader has to translate? A metaphor, a "the" with no referent,
or a term of art all fail. Say the thing in words a stranger does not unpack: "Legal work
with no court dates, for clients abroad who pay in dollars."

Related: [[feedback_ad_clarity_rules]] (no headline wordplay, 14 Aug 2026),
[[feedback_callout_no_riddles]], [[feedback_half_asleep_callouts]].

**A fourth kind, found 1 Oct after three rounds of fixes: REGISTER.** "Choose data privacy
as your **specialism**" - reported as "specialism??". It is a formal British noun; an Indian
law graduate says "area of law". It appeared **13 times** across the kit, so it was a habit.
The same pass caught "a company's privacy **position**" (abstract noun that sounds precise
and means nothing), "the overseas **parent**" (a parent what?) and "sell a review **on** your
own name" (simply the wrong idiom).

**And a fifth, structural, found only when all twenty heads were read in one column:** the
words "privacy" or "data" appeared in just **6 of 20**. The rest said "advise the overseas
parent", "a compliance skill", "a service you can sell", "add a line to your practice", and
left the reader to infer the field from the deliverables list below. A headline that needs
the rest of the face to supply its subject is ambiguous however plain its words are.

**The question to ask.** Mine was always "can this be understood?" The right one is
"**would this reader say this?**" The gates can only ask the first: a blind reader who
concentrates always comprehends, so comprehension gates cannot see wordplay, jargon,
register or a missing subject. Read the twenty heads in one column, out loud, before any
gate runs.
