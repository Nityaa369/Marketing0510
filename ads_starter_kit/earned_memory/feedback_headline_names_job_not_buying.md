The biggest line on an ad face must be about **the job**, never about **buying the
product**. A headline whose verb asks the reader to read, compare, check, consider or
decide-whether hands her homework instead of a prize.

Evidence: `WomenRemote_Wave9`, 31 Aug 2026, 7 built and 6 approved. The single rejection
was *"Read all three afternoons, then decide on the work."* Ramanuj: **"cryptic, we can
drop it."** Its five approved siblings all name a destination or a prize instead
(*"Become the assistant US owners cannot find."*, *"Your first client. Not luck, a route
you can walk."*).

**Why:** the reader is scrolling, not shopping. A face that opens on the evaluation
process assumes she has already decided to consider the product, which she has not, so
there is nothing on the face she wants. This is distinct from [[feedback_ad_clarity_rules]]
cryptic-ness (unclear meaning) — the meaning was perfectly clear, the *appeal* was absent.

**How to apply:** before rendering, read the largest line alone and ask "is this about the
job, or about buying?" Note that two gates do NOT catch it: the blind-reader test passed
it on all five questions (it measures comprehension, not desire), and `hook_rules.py`
passed it because "decide" sits in the forward-verb lexicon — but "decide" moves her
through a purchase decision, not into a job. Human read, every time.
