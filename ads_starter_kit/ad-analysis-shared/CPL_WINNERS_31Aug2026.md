## Finding 1 — the pay chart is the worst format in the account

Replicated in **three campaigns across three product lines**, each time against the best ad in
its own campaign:

| Campaign | Pay chart | Best in same campaign | Penalty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independent Director 12 Sep | ₹195.36 | `banker` ₹60.42 | **3.2× worse** |
| Patent 19 Sep | ₹380.20 | `pc_patent_icp26_ayurveda_bams` ₹121.53 | **3.1× worse** |
| US Accounting 12 Sep | ₹72.65 | `USACC_us22_notice_fifties` ₹29.65 | **2.5× worse** |

Normalised against its own campaign median, the pay chart indexes **2.34** where par is 1.00.
Every other format sits between 0.64 and 1.22.

A pay chart makes a salary figure the hero. `CLAUDE.md` already bans salary ladders from the ad
face on compliance grounds. **Performance and compliance agree, which is rare and worth
saying:** this is not a rule we are paying for. Stop building them.

## Finding 2 — CTR does not predict CPL. It sometimes runs backwards.

Across all 90 ads: **CTR vs cost per result r = +0.17 (leads), +0.08 (purchases)** — no useful
relationship. Within campaigns it is worse than nothing in two of them:

| Campaign | n | CTR vs cost-per-result |
|---|---|---|
| Senior AI 22 Aug | 8 | **r = +0.61** (higher CTR, *more* expensive) |
| Academic Writing Aug | 7 | **r = +0.52** |
| Corporate Finance | 11 | r = +0.25 |
| Patent 15 Aug | 15 | r = +0.10 |
| Independent Director | 7 | r = +0.05 |
| Data Privacy | 12 | r = −0.04 |
| HR 22 Aug | 9 | r = −0.11 |
| US Accounting 8 Aug | 7 | r = −0.24 |

The clearest single case: `SeniorICP|Toon|st02_canteen` has the **highest CTR in the account at
4.12%** and costs **₹234.71**, worse than its campaign median. `cf_cma` has a CTR of **0.61%**
and costs **₹170.17**.

**This matters for how we judge our own work.** The hook doctrine in
`AD_CREATION_DIRECTOR.md` §4 optimises for stopping the scroll ("would someone scrolling
half-asleep understand this in 3 seconds"). That test is about comprehension and it should
stay. But a creative that wins attention is not thereby a creative that wins leads, and the
toon/chat formats are where the two come apart hardest (format index 1.22 on 4%+ CTR).
**Never argue an ad is good because it got clicks.**

## Finding 5 — Data Privacy is the cheapest paid camp, and it is the line the rejections built

Data Privacy runs at **₹122.02**, the cheapest of eight paid camps. It is also the campaign whose
Wave 1 produced four Rule 1 rejections on 12 Aug and became the source of `CLAUDE.md` Rule 1.

One tension worth naming honestly: the shipped DPDP body copy still carries obligation framing
("a second set of rules now lands on the same files", "their contract is about to push the
obligations down to your floor"), which reads close to what Rule 1 rejects. What changed after
Wave 1 is **the headline**, which is now pure destination: *"Add data privacy. Grow faster."*
So the working shape for a regulation-driven product is **destination in the headline, pressure
in the body** — not pressure everywhere, and not pressure in the biggest element.

## Limits of this pull

1. **One account.** `954464642377027` only. LawSikho `164485738329334` is DISABLED (flagged,
   needs a Meta appeal) so the LawSikho-brand ads are not in here at all.
2. **CPL is not revenue.** Cost per lead and cost per ₹10 purchase say nothing about who
   actually showed up to the camp or bought the programme after it.
3. **Creative is confounded with everything else** — audience, placement, budget, bid, day of
   week, and how long a cell was left running. Only the pay-chart finding is replicated across
   enough independent campaigns to carry real weight on its own.
4. **Format labels are inferred from ad names**, so `notice` shows n=1 here even though more
   notice-format cells ran under other names.
