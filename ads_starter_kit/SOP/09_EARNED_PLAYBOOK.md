# The earned playbook — population-specific knowledge

**Conditional reading.** None of this is general procedure; every section was paid for by a
specific rejection on a specific population. `SOP/00_CONSTANTS.md` carries the rules that
apply to every kit. This file carries the ones that apply when you are building for the
population named in the heading.

Read the matching section **before** writing `value_props.md` for that population, not after.

## When the brief is remote work / women / SMB


CTR vs cost per result across 90 ads: **r = +0.17 (leads), +0.08 (purchases)** — no useful
relationship. Inside Senior AI it is **+0.61** and Academic Writing **+0.52**: higher CTR,
*more* expensive. The account's highest-CTR ad (`SeniorICP|Toon|st02_canteen`, 4.12%) costs
₹234.71, worse than its campaign median; `cf_cma` at 0.61% CTR costs ₹170.17. Toon and chat
formats index 1.22.

Keep the three-second comprehension test in Director §4 — that test is about **being
understood**, and it stays. But a creative that wins attention is not thereby one that wins
leads, and toon formats are where the two come apart hardest.

### Destination in the headline, pressure in the body

Data Privacy is the cheapest paid camp in the account (₹122.02) and is also the line that
produced four Rule 1 rejections on 12 Aug. Its shipped body copy still names regulatory
pressure; what changed after those rejections is the **headline**, now pure destination:
*"Add data privacy. Grow faster."* For a regulation-driven product that is the working shape.
**Never put the pressure in the biggest element on the face.**

## START FROM THE FOUNDER'S CHASSIS — `WomenRemote_Wave4_SEND_THIS/`

**Read that folder's `README.md` and two of its faces before designing anything for a
remote-work / women / SMB brief.** It is the same camp, built by the founder, gated at
**28 of 28 ACCEPT**, and every cell is tied to measured historical performance. It
answers most of the questions a new kit would otherwise rediscover expensively.

Five elements every cell carries. A face missing any of them has a known defect:

1. **A promise block, labelled `WHAT WE DO IN 3 DAYS`.** A light panel with an accent
   left border. That README treats a missing promise as a *shipping defect*: six
   already-approved cells were patched purely to add one, because without it they
   "make no promise at all". **A DAY 1 / DAY 2 syllabus is not a promise** — it lists
   topics and commits to nothing. The dictated wording is: *"Join our 3 day live
   bootcamp where we will teach you over 9 hours how women like you land this work
   with foreign SMBs and earn in dollars."*
2. **An outcome with a clock.** *"Land your first client as a virtual assistant in the
   next 15 days."*
3. **"Earn in dollars"**, on the face, with no figure. Ramanuj dictated this verbatim
   for N25. This is also how the money gets carried compliantly — see the money rule
   below.
4. **A concrete work list, with the buyer named on every row.** *"The inbox and the
   calendar / For a founder abroad"*, *"Orders, returns, refunds / Sorted in writing"*.
   Not adjectives, not benefits: the literal tasks, and who buys each.
5. **The ICP band is a SITUATION she is living in, not a demographic or a wish.**
   Night shift with a headset · the quiet house · a long gap · caregiving · small city
   · office evenings. Every one is pointable.

### VA pairs with a SITUATION. SMM pairs with a CREDENTIAL.

Learned 26 Aug 2026, and it explains a rejected cell. Scheduling, inbox and calendar
work needs no qualification, so **"become a virtual assistant" reads as a step DOWN to
a credentialed reader** — the same self-image filter that cut the non-practising doctor
and the PhD scholar in `ICPs_20_Wave2.md`. A dietitian-VA ad had one duty of four that
used her training; the qualification was decorative and the value prop read as unclear.

- **Social media manager** survives a credential ICP, because domain knowledge is the
  hiring criterion: a generic manager cannot write for a nutrition or design practice
  and she can. That contrast IS the ad.
- **Virtual assistant** needs a situation ICP, where the job is a step up.

**The mechanism underneath, found 26 Aug 2026 while re-cutting six credential ICPs
(`WomenRemote_Verticals_MediaBuyer_5-7Sep`): the ad must offer the SAME CRAFT to a
better payer.** That is Rule 1 restated, and it is the general form the VA/SMM pairing
is a special case of. Inbox and calendar work is a *lesser, different* craft than
nutrition, so a dietitian reads it as a demotion. SMM survives a credential because
domain knowledge writes the content. But so does any **vertical** role where the
credential is load-bearing in the duties themselves: screening a therapy practice's
first call is psychology; clearing a prior authorisation is nursing; holding a case
chronology is legal. The test is not the word "VA", it is **how many of the duties would
be done worse by a generalist.** All of them, or the qualification is decorative and the
cell reads as unclear.

### A PRACTISING CREDENTIAL CANNOT BE THE ICP FOR THIS PRODUCT. Proven three ways.

Settled 26 Aug 2026, after `WomenRemote_Verticals_MediaBuyer_5-7Sep` spent a full spec
cycle trying to make it work. **Do not propose this a fourth time.**

1. **The archive:** `WomensRemoteWork_USRoles_MediaBuyer_5-7Sep` ran exactly this idea
   (credentialed woman, VA or SMM role) and shipped **5 ads, 0 approved**.
2. **The filter:** `womens_ai_bootcamp_5-7Sep/ICPs_20_Wave2.md` cut non-practising BDS and
   PhD scholar on self-image, in its own words: *"The product's deliverables cap how senior
   an ICP can usefully be."*
3. **Seven blind readers, 26 Aug 2026:** six credential folds, all five ICP types, came back
   **step DOWN, 6 of 6**. A rewrite of the worst one, with every work row changed to a
   judgement only her training can make plus a ladder showing where it leads, came back
   step DOWN again. Reader, on the rewrite: *"the ad opens by naming my clinical training
   and then hands me a phone and a scheduling queue. That makes the swap explicit instead
   of hiding it."*

**The mechanism, and it is the opposite of the intuition.** Making the credential
load-bearing does not lift the offer, it sharpens the contrast between what she trained
for and what she is being handed. The harder the ad leans on the qualification, the worse
the demotion reads. Copy cannot fix this, because the defect is in the ICP-to-offer
distance, not in the words.

**Two things that DO generalise from the failure:**

- **The self-image filter is about PRACTISING IDENTITY, not prestige.** A nurse who left
  the ward still says "I trained in clinical care"; a B.Com graduate who never worked in
  finance has no bruisable identity. Score the credential on whether she *identifies* with
  it, not on how senior it sounds.
- **Ownership beats assistance.** Every approved Wave 4 cell puts her in charge of
  something: *"an income that is yours"*, *"your own list"*, *"what comes in has your name
  on it"*. Wave 2's approved ex-banker hook was "the same client handling, **on her own
  hours and her own list**". Ours said "assistant to a US advisor". Same work, opposite
  reading. If the face gives her a boss instead of a book, it reads as a step down however
  the title is worded.

**What to do with credential research instead:** it is still the best source of *where the
clients are*. Put it in targeting, in Day 1's "the work SMBs actually buy", and on the
landing page as proof that a health or legal or finance background gets picked. Keep the
ad's identity band a SITUATION, which is what all 28 of the 28/28 approved Wave 4 cells do.

---

**Keep the role name generic. Put the specialism in the duties.** Corrected by Ramanuj
26 Aug 2026, same day, on a draft that had done the opposite. That draft named the real
US job titles for these seats - intake coordinator, prior-authorisation coordinator,
client services coordinator - reasoning that "assistant" reads as a demotion to a
credentialed reader. His answer: *"the routes are roles people will not recognise -
generalise the roles."*

He is right, and it is Rule 2. A title the reader has never heard of reads as invented,
which is the *other* recorded rejection - *"those jobs don't really seem real, I doubt
anyone is hiring"* - reached from the opposite direction. **Use virtual assistant and
social media manager**, which is what the camp already sells and what the approved W5
face already says.

The same test applies to the BUYER, not only the role: "med spa", "ABA practice", "RIA"
and "prior authorisation" are US trade words. Write "a US skin and cosmetic clinic", "a
US practice that treats children", "a US financial advisor who has just started on her
own", "getting a treatment approved by the insurance company".

This does not reopen the dietitian-VA rejection, because that cell failed on its
**duties** (one of four used her training), not on its title. So: generic title,
domain-loaded duties. She handles the first call from someone in distress; she gets the
treatment approved; she keeps the case timeline. A generalist does each of those worse,
and the work list is where the reader sees it.

**Check the credential against the Wave 2 cut list before writing anything.**
`womens_ai_bootcamp_5-7Sep/ICPs_20_Wave2.md` already cut non-practising BDS and PhD
scholar on self-image, and insurance agent, pharmacist, architect, bank branch officer
and *working* clinic nursing staff on means, volume or time. A credential on that list
needs either a different situation (the nurse who **left** the ward has the two free
hours the working one does not) or it does not run.

### Money on the face: name the payoff, never the figure

Checked against the approved corpus, six faces: **not one carries a rupee or dollar
figure except the ticket price.** An approved face headlined *"What Writing Work
Actually Pays"* has a **percentile** axis and zones reading "nobody pays much" / "paid
properly" / "one brief pays big". Where an approved face cites a number it is a
percentage with a named source (*"Meta CPMs up 40 to 60% since 2023. India D2C budget
benchmarks, 2026."*). The founder's cells say **"earn in dollars"** and stop there.
Rate tables belong on the landing page, sourced line by line, exactly as the approved
pages do it: *"A campaign retainer with an Indian small business: ₹35,000 to ₹80,000 a
month. Published Indian agency pricing, 2026."*

    ```
      README.md  ad_copy_wN.md  ads/  landing_pages/  manifest.json  kit_meta.json
    ```

    **Nothing else ships.** No `html/`, `previews/`, `photos/`, `value_props.md`, `hooks.md`,
    `.word-cap`, `*.py` or contact sheet — those are working files and stay behind. Ad stems
    continue one X-series across waves of the same product (Wave 6 `X1`–`X6`, Wave 7
    `X7`–`X11`, Wave 8 `X21`–`X30`, so Wave 9 starts `X31`); the page is always `lp_` plus the
    ad's exact stem. **`manifest.json` is the deployment contract and a kit without one cannot
    ship** — its `slug` carries no `/f/` prefix even though the page comment and README both
    do, so verify the two agree before zipping.

    Learned 31 Aug 2026, when a Wave 9 handover shipped the entire working folder, then a
    stripped-down invention of my own, before anyone pointed at the format that already
    existed. **Look at what the last approved wave actually shipped before designing a zip.**

### Never build a pay chart

Replicated across three campaigns and three product lines, each against the best ad in its own
campaign: Independent Director ₹195.36 vs ₹60.42 (**3.2x worse**), Patent ₹380.20 vs ₹121.53
(**3.1x**), US Accounting ₹72.65 vs ₹29.65 (**2.5x**). Normalised, the pay chart indexes **2.34**
against a campaign par of 1.00; every other format sits between 0.64 and 1.22. A pay chart makes
a salary figure the hero — which `CLAUDE.md` already bans from the face on compliance grounds.
Performance and compliance agree here, which is rare enough to be worth saying.
