## 2. How the NCA works (process overview)

URL: https://nca.legal/process/ (updated: not shown)

- "The National Committee on Accreditation (NCA) is a standing committee of the Federation of Law Societies of Canada." [web]
- "The NCA evaluates the qualifications of anyone with a law degree, whether you are: a Canadian citizen who got your legal education abroad, a Canadian civil law graduate, a newcomer to Canada with legal qualifications earned outside of Canada, someone considering immigrating to Canada." [web]
- "During the assessment, the NCA reviews your academic and professional experience. We find gaps between your education and training on one hand, and the knowledge you would get from an approved Canadian law school program on the other hand." [web]
- Six steps listed: Self-assessment, Application, Get assessed, Do the work, Certificate of Qualification, Apply to the bar. [web]

URL: https://nca.legal/about/ (updated: not shown)

- "a standing committee of the Federation of Law Societies of Canada" whose purpose is that people trained outside Canada have "knowledge of Canadian law similar to that offered by an approved Canadian common law degree program." [web]
- The Federation is "the national coordinating body of Canada's 14 provincial and territorial law societies", which regulate "more than 136,000 lawyers and 4,200 Quebec notaries and Ontario's nearly 10,600 licensed paralegals." [web]
- "In 2017, the Federation of Law Societies of Canada reviewed the NCA process and improved how it functions." [web]

URL: https://flsc.ca/what-we-do/nca/ (updated: not shown)

- "In August 2024 the Federation retained the Canadian Centre for Professional Legal Education (CPLED) to develop options for assessment tools" [web]
- "In the fall of 2025, the NCA Assessment Modernization Committee recommended an assessment framework and a strategic direction to modernize the NCA assessment process" [web]
- "At its October 18, 2025 meeting, Federation Council approved the framework and strategic direction" [web]
- Note: the modernisation is a plan. Nothing fetched says what will change or when.

## 4. Assigned requirements (what you must do)

URL: https://nca.legal/process/assigned-requirements/ (updated: not shown)

- The NCA assigns five core subjects to all applicants: "Canadian Administrative Law", "Canadian Constitutional Law", "Canadian Criminal Law", "Canadian Professional Responsibility", "Foundations of Canadian Law". [web]
- "If your qualifications are assessed after January 1, 2022, you will also need to complete a course in legal research and writing" [web]
- "All NCA applicants are required to demonstrate competence in Indigenous Law and Peoples" [web]
- "You must successfully complete your assignments within five years of the date your assessment was issued." [web]
- Three ways to meet assignments: write and pass NCA exams, take courses at a Canadian law school, or combine both. [web]
- FAQ [web]: "The number of exams, subject areas or courses you will need to take depends on your legal education and professional experience. You will generally need to take at least the five mandatory Canadian law subjects." The FAQ summary also lists Contracts, Torts and Property as typically required in addition (see Flags).

## 5. The exams

URLs: https://nca.legal/exams/information/ , https://nca.legal/exams/online-exam-rules/ , https://nca.legal/process/assigned-requirements/ , https://nca.legal/faqs/ (all: updated not shown; the exam information page shows a fee-change date of February 7th, 2025)

- Format: "NCA exams are fact-based and open-book" ("similar to those in Canadian law schools"). [web]
- Online and proctored: "Exams are proctored online, fact-based, open-book and three (3) hours long" [web, assigned-requirements]. "The NCA, through the resources of Paradigm Testing and MonitorEDU, ensures the security and integrity of the NCA exams." [web, online-exam-rules]
- Open book detail: "NCA exams are long response form, and open- book. Only hard copy study materials will be permitted; you will NOT have access to electronic copies of your notes or textbooks." [web, online-exam-rules]
- Typing: "Only responses to exam questions entered using a keyboard will be considered as an answer." [web]
- Duration: "You have a maximum of 3 hours to complete an NCA exam." and "If you finish early, you must to stay in place, with your computer still locked down, for the full 3 hours." [web]
- Pass mark: "Examinations are graded on a pass/fail basis (i.e. 50 percent is considered a pass)." [web]
- Attempts (web): "You may write NCA exams three times (a first attempt and two re-writes)." [web, online-exam-rules]
- Attempts (policy) 16.2 [pdf]: "An Applicant is permitted to write an NCA examination in a particular Core Subject or Additional Legal Subject to a maximum of three (3) times." 16.2.1 [pdf]: "The Executive Director may, at their sole discretion, grant an Applicant a fourth (4th) and final attempt... if unsuccessful, the Applicant will be required to take the course in law school"
- Re-sits: 16.1.1 [pdf]: "If an Applicant fails an NCA examination in an assigned subject, they must clear the failure by successfully completing the examination in that Core Subject or Additional Legal Subject or through an NCA approved course... they will not be permitted to pursue a different examination subject."
- Validity: 16.3 [pdf]: "NCA examinations must be successfully completed within the five (5) year validity period of the Applicant's Assessment Decision" ; 13.4.1 [pdf]: extension may be requested "prior to or within six (6) months after the expiration" and is at the Executive Director's "sole discretion".
- Registration only after assessment: 16.1 [pdf]: "An Applicant may register for NCA examinations only after they have received an Assessment Decision that indicates they are eligible to do so."
- Results timing: "Results are released about 10 to 12 weeks from the date of the last exam in each session." [web, exam information]; FAQ: "Results are generally released 10 to 12 weeks after the date of the last scheduled exam in each session." The NCA "will not expedite individual exam grading". [web]
- Exam fee: "As of February 7th, 2025 the fee for registration for each NCA exam (starting with exams being held in April 2025) will be $500 CDN plus applicable taxes." [web]
- Self-study: "You have to study for your exams on your own (i.e., without help from the NCA)." [web]
- Missed exam: "Your failure to appear for an exam will not be recorded as an attempt. However, you will lose your exam fee for that exam." Deferral is not allowed: cancel and re-register. [web, FAQ]
- Review and appeal (https://nca.legal/exams/review/, updated not shown) [web]: review materials appear "approximately 3-4 weeks after the exam results are released"; appeals only "if there are convincing reasons to believe that your failing grade was the result of a major error or injustice"; appeal fee "$250.00 CDN, plus taxes, for each NCA exam"; appeal "must reach the NCA within two weeks"; re-evaluation "can take about two to four weeks."
- Tech (https://nca.legal/exams/technical-requirements-and-testing-for-online-exams/, updated not shown) [web]: laptop or desktop with webcam, "Windows 7 or higher, or Mac MAC O/S 10.15 or higher"; internet "3 Mbps upload and download"; "two cameras, either a laptop or desktop camera and a camera on a tablet or phone"; system check "by 6:00 p.m. (Eastern Time in Canada) the day before your first exam." (Looks dated: Windows 7, Chrome 50+.)

### Schedule: how often, and how often each subject
Sources [pdf]: 2026 schedule https://nca.legal/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2026-NCA-Exam-Schedule-FINAL.pdf ("Issued: October 9th, 2025"); 2027 schedule https://nca.legal/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2027-NCA-Exam-Schedule-FINAL.pdf ("Issued: September 18, 2026"). The page https://nca.legal/exams/schedules/ says [web]: "Dates and schedules are subject to change without notice."

- The PDFs list 12 registration windows a year (2026: registration opening 17 Nov 2025 for the January exams, then monthly). So "offered monthly" is true for sessions.
- Each session opens about 3.5 weeks before the exam month starts and closes about 3.5 weeks after opening; e.g. "January 2027: Registration opens: November 16, 2026 Closes: 11:00 PM (Eastern Time) December 10, 2026". Costs page says registration is "six to eight weeks before exam dates" [web].
- The five core subjects each repeat only every third month, in both 2026 and 2027 (read from the PDF tables):
  - Constitutional Law and Foundations of Canadian Law: January, April, July, October
  - Criminal Law and Professional Responsibility: February, May, August, and the late-year session (labelled November)
  - Administrative Law: March, June, September, and the final late-year session (labelled November in the PDF)
- PDF quote: "All NCA exams have a time limitation of three hours." "Morning exams will start between 07:00 and 10:00 hrs (Eastern time)" and "Afternoon exams will start between 14:00 and 17:00 hrs (Eastern time)". Exams are in Eastern Time, so for India that means roughly late afternoon to night.
- FAQ/exam page: "The NCA offers twelve exam sessions per year, but not all subjects are offered in each exam session."
- Cancel: "You must cancel your exam no later than midnight (ET) the day before it is scheduled." 2027 PDF: "We charge an administrative fee of $100.00 CDN, plus taxes, per each exam you cancel. If you fail to cancel... you will lose your exam fees."
- French: 2027 PDF: "If you would like to write your exams in French, please send an email to exam@flsc.ca".

- [pdf] Exemption: "an Applicant will be exempt from completing the NCA language screening test if they provide evidence of a valid test... Test results must be less than two years old when submitted to the NCA."

## 11. Certificate of Qualification

URL: https://nca.legal/process/certificate-of-qualification/ (updated: not shown) [web]

- Shows your "knowledge of Canadian law is similar to the knowledge of someone who got their law degree through an approved Canadian law school program."
- "The Certificate of Qualification is not the same as a Canadian law degree."
- Law societies in common law provinces and territories require it "for entry to their bar admission process."
- Request: portal, "Request Certificate". After it: "Apply for entry to a law society bar admission program."
- FAQ [web]: "The Certificate of Qualification does not expire. However, each law society has rules outlining the acceptable delay between when the NCA issues your Certificate of Qualification and when you enter the bar admissions process."
- FAQ [web]: "No, the Certificate of Qualification does not admit you to the bar. To become a licensed lawyer in a Canadian common law jurisdiction you must apply to that jurisdiction's law society for bar admissions once you have your certificate."
- FAQ [web]: "If you have studied law abroad, you must have an NCA Certificate of Qualification to qualify for law society bar admissions in all common law provinces and territories in Canada."
- FAQ [web]: "The rules and regulations for admission to practise law in Canada, which often include an articling process, are set by the provincial and territorial law societies."
- Policy 4 [pdf, language]: language rule says assigned language test must be done "before they will be issued a Certificate of Qualification."

## Flags: contradictions, out-of-date items, and differences from LawSikho claims

Contradictions and stale items
1. Attempts: the web pages say exams "three times" with no mention of a fourth. The Policy Manual 16.2.1 allows a discretionary 4th and final attempt. Use "three, with a fourth possible at the Executive Director's discretion" or just "three".
2. Resources page date labels are stale (costs and timelines "2019.01.30", policies "2022.07.01", exam outlines "2018.08.22") while the content is current (2024, 2026). Do not cite those dates.
3. Technical page lists Windows 7, Chrome 50+, Edge unsupported; looks out of date.
4. CPLED legal research and writing fee: $375 (June 2020 notice) vs $475 plus taxes (search snippet). Current fee unverified; CPLED payment page not opened.
5. Exams information page and the 2026 schedule PDF label the last two sessions inconsistently (both "November" in text); I read 12 registration windows. Treat the exact late-year month names with care.
6. Exam registration timing: costs page says "six to eight weeks before exam dates"; the schedule PDFs show registration opening roughly 7 weeks before the exam month's first date and closing about 3 weeks before. Consistent in range, but plan from the PDF.
7. The NCA exams page summary and one advisory summary call the body "National Committee of Admission". That is a summariser slip; the site says "Accreditation".
8. The "five core subjects" list is the minimum for everyone. FAQ and policy also treat Contracts, Torts and Property as part of the competency set (policy 8.3 tests performance in them), and the self-assessment page says mixed common law applicants must show competence in "all eight core subjects". The schedule offers Contracts, Torts and Property as exam subjects. So "five core subjects" is right as the always-assigned list, but many applicants are assigned more.
9. Exam fee: $500 per exam, cancel $100 per exam, appeal $250 per exam, all plus Canadian taxes. "Approximately $3000" minimum is on the costs page; five exams alone are $2,500 plus the $400 assessment fee (my arithmetic, not an NCA statement).

LawSikho claims versus the NCA site
- "Exams are online": CONFIRMED, with a catch the ads may be missing: proctored via MonitorEDU, hard-copy books only, two cameras, typed answers, Eastern Time slots.
- "Offered monthly": PARTLY. Twelve sessions a year is confirmed ("twelve times a year or once each month"). But "not all subjects are offered in each exam session". Each of the five core subjects is offered only every third month (Const and Foundations: Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct; Criminal and Prof Resp: Feb/May/Aug/Nov; Admin: Mar/Jun/Sep and the last session). Say "a session every month" not "every exam every month".
- "Best case 10 months": CONFIRMED ("10 months", costs page).
- "Average two years": CONFIRMED ("About two years").
- "Up to five years to finish": CONFIRMED, from the assessment date (not the application date); one extension request is possible at the Executive Director's discretion.
- "Five core subjects": CONFIRMED as the always-assigned list; see Flag 8. Also Legal Research and Writing (assessed after 1 Jan 2022) and Indigenous Law and Peoples are separate requirements, not exams.
- "English screening and an Indigenous law course since 1 March 2026": CONFIRMED with detail. Both effective 1 March 2026. English screening is a proctored Versant English Placement Test, score 61, one attempt, included in the assessment fee, and it applies to everyone (language, English or French), with exemption by a valid IELTS/CELPIP/PTE less than two years old. The Indigenous requirement applies to applications "assessed on or after March 1, 2026" and is done via CPLED's six-week online course (or an approved law school course). Subtle difference: the language rule triggers on "before their qualifications are assessed" from 1 March 2026, the Indigenous rule triggers on assessment date, so someone assessed before 1 March 2026 may not need the Indigenous course. The site does not say how people assessed earlier are treated.

## DID YOU KNOW: 16 facts for tired readers

Each: plain sentence, then exact source quote and URL. Quotes marked [pdf] are exact from the NCA's PDFs; [web] quotes came through a summariser.

### The exams
1. Every NCA exam is taken on your own computer at home. Quote: "Exams are proctored online, fact-based, open-book and three (3) hours long" [web]. https://nca.legal/process/assigned-requirements/
2. You can bring books to the exam, but only paper ones. Quote: "Only hard copy study materials will be permitted; you will NOT have access to electronic copies of your notes or textbooks." [web]. https://nca.legal/exams/online-exam-rules/
3. You pass with half marks. Quote: "Examinations are graded on a pass/fail basis (i.e. 50 percent is considered a pass)." [web]. https://nca.legal/exams/information/
4. There is an exam session every month, but each core subject comes round only every third month. Quotes: "The NCA offers twelve exam sessions per year, but not all subjects are offered in each exam session." [web] https://nca.legal/exams/information/ ; and the schedule tables [pdf]: "January 6 Canadian Constitutional Law", "February 3 Cdn. Professional Responsibility" at https://nca.legal/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2026-NCA-Exam-Schedule-FINAL.pdf (my reading of the tables, not an NCA sentence).

### The cost and time
5. The assessment costs CAD 400 and each exam costs CAD 500, plus Canadian taxes. Quote: "Effective March 1, 2024, the fee will be $400 CDN plus applicable taxes" and "$500 CDN plus applicable taxes" [web]. https://nca.legal/costs-and-timelines/
6. The NCA says the fastest finish is about 10 months and the usual finish is about two years. Quote: "10 months" (best case), "About two years" (average) [web]. https://nca.legal/costs-and-timelines/
7. You get five years from your assessment date to finish everything. Quote: "You must successfully complete your assignments within five years of the date your assessment was issued." [web]. https://nca.legal/process/assigned-requirements/
8. Exam results come about 10 to 12 weeks after the last exam of the session. Quote: "Results are released about 10 to 12 weeks from the date of the last exam in each session." [web]. https://nca.legal/exams/information/

### The rules that changed in 2026
9. Since 1 March 2026 everyone must pass a short English or French screening test before the NCA assesses them. Quote: "As of March 1, 2026, all NCA applicants must complete a language screening test before their qualifications are assessed" [web]. https://nca.legal/faqs/
10. The English screening is one proctored test with one attempt, and its cost is inside the assessment fee. Quote: "a proctored Versant English Placement Test (VEPT), the cost of which is included in the assessment fee, with a minimum score of 61 overall (equivalent to a minimum of 7 in IELTS)." [pdf]. https://nca.legal/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/EN-Interpretive-Statement-on-Language-Requirements-March-1_2026v9clean.pdf
11. If you already have a recent IELTS, CELPIP or PTE Core result at the NCA's scores, you skip the screening. Quote: "Test results must be less than two years old when submitted to the NCA." [pdf]. Same URL as fact 10.
12. A new Indigenous law and peoples requirement started on 1 March 2026, done through a short online course. Quote: "Subsection 10.5.1 will apply to applications assessed on or after March 1, 2026." [pdf]. https://nca.legal/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/FLSC_NCA_Policy_March2026eng_v1.5.pdf ; course: "self-directed and delivered entirely online over six weeks" [web]. https://cpled.ca/our-programs/indigenous-peoples-and-the-law/

### What the certificate is and is not
13. The Certificate of Qualification says your Canadian law knowledge is like a Canadian law graduate's. Quote: "your knowledge of Canadian law is similar to the knowledge of someone who got their law degree through an approved Canadian law school program." [web]. https://nca.legal/process/certificate-of-qualification/
14. The certificate is not a Canadian law degree and does not make you a lawyer. Quotes: "The Certificate of Qualification is not the same as a Canadian law degree." [web] https://nca.legal/process/certificate-of-qualification/ ; "No, the Certificate of Qualification does not admit you to the bar." [web] https://nca.legal/faqs/

### After the NCA
15. After the certificate you apply to a provincial law society, and it works in every common law province and territory but not Quebec. Quote: "all the provinces and territories except for Quebec." [web]. https://nca.legal/process/bar-admissions/
16. The certificate never expires, but each law society limits how long you can wait before you start its licensing process. Quote: "The Certificate of Qualification does not expire. However, each law society has rules outlining the acceptable delay between when the NCA issues your Certificate of Qualification and when you enter the bar admissions process." [web]. https://nca.legal/faqs/
