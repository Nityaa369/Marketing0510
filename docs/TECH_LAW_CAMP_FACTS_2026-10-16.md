## 1. What tech law is (in the script's words)

- **One-line definition for ads and pages:** tech law is the legal work that technology companies
  need: their contracts, compliance, data privacy, policy and disputes.
- Script: "The legal work depends on the business model and does not require any special
  understanding of tech, science or coding. You just need to understand how tech businesses work
  and make money."
- **Three kinds of tech business (by who they sell to):**
  1. Consumer internet: apps for people (Swiggy, Instagram, Paytm, Netflix, Amazon). "Every one of
     these apps runs on user data, user content and user payments. And each of those three things
     is now heavily regulated."
  2. B2B and SaaS: software for businesses (Google, Dropbox, Hubspot, Slack, Shopify, Adobe, AWS,
     Zoom).
  3. Emerging and deep tech: AI, biotech, space, defence, semiconductors, robotics. India has
     "3,600+ deep tech startups"; government support of "a ₹1 lakh crore fund, soft long-term loans
     at just 3-4% interest for 12-15 years, and even equity investment of up to 25%".
- **The 8 kinds of work every tech company needs:** structuring of tech business models; tech
  contracts; legal and regulatory compliance and AI governance; data protection and privacy
  management; policy and government advocacy; advisory work; tech disputes (regulatory,
  cybercrime); legal ops, vendor contracts and contract management.
- **The 5 the script expands on:**
  1. Structuring: can this business be done, in which jurisdiction, at what cost (example: a
     lending-algorithm startup that wants to work with NBFCs and banks).
  2. Drafting contracts and policies: terms, privacy and cookie policies, software development,
     MSAs, SOWs, change orders, SLAs, EULAs, API integration, tech transfer, reseller and
     co-branding agreements, influencer agreements.
  3. Compliance: RBI, SEBI, IRDA sandboxes; sector filings; privacy compliance (DPDP deadlines
     "November 2026 and May 2027"); intermediary duties for platforms. "Compliance work is
     recurring."
  4. Policy advocacy: research, whitepapers, responses to draft laws (Ikigai cited).
  5. Disputes: recovery, tech contract arbitrations, CCPA/RBI/SEBI disputes, cyber crime
     complaints, impersonation and defamation, DMCA takedowns, trademark abuse and cybersquatting.
- "You don't have to learn all of these. You can learn 2-3 of these and get started."

## 2. Why now (the script's case)

- Litigation is high supply, flat demand. Criminal cases instituted: 81.6 lakh (2018) to 62.4 lakh
  (2025). Source cited as "People's Archive of Rural India's annual report". Flag: the 2023 and 2025
  figures are identical (62,41,569) with different growth rates. Check before using.
- Digital economy is 11.74% of national income (₹31.64 lakh crore), heading to nearly one-fifth of
  GDP by 2030 (MeitY).
- Indian legal market about $27.95 billion, heading to $42 billion by 2034, with tech law and data
  privacy named as new practice areas.
- "India just rewrote its entire tech rulebook in the last 18 months" (Day 1, Tab 5): DPDP
  penalties up to ₹250 crore; national AI governance framework; takedowns cut from 36 hours to 3
  with AI labelling; RBI's 244 Master Directions and 9,445 circulars withdrawn; 28% GST on gaming
  upheld with ₹1 lakh crore in disputes; a High Court line "Section 79 is not a free for all safe
  harbour". "A 30-year veteran and a fresh graduate are standing on the same starting line."
- Seniors don't know it; college stops at the IT Act ("less than 2% of the work").
- Fintech (Day 1, Tab 4): ₹314 lakh crore through UPI; "over a thousand fintech legal roles open";
  one DMI Finance role with 200+ applicants; an Adyen Senior Regulatory Counsel JD. The script says
  do not invent a fintech salary.
- ESOP story: lawyers at Zomato, Paytm, PolicyBazaar made more at IPO than top partners; Coinbase's
  legal chief "18 million USD"; Swiggy IPO made "500 employees crorepatis".
- Early mover: "The next 2 years are open for newcomers"; Upwork was free in 2020 and now charges
  for proposals.
- Ad rules: the ESOP and dollar figures are page-only and need a source. "Be early" framing must
  stay traceable to these lines. Never use the AI-fear angle (Day 2's "grunt work is fast
  disappearing" stats) on a face.

## 6. The 6-month plan (Day 2)

- 2 hours a day: 1 hour learning, 1 hour outreach and track record.
- First 2 months: easiest skills (DPDP, GDPR, policies, contracts) and outreach from week 1.
- 20 skills across 6 months (structuring SaaS, e-commerce, fintech, gaming and blockchain;
  website policies; software and tech services agreements; IP and takedowns; healthtech, space,
  IoT, drones; DPDP and GDPR compliance; DPIAs; data breach response; tech disputes; international
  expansion; e-commerce and fintech compliance; crypto licensing; foreign investment).
- Outreach: post on LinkedIn; 1 hour a day pitching startups (Product Hunt, Shopify store list);
  Fiverr gigs and Upwork proposals; help foreign lawyers build their brand first.
- Targets: 3 to 4 testimonials and a first paid gig by end of month 1; by month 6 "$1000 per
  month or get a job" after 20 to 25 skills, 50 pitches, 3 to 4 articles. CIPP mocks weekly.
- Months 6 to 12: CIPP exam optional, 2 new skills a week, 3 to 4 hours pitching a week.
- Ad rule: these are page-only outcomes. Never put "$1000 in 6 months" on a face.

## 10. What this means for ads and pages

- **Prize to sell:** a tech law career (clients, a firm tech team, an in-house role, or remote work
  for US lawyers) built on the lawyer's existing training. Same craft, better payer.
- **Mechanism to cite:** the work depends on the business model, not code; the rules are new so a
  veteran and a fresher start level; the camp teaches real documents (software development
  agreement, terms review, refund policy, influencer agreement).
- **Audience:** lawyers and law students only. Name the reader's practice or situation.
- **Never on a face:** income or dollar figures, ESOP numbers, "$1000 in 6 months", job promises,
  AI-fear, "not for you if" (Day 2 has a "people who should not do this course" block; keep it off
  pages too).
- **Facts for the face:** 24 to 26 Oct; live, no recordings; Rs 10 incl. GST and refundable per
  `HISTORICAL_CONTEXT.md` (the camp price is not in these uploads, so confirm on the sheet).
