# Landing page rule book: informative pages win trust, and trust wins learners

Saved 16 Oct 2026 on divyanshu's instruction. Ramanuj's rules still win; this adds to them. A more informative page earns more trust and so more learners. Add every persuasive element below to every page, written for that reader (not copied between pages in a kit).

## Hard rules (never break)
1. **Never a countdown**, timer or "starts in" widget, and never invented urgency ("closes today", "last chance", "seats left"). Show the real dates as plain text.
2. **Dates live on the landing page** (camp days, times, price terms). Ad creatives carry no dates (Ramanuj, 13 Aug).
3. **The button states the joining price: the full exchange.** Holy grail, always followed. The price is exclusive to joining: Rs 10 incl. GST for bootcamps, a different set price for community camps (SQE Rs 100, Contract Drafting Rs 200). Never a bare "Join now", never "Reserve my seat", never "Know more". Keep "refundable" wherever the price appears. (Wording: "Join the 3 live evenings for Rs 10" or "Join the Rs 10 bootcamp".)
4. No hedge above a pay button. The only disclaimer is at the very end.
5. Works with JavaScript off; one real pay URL on every button; test-buy before launch.

## Persuasive elements every page carries (in this order, 21-step spine)
1. **First fold = the ad's promise in its words**, the reader named, the one-sentence field definition, the three dated sessions, the price card and a pay button.
2. **Name the reader and their symptom** in their own words (lede line).
3. **The outcome**: the new, better career, same craft, better payer.
4. **Three failed alternatives** this reader already tried, each with why it did not change the job.
5. **Reframe as positioning, not effort**, and the cost of staying unclear (no fear).
6. **Why now: sourced, dated facts framed as demand** ("Did you know?" cards, each with what it means for this reader and a source). No penalties, no pay figures on the face, nothing predicted.
7. **The chain of buyers**: who creates the work (startups, global capability centres, legal tech and AI companies), with counts and sources.
8. **What the work looks like for this reader**, using their own trade's nouns.
9. **The offer and the mechanism in three steps**: the three live sessions day by day, and the method (skills, track record, outreach).
10. **The first usable artifact**: the document drafted on Day 1, shown as a real clause with a margin note for this reader.
11. **The vocabulary made easy**: the five terms every contract uses, each explained in plain words.
12. **The plan after the camp** in plain steps (hours a day, months, outreach).
13. **Two real proofs** from learners with consent; individual results are never promised.
14. **Cost against alternatives**: alone, a long course, this bootcamp; the joining price highlighted.
15. **Risk removal**: refundable, live only, what to bring, how joining details arrive.
16. **FAQ in the reader's own worries** (first one open).
17. **A section that names the reader's role** (Ramanuj, 4 Sep), then buyer language they can repeat, the next step, and then stop selling.
18. **Sticky pay button** after the first fold at every width; reader-specific closing heading in two plain clauses.
Design aids that earned a place (3D flip cards, tilted buyer chain, stacked contract, price coin, redline mock) may be used when they carry one of the elements above. They never replace one.

## Checks before a page goes out
- No countdown; dates present and match the ad's camp; the price is in the button.
- Facts re-opened at source on launch day; "VERIFY" notes cleared.
- Every claim traceable to the camp script or a named source and date.
- Pages in one kit differ in argument, not only in colour (80% sentence overlap is a block).
- Read the whole page once as the reader, half asleep.
