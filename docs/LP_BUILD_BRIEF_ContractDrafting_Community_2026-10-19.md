## 2. What the camp actually teaches (the only facts the page may use)

From the CD 64-65 calendar (`docs/COMMUNITY_CURRICULA_Oct-Nov2026.md`). Every claim about the work
must trace to this list or to the camp script. **Get the script before writing the body.**

- **Dates:** 21 Oct (Wed) to 11 Nov (Wed). Holidays 30 Oct, 6 Nov, 7 to 8 Nov (Diwali).
- **Format:** a short reading and a quiz every day on WhatsApp; 9 live sessions in the evening
  (5, 7 or 8 PM). Live only, no recordings promised.
- **Documents taught, one a day (16 plus the basics):** parts of a contract; non-compete clause;
  e-commerce terms and conditions; employment agreement (plus a remote work monitoring policy);
  NDA and confidentiality clauses; cookie policy; co-founder agreement; DPDPA-compliant privacy
  policy; ESOP; SAFE agreement; term sheet; SaaS agreement; representations and warranties clause;
  IP licensing clause; commercial lease; vendor service agreement; joint development agreement.
  Extra: auditing D2C websites for silent obligations.
- **The 9 live sessions:**
  1. Orientation, and using AI to find missing clauses and red flags (an influencer agreement)
  2. Finding issues in terms and conditions using AI
  3. Listing your first gig on an online services marketplace (**never name Fiverr; never say freelance**)
  4. Questions to ask a client before drafting (co-founder agreement), and the roadmap
  5. Reaching UK small businesses with a GDPR-compliant privacy policy
  6. Pitching startups directly (Y Combinator lists, Apollo)
  7. How law firm partners and in-house counsel think
  8. A LinkedIn profile that makes you a known contract name
  9. Publishing your first article
- **Field definition (first fold, required):** "Contract drafting is writing the agreements a
  business signs: who does what, who pays, and who carries each risk."

**Blocked claims until the script confirms them:** "21 contracts in 21 days"; "an expert corrects
every one"; "certificate"; "templates for every contract"; any count of students, cohorts or jobs;
"no seniors", "no competition" or any newness claim without a dated fact.

## 3. The page spine (21 steps, in this order)

Numbered steps, each a section with a plain-English h2. Steps 4 to 6 are one section with three
cards; 13 and 14 are one proof section. Target 10 to 14 visible sections.

| # | Step | What it must contain on a CD page |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **First fold: name the buyer** | ICP call-out (same words as the ad's tape), the ad's hook as the h1 word for word, the field definition, a from/to strip ("Today: you ... After 21 days: you ..."), the dates as plain text, price card "₹200 incl. GST · Refundable · Live online", full-width button. Short: about 130 words, like the liked page. |
| 2 | **Symptom** | The reader's situation in their own words (lede). Hope, not pity: write "want", never "struggling". |
| 3 | **Outcome** | The better career: same craft, better payer. The contract drafting lawyer startups and businesses hire. Income only as a direction, no figure. Button. |
| 4 to 6 | **Three failed alternatives** | Three things this reader already tried (copying precedents, waiting for a senior to teach, a long theory course), each with why it did not change the job. |
| 7 | **Reframe** | The gap is positioning, not effort: they know the law, they have not drafted for a paying client. |
| 8 | **Why now** | 3 to 5 "Did you know?" facts framed as demand (startups formed, businesses moving online, DPDPA privacy policies needed), each with what it means for this reader and a **named, dated source on the card face** (visible with JavaScript off). No penalties, no predictions, no pay figures. |
| 9 | **The offer** | 21 days, 9 live sessions, daily practice, dates, ₹200. Button. |
| 10 | **Mechanism in 3 steps** | Learn one document a day; practise it live with AI red-flag checks; take it to clients (UK SMEs, startups, LinkedIn, first article). |
| 11 | **First artifact** | What they draft in week 1, shown as a real clause with a margin note written for this reader (e.g. a non-compete clause with "HR teams issue these every week" for the HR reader). |
| 12 | **Plan after the camp** | Plain steps: hours a day, the first three clients, the article, the profile. No promised result. |
| 13 to 14 | **Two real proofs** | Real named learners with consent only, below the money section. **None supplied yet: if none arrive, drop the section; never invent.** |
| 15 | **The work, day by day** | The calendar as a weekly grid (the calendar face is the cheapest community ad: crimlit full-calendar, 943 buyers). No dates on cards beyond the plain dates line. |
| 16 | **Implementation** | WhatsApp group the same day, daily reading on the phone, evening sessions on Zoom, what to keep ready. |
| 17 | **Cost comparison** | Time and output only: learning alone, a long course, this community. ₹200 highlighted. No fees or incomes. |
| 18 | **Risk removed** | Refundable, live, ₹200 incl. GST, what happens if they miss a session (only what the script says). |
| 19 | **Buyer language** | One plain line they can repeat to family ("I am learning to draft the contracts startups sign, 21 days, ₹200"). |
| 20 | **Next step** | Closing fold: heading in two plain clauses, reader named first as a plural occupation ("Final-year law students already study contract law. These 21 days turn it into contracts businesses sign."), one lede, one offer sentence, button, terms. |
| 21 | **Stop selling, then the only disclaimer** | At the very end. Nothing hedges above any button. |

**Also on every page:**
- **A section that names the reader's role.** At least one h2 carries a word from the ad's ICP line (lint L6; Ramanuj 4 Sep: "make sure there is at least 1 role-specific section").
- **"You are in the right room if..."** as the qualifying block. Never "Is this for you?" and never "not for you if" (Ramanuj 19 Jul: "i hate this never do this").
- **An FAQ in the reader's own worries,** with the first item open.
- **The work as named jobs.** Copy the liked page's grid: the 16 documents as short h3 cards.

## 6. Copy rules (all blocking unless marked)

- No em or en dashes (write "21 Oct to 11 Nov"). Sentences 34 words or fewer; headings 16 or fewer.
- Banned words: transform, unlock, discover, elevate, leverage, empower, journey, game-changing,
  revolutionary, seamless, cutting-edge, freelance, freelancer, skyrocket, unleash, harness,
  navigate, landscape, adapt. No "Fiverr". "Want", not "struggling".
- No regulator or liability threat, no "more work at the same desk", no "get named the person who
  fixed it" (Rule 1). No AI-fear.
- No income figure in the copy; income as a direction is fine. A salary ladder only if matched to the
  reader, sourced (AmbitionBox count and date), and never with a hedge above a button.
- No job, placement or client guarantee. Individual results never promised.
- Every fact: primary source and date, re-opened on launch day; clear every `[[VERIFY]]` mark.
- Plain reading: 19px body, 62ch lines, line-height 1.65. Expand every acronym once (NDA, SAFE,
  ESOP, DPDPA, GDPR, SaaS, JDA).

## 8. Gates before handing over

1. `python ads_starter_kit/check_all.py <kit>`: the only report that counts. Includes preflight
   (pay stubs, hedge within 120 characters of a button, "not for you if", guarantees, dashes,
   page overlap, ad-to-page pairing, definition in the first fold).
2. Lint L1 to L8: stub link, banned qualifier, dashes, definition in the fold, length, reader-named
   h2, sentence and heading length, contrast.
3. Closing read: `python closing_reader.py --prepare <kit>`, one fresh subagent per packet, then
   `--score <kit>`.
4. Manual: open every page; test-buy; JS off at 360/390/768; no countdown; dates match the camp;
   facts re-checked at source.
5. Handover lines: preflight result, better-job lines, the definition, each page opened with a real
   pay URL, blind and closing read results.
