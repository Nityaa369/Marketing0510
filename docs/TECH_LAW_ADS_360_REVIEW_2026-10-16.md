## 1. The answer on one screen

**Verdict: fix first.** The campaign is cheaper and bigger than August, but it is running out of
fuel eight days before the camp, and three rule breaks sit inside its best-selling ads.

The five things wrong, in order of seats and money at risk:

1. **We will miss the seat target at the current pace.** The Daily Ad Desk target is 6,000 seats.
   GrowthX shows 3,093 paid by the morning of 16 Oct. Daily paid seats from Meta fell from 139 on
   10 Oct to 75 on 15 Oct. At about 150 a day across Meta and Google, the camp lands near 4,300,
   about 1,700 short. *Why:* one ad carried the camp and it is wearing out, and no fresh wave has
   launched since the drawer video on 8 Oct.
2. **The winning ad is tiring and the cheapest ad is starved.** TechLaw_Video_unusedllb took 78%
   of video spend. Its cost per purchase rose from Rs 149 (week to 6 Oct) to Rs 324 (13 Oct), and
   its daily buyers in GrowthX went 79, 65, 63, 55, 33, 12, then 0 on 16 Oct. TechLaw_Video_comeback
   is the cheapest ad with 20+ buyers (Rs 167 ex-GST on 205) but the CBO gives it about Rs 1,400 a
   day. *Why:* one CBO ad set, no spend floor for proven readers.
3. **Google sells the form, not the seat.** Google sign-ups pay 29% of the time against 75% on
   Meta (GrowthX, October). Google cost Rs 339 per paid seat from 6 to 15 Oct against about Rs 218
   incl GST for Meta video. Google funnels show 448 expired checkouts in a week against 210 on Meta,
   and Google spends heavily between midnight and 6 AM, when about 80% of checkouts expire. *Why:*
   intent mismatch and timing, not a broken page: the checkout works, and one Google page
   (`lp2-that-someone-can-be-you`) pays at 74%.
4. **Rule breaks in the ads and pages that sell best.** "$2,000+ a month" sits in the names of the
   two funnels that sold the most "US paralegal" seats and in the planned live-action Drawer script
   (income figure on the face is banned). The Restart and Litigator videos keep "Very few lawyers can
   do it" and "The work comes by email". August's primary text promised "tech law work in 6 months".
   **No Ramanuj verdict exists on any October Tech Law ad, reader or page.** Every approval gate in
   the artifacts was cleared by the requester.
5. **False or unprovable claims in the script and brief, and proof nobody can check.** The brief's
   "the DPDP deadline in November 2026 is weeks away" is false for ordinary companies (November 2026
   covers consent managers; most duties start 13 May 2027). Six more script lines fail a fact check
   (section 5D). None of the learner money figures is visible on any public profile, and LawSikho's
   own materials give different numbers for the same people. Prospects who search find refund and
   EMI complaints, a 2022 complaint calling a bootcamp seat count fake, and a 2025 Delhi High Court
   loss on a defamation suit.

Top three fixes, today:

1. **Launch a fresh wave by 18 Oct** in its own ad set with a spend floor: the three finished
   videos (after removing the flagged lines), a no-income twin of the unused LLB winner, and two or
   three new named-occupation lawyer readers. The November playbook data says no new ads in the last
   4 days before a camp, so 20 Oct is the hard stop.
2. **Move about Rs 9,000 a day from Google to Meta** (the Desk's own recommendation), switch Google
   off from midnight to 6 AM, and keep only the Google funnels that pay above 40%.
3. **Get Ramanuj's call today** on the income figure, the seat counts, and the readers in the new
   wave. Nothing in this camp has his approval.

Half-asleep test on the current best ad (unused LLB video): *who is it for?* LLB holders working
outside law, clear in the first words. *What job?* Tech law work, clear. *Why say yes?* Rs 10, but
the reason it sells is a dollar figure the rules ban. Pass on who and what, fail on how it earns
the yes.

## 5. What is wrong, why, and how to fix it

### 5A. Volume and pacing

| Problem | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| About 1,700 seats short of 6,000 at the current pace | The winner is fading and no wave has launched since 8 Oct | Launch a wave of 6 to 8 ads on 17 or 18 Oct. A fresh wave made campaigns 7% to 9% cheaper for 3 days (November Ads Playbook) |
| Unused LLB video fading (Rs 149 to Rs 324; 79 buyers a day to 0) | Frequency 2.76 and a narrow reader. Check in Ads Manager whether budget moved before calling it audience wear | Keep it running and do not refresh its face (0 of 17 refreshed winners improved). Add a different ad for the same reader instead |
| Comeback starved at Rs 1,400 a day | One CBO ad set lets Meta pile spend on the biggest ad | Give proven readers (career break, unused LLB twin) their own ad set with a minimum daily spend, as the CF waves on 30 Sep showed |
| No new ads allowed late | New ads in the last 4 days ran 1.12x | Last launch 20 Oct. Anything not live by then waits for the next camp |

### 5B. Audience (who)

| Problem | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Three aspiration cells took about Rs 92,500 incl GST at 1.45x to 1.90x | Readers named by what they want, not what they are | Stop them. Do not rebuild "US paralegal", "remote work" or "TMT associates" as readers |
| 18 cells never got enough spend to judge | One CBO, too many ads per wave | Fund 3 new lawyer readers properly instead of 20 thinly |
| Readers worth testing now | Evidence ranks named occupations first (21% beat par), then life situations | Junior lawyers in years 1 to 3 (75% paid on 36, video almost ready); litigators (UGC video ready; August "No seniors" led); banking and NBFC lawyers (94% on 16). The 15 Oct ICP session listed tech-adjacent litigator readers (frozen bank account and mule account cases, cyber fraud cases, cheque bounce, consumer forum); these need Ramanuj before build |
| The camp script still welcomes CS, CA, MBAs and techies | Script written for a mixed room | Ads stay lawyers only (Ramanuj, 24 Sep). Never target non-lawyers for Tech Law |

### 5C. Creative and copy (offer, prize, clarity, rules)

| Problem | Why it matters | Fix |

Move into tech law." with one plain line defining tech law (contracts, privacy and compliance for tech companies) |

### 5I. Competition and positioning

- Rivals sell Indian compliance on a regulator clock: NLU Delhi with Mentblue (TMT, data protection
  and fintech certificate, launched "as DPDP Rules take effect"), IALA (DPDP plus AI, Rs 1,999 to
  2,999), Lawctopus (free workshop into a Rs 19,999 course; closest rival by traffic), DSCI DPO
  (Rs 48,000 to 55,000). Free DPDP certificates have made "a certificate" worthless as a promise.
- Gaps we can own: a career with foreign clients, not a compliance certificate; the career framed
  as demand, never as a regulator threat; one named lawyer per ad (every rival names everyone at
  once); a Rs 10 live trial; work samples over certificates; CIPP readiness in rupees with EMI.
- The IAPP exam fee (USD 550) is not part of the stack. Never imply it is.
