For Ramanuj's review BEFORE any creative. Replaces the 9-row table in
`SQE_WAVE_STAGE1_ICPs_HOOKS_2026-10-19.md` (read its section 0 first: product, price line and
routing are still his calls; every face below carries `[PRICE LINE]`).

**Brief from Virat:** primary audience is **litigators**, plus **lawyers who want to move abroad
and build an international career**. So rows 1 to 10 split litigators by the court work they do
(each already doing work that maps to English law), and rows 11 to 15 split movers by where they
are going. Rows 16 to 20 are the other lawyers in the room.

Field definition on every face and page: "The SQE is the one exam that makes you a solicitor of
England and Wales. The written parts are taken in India." (Day 0 slide 8, Day 2.)
Route on every face: SQE exams (written in India, at six Pearson VUE cities), then admission by the
SRA, then practise. Never "automatic", never "skip SQE2" as a promise: the SRA decides the exemption
case by case (Day 2 slide 31).

## The learner's seat: what makes me click, then show up
I do not want to learn about an exam. I want to know if a UK qualification is real for someone like
me, and what my next 12 months look like.
1. Is this for someone like me? (the call-out names my job)
2. Is it real, and can I do it from here? (written in India; I keep my practice)
3. What do I walk out with? (below, per day of the 7)
4. What does it cost me? (`[PRICE LINE]`, live only)
5. Will my life get bigger? (a second qualification, never a replacement)
6. Why now? (the next window, no fear in it)

What every reader walks out with, from the Orientation roadmap: Day 1 the global opportunity,
Day 2 SQE decoded and the SQE2 exemption route, Day 3 a mock SQE1 on a real topic, Day 4 career
pathways after SQE, Day 5 their international profile, Day 6 the launch lab (their draft exemption
reference letter reviewed line by line), Day 7 live Q&A. Only the outcome clause changes per row.

## Critique, in Ramanuj's order (self-review; I have not seen his verdict)
- **Who**: rows 1 to 6, 8, 9 and 11 to 18 are real lawyer occupations. Row 10 (family) and row 7
  (tax) are weak: narrow pools, no SQE subject for tax. Row 14 and 19 are small. Row 15 needs a
  list before it can be targeted.
- **Offer**: heads for 1, 2, 9, 11, 12, 16 and 18 name a move. Rows 4, 5, 6 and 17 risk "same craft,
  same desk" (the hook says "the exam covers what you do"). Fix: lead with the move abroad in the
  head and keep the subject-matter link as the reason line only.
- **Prize**: the A-line must name what the reader becomes. "UK solicitor" is used in 12 of 20;
  vary it ("English-law deal lawyer", "arbitration lawyer") so a kit does not read as one template.
- **Clarity**: "Are you a litigating advocate?" is plain. "NCLT" needs no gloss for this reader.
  Hooks 7 and 20 have weak reasons; blind-read before layout and drop the two lowest.
- **Rules**: no dashes, no number, no income, no guarantee, no fear, no "online". Reasons 2 and 5
  need care: "can judge your years in court" is not a promise of exemption; keep the word "can".
- **Verdict: fix first.** Rows 1, 9, 11, 12 and 16 are the likeliest approvals. Rows 7, 10, 14 and 15
  are probable rejections or too thin. Send Ramanuj the top 10 for a pick and keep this list as the
  bench. The audience pick, the product line (free 7-day community or Rs 10) and routing against
  NCA are his decisions, not mine.
- **Half-asleep test, hook 1**: it is for a litigating advocate, the job is UK solicitor with the
  practice kept, and saying yes is easy because the first step costs `[PRICE LINE]`. Passes.
