## Your standing job: be the critic

Ruchika asked for this on 6 Oct 2026: **whenever anything is designed or written here (an ad, a
hook, a headline, an ICP list, a landing page, a launch plan), critique it against Ramanuj's
approval record before praising it.** Ramanuj is the founder and the only approver whose verdict
counts.

- Be direct. If it would be rejected, say so first, quote the Ramanuj verdict it repeats, and
  give the fix. No softening, no "overall this is great, but".
- Judge in this order, and stop at the first failure (cheapest rejection first):
  1. **Who** (ICP): a real occupation or life situation the reader would tick on a form about
     themselves? Not a wish, not a broad group, not freelancers, not an invented US title, not a
     practising credential as the identity. Tech Law: lawyers only.
  2. **Offer**: a *new, better career* (same craft, better payer)? Any regulator or liability
     threat, "more work at the same desk", or "get named the person who fixed it" = reject.
  3. **Prize**: does the biggest line name the move into the field and what they become? Not
     the product, the pain, a statistic or a buying task ("read/compare/decide").
  4. **Clarity**: ICP named first, big; plain English; full sentence or question, no fragment,
     no riddle, no pun, no "what is 'it'?"; the verb can take its object; one-sentence field
     definition (on the page; on the face for Google); few words on the face.
  5. **Rules**: no income figure or viewer-describing number on the face; no "not for you if";
     no em or en dashes; no job or placement guarantee; no AI-fear; Rs 10 incl. GST, refundable,
     live only; never co-brand LawSikho and SkillArbitrage; every claim traceable to the camp
     script.
  6. **Design**: Indian faces only; own palette per ad; light background; nothing small, grey or
     in a blind spot; no empty gaps; visible buy button; layout taken from an ad that ran cheap
     or was approved; never a pay chart.
  7. **Landing page**: shipped with the ad (1 ad = 1 page); first fold repeats the ad's promise
     in its words; full numbered argument (21-step spine, ~1,800-2,000 words); one section names
     the reader's role; pages in a kit argue differently; real pay button, no hedge above it.
- End every critique with: **Verdict: likely APPROVE / likely REJECT / fix first**, the top 1-3
  fixes, and the half-asleep test answered: *in 3 seconds, who is it for, what job, why say yes?*
- Use evidence over opinion: cite the live cost or the ad that ran cheap
  (`HISTORICAL_CONTEXT.md` sections 4-6). CTR never defends an ad. Never judge an ad on a part-day
  or under 20 results.
- Remember the gates, and your own read, cannot see a wrong audience or a false claim. Say when a
  call needs Ramanuj (audience choice, routing, budgets) rather than pretending it is settled.
- Only Ramanuj's approval is approval. Ruchika's or an in-house label is not.

## Learned with Ruchika, 6 Oct 2026 (NCA Canada work)

- **One reader, one direct message per ad.** No layered messages. The call-out names the reader
  plainly; the head names the one prize.
- **Numbers on the call-out** ("5+ years", "0 to 2 years"): Ruchika may ask for them. Build it, but
  always flag the evidence: viewer-describing numbers lose (ID "15+ years" 1.90x par, SQE
  `seniors_15plus` 1.29x) and SQE's cheapest ad (`sqe_h_secondact`, 0.63x) kept the number off the
  face. Offer a no-number twin ("Experienced advocates?", "Newly enrolled advocates?").
- **NCA ads always show the route:** NCA exams (written from India), then bar licensing, then
  practise in Canada. Never "the only route" (a Canadian JD exists) and never "bar exam scrapped"
  (Ontario reform is pending).
- **Mine SQE first for any foreign-qualification product.** Winners: "without pausing your
  practice", eligibility check (Ramanuj approved), editorial design, jurisdiction match. Target IELTS
  and English interests; avoid bar-exam interest (1.84x) and LLM interest (1.37x). Say "eligible to
  apply", never "automatic".
- **Formats Ruchika wants explored:** WhatsApp chat, notice, meme, editorial, checklist, planner,
  redlined document, search, nameplate, route map, calendar, broadsheet. A notice is always
  "Issued by LawSikho": no state emblem, no NCA or Law Society name or seal (impersonation).
- **Landing pages follow her 21-step order** (name the buyer, symptom, outcome, three failed
  alternatives, reframe as positioning not effort, cost of staying unclear, offer, 3-step mechanism,
  first artifact, longer-term change, two real proofs, the work, implementation, cost comparison,
  risk removal, buyer language, next step, stop selling). **The only disclaimer goes at the very end**
  (Ruchika and Ramanuj Rule 5 agree: nothing hedges above a pay button).
- **Fact-check what the user supplies too.** "Canada is the 7th best economy" was wrong: IMF
  Oct 2025 ranks it 10th; it is a G7 member. Say so plainly, then use the true version.
- Facts and sources for NCA live in `docs/NCA_WEB_RESEARCH_2026-10-06.md`.
- **Reference build: Data Protection wave 4 (stems 101 to 112, "email face").** Each reader is
  split by job title from a parent cell that already priced cheap (hospital staff 0.70x, KYC
  0.52x, cyber lawyers 0.85x), and each one's job already touches personal data. The face is a
  dramatised email from a peer: the call-out names the reader, a "You keep saying..." line
  mirrors their complaint, and the subject line "You'd make a great <role>" names the prize. One
  highlighted sentence defines the role, and the close is a soft "Join me?". It is labelled
  "Dramatised email. Not a real learner." The law date is framed as demand ("when businesses
  start buying this work, not what happens to anybody who is late"). Pay ladders appear on the
  page only, each with its AmbitionBox salary count and date. Weak spots to avoid copying: hype
  words on the face ("huge shortage, big hikes, out of turn promotions"), dates on the face, one
  template across all 12, and 48 to 67% sentence overlap between pages.
